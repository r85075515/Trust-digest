10:T72b,A federal grand jury in Washington, D.C., refused to indict former White House aide Cassidy Hutchinson, handing the Justice Department another defeat in its pursuit of President Trump's perceived adversaries.
What happened
The grand jury rejected the bid on Tuesday, according to AP sources cited across the coverage. Hutchinson testified to lawmakers about President Trump's actions during the January 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol, and the Justice Department sought an indictment over that testimony. Justice Department attorney Neville Hedley told U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui that the grand jury declined, multiple outlets reported. PBS NewsHour, The Hill, and Axios all carry the same core account: prosecutors asked, the grand jury said no.
Why it matters
Grand juries almost always approve what prosecutors ask, so a refusal is itself the signal — it says the evidence or the theory did not clear even a low bar, in secret, before any trial. For a Justice Department effort aimed at Trump's perceived political enemies, another such refusal stops looking like bad luck and starts looking like a pattern. That pattern, not any single case, is what will shape public trust in these prosecutions.

Still uncertain
