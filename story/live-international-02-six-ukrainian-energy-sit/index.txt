10:T67b,Ukraine says Russia is ignoring the "energy ceasefire" President Trump claims to have brokered. President Volodymyr Zelensky says six Ukrainian energy sites have been hit by Russia since Trump announced the truce, and Ukrainian officials report bombs kept falling overnight on gas facilities, wounding 33 people.
What happened
Trump said he had brokered a ceasefire to protect energy infrastructure in both Russia and Ukraine, and has blamed high energy prices on Ukraine. Kyiv disputes the claim outright — NPR's headline asks whether either country even knew about the supposed deal. Separately, Trump made a surprise decision Friday to suspend sanctions against Russia, and Zelensky publicly attacked Trump over a Russia diesel deal that has drawn fire. PBS's William Brangham discussed the latest developments with Andrew Weiss, a former State Department official now with the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.
Why it matters
An announced ceasefire that neither side observes is worse than no announcement: it lets each side claim the other broke the deal while strikes continue. The diesel deal and the sanctions suspension suggest Washington's pressure is moving in the opposite direction from Kyiv's needs.
Sources agree / disagree
BBC, NPR, PBS, and The Hill agree on the core facts — six sites hit, 33 wounded, Kyiv disputing Trump's claim — while each adds its own angle: the diesel deal (The Hill, PBS), the "did anyone know" question (NPR). No conflicting facts in the snippets.
Still uncertain
The snippets do not include any Russian response to the ceasefire claim or details of the diesel deal's terms.
