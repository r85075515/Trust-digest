10:T6b5,US consumer prices rose 0.4% from September to October, slightly above expectations, in a report that complicates the Federal Reserve's next interest-rate decision. The Bureau of Labor Statistics did not publish the usual year-over-year inflation figure alongside the monthly reading.

What happened
The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% rise economists had forecast, while gasoline costs fell during the month. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, was forecast to rise 0.2%. The report arrived days after Republicans' midterm losses, widely read as a rebuke over high prices. A separate CNBC headline reported the US economy added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than expected, with unemployment slipping to 4.1%, against forecasts of 59,000 new jobs and a steady 4.2% jobless rate. The New York Times noted that last year's government shutdown will distort the October data.

Why it matters
October inflation is the last major price reading before the Fed's next policy meeting, and a hotter-than-expected monthly figure narrows the room for rate cuts. Voters have already punished the governing party over prices at the ballot box, so the political stakes are as high as the economic ones.

Sources agree and disagree
The Guardian, CNBC and the NYT agree on the headline 0.4% monthly rise. The 0.3% figure in some coverage was the pre-release forecast, not a competing reading, so there is no conflict on the actual number.

Still uncertain
The missing year-over-year figure and the shutdown's statistical hangover leave the underlying trend unclear. The jobs numbers come from a separate report and need confirmation in the full release.
