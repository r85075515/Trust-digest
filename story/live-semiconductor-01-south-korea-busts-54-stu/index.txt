10:T724,South Korea turned its emergency disaster alert system into an exam-hall trap, and it worked: alerts sent during the national college entrance exam exposed banned smart devices and caught 54 candidates, with 111 caught in total once other detection methods are counted. The crackdown follows a confession that showed just how high-tech cheating has become.

What happened
Tom's Hardware reported, citing The Korea Herald, that the alerts caught 54 candidates during the Suneung, South Korea's high-stakes college entrance exam, and 111 in total when combined with other means. The students were caught with banned smart devices. BBC News reported the same crackdown with the 111 total. The measure was announced after a cheater in South Korea's tax accountant exam confessed to using AI and a micro-camera to cheat.

Why it matters
Cheating has gone high-tech — AI plus a micro-camera — and enforcement is answering in kind, repurposing a public-safety alert network as a detection tool. The 111 total shows the problem is not a handful of cases, and other exam systems facing the same tools will be watching whether the tactic holds up.

Sources agree / disagree
The two outlets agree on the crackdown and the method. The figures differ only in scope: 54 is the number caught by the disaster alerts alone (Tom's Hardware, citing The Korea Herald), while 111 is the total across all detection methods (BBC News). Both numbers are reported side by side, not in conflict. Trust label: Two-source agreement — both outlets report the same crackdown, with the two figures covering different scopes.

Still uncertain
