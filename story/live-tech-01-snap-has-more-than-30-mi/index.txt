What happened
TechCrunch and Engadget both report the 30-million figure. The context is last year's controversial decision: Snapchat limited free storage for Memories — the feature that saves users' snaps — and told users they would need to pay $1.99 a month for extra storage or subscribe to Snapchat+ to keep their saved memories. A year on, the subscriber count suggests the bet worked, with power users apparently willing to pay for AI-powered effects as well as storage.
Why it matters
The verdict is straightforward: charging for something users once got free did not stall Snap's subscription business — it built it. Thirty million paying subscribers turns a backlash into a revenue line and validates the wider industry shift toward paid tiers for AI features. Watch whether Snap can hold that growth once the storage-driven conversions run their course.
