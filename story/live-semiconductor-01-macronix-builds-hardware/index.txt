10:T620,Macronix is building hardware security directly into its NOR flash memory, positioning the component for the European Union's Cyber Resilience Act, which becomes mandatory in December 2027. The move reflects a broader shift: connected-device makers can no longer treat security as a software patch applied after sale.

What happened
EE Times and EE Times Asia report that Macronix is embedding hardware security in NOR flash ahead of the EU law. Rather than relying on software patches to fix vulnerabilities after they emerge, manufacturers face growing pressure to build protection into products from the outset. For connected devices, that change gives flash memory a new role as a security anchor, not just storage.

Why it matters
The December 2027 deadline turns product security into a market-access condition for connected devices sold in the EU. Suppliers that build security into hardware early gain a selling point with device makers racing to comply, while laggards risk redesigns and delays.

Sources agree / disagree
EE Times and EE Times Asia carry the same story and the same December 2027 date, with no conflicting figures in the available material. The two outlets are part of the same publishing family, so the agreement is narrower than independent corroboration.

Still uncertain
The feed material does not name the specific security features, the first products carrying them, or their pricing and availability. Those product details remain to be confirmed from Macronix announcements or full product reporting.
