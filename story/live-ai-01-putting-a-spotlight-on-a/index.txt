What happened
The report found students are using generative AI in ways that cut into the slow, social work of learning: fewer office-hour visits, less time on problem sets, less collaboration with peers. A growing number of MIT faculty are concerned about how generative AI is changing how people work and how they think, learn, and relate to each other. MIT News reported the findings as part of a spotlight on AI, society, and humanity.
Why it matters
The shift is not about cheating; it is about students skipping the friction where learning actually happens, and faculty are noticing the cost. If office hours and peer work keep thinning out, universities will have to redesign teaching rather than just police AI use.
