10:Td82,NVIDIA announced on Monday that Jensen Huang has been reinstated as President and Chief Executive Officer and as a member of the Board, effective immediately — 11 days after he resigned amid revelations of two extramarital affairs, one with a woman US officials suspected of working with Chinese intelligence. The Board voted unanimously, the company said, after a series of discussions and meetings with Huang left directors satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Board Chair Harvey C. Jones said. NVIDIA filed a Form 8-K with the SEC the same day.

What happened: The reversal caps an extraordinary six days. On Nov. 17, Jones had publicly ruled out bringing Huang back and said the board had hired Heidrick & Struggles to run a CEO search. Over the weekend, according to MarketWatch, the Trump administration — and the president himself — pushed for Huang's reinstatement; the New York Post reported the board acted after receiving input from senior US government officials that left directors confident his return posed no national security risk. Investors cheered: NVIDIA shares rose about 27% in premarket trading (WSJ put the gain at as much as 30%, to just under $165), the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) rose about 12–13%, and Nasdaq 100 futures jumped 6.2%. The rebound follows the stock's collapse to $125.53 on Nov. 20 — down more than 47% from its Oct. 6 all-time closing high of $239.24 — amid an SEC accounting investigation, a California attorney general subpoena, and congressional scrutiny that remain unresolved.

Why it matters: The board has reversed itself completely in less than a week, under visible political pressure, while every formal investigation that the scandal triggered is still open. The reinstatement settles the leadership question that has hung over the world's most important AI chipmaker — and immediately raises a governance one: what the board learned, and what Washington assured it, to flip from "no intention to reinstate" to a unanimous vote in six days.

Sources agree/disagree: The official release, the 8-K filing, WSJ, MarketWatch, Bloomberg Law, Barron's and the New York Post agree on the unanimous vote, the immediate effect, and the share surge. They differ only on the size of the premarket move (about 27% per MarketWatch and the Post; as much as 30% intraday per WSJ and Barron's) and on emphasis: the role of US government input is reported by the Post and Bloomberg Law and echoed by MarketWatch's account of administration lobbying, while NVIDIA's own release does not mention it.

Still uncertain: The announcement does not say what happens to interim CEO Colette Kress, whether the Wachtell board investigation continues or what it found, or how the pending SEC accounting probe, the California AG subpoena, and the December congressional testimony schedule (Huang was due before House committees on Dec. 1 and the Senate on Dec. 3) are affected. Whether Huang's return holds if investigators reach different conclusions is also open.
