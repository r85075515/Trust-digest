10:Tc28,TSMC confirmed on Thursday (Nov 12) that information belonging to some current and former employees was posted on an external website without authorization, after a seller on underground forums claimed to hold about 46,000 employee records (6.9GB) and priced them at 3 bitcoin - roughly NT$7.4 million in media conversions - threatening to publish everything within a week if no buyer appears. TSMC says there is currently no evidence its own systems were breached: the leaked data matches data held in a cloud service provider's system, and is unrelated to production or customer data. The scale and the fields involved rest on the hacker's claims and sample, which are unverified.
What happened
The listing surfaced on underground forums including DarkForums and BreachForums, naming TSMC and offering what the seller says are some 46,000 employee records totalling 6.9GB for 3 BTC, with a sample file ('tsmc.txt') of 499 records at 19 fields each. Fields circulating from that sample include names, national ID numbers and banking details - all from the seller's sample, not confirmed by TSMC. TSMC's statement says it has been notifying affected current and former employees and providing assistance, and is working with the cloud provider, investigative authorities and third-party cybersecurity firms to clarify what happened. TSMC has not named the provider, has not said which fields were actually leaked, and had announced no compensation plan as of Nov 12.
Why it matters
This is a supply-chain breach, not a fab breach - and that distinction cuts both ways. TSMC's crown jewels (process technology, customer designs) are not implicated on current evidence, which limits the strategic damage; but the incident shows that even the world's most security-conscious chipmaker can have its people's data walk out through a vendor's system, exposing tens of thousands of employees to identity theft and targeted phishing - a pointed risk at a company whose staff are perennial intelligence targets. The seller's one-week deadline also creates a dated pressure point: if the data is dumped, the unverified claims become checkable facts. The takeaway: TSMC's statement credibly separates 'our systems' from 'our data' - but for the employees in the file, that distinction is no comfort.
Sources agree / disagree
Taiwanese outlets (Commercial Times, BusinessNext, iThome, FTV) report the same core account from TSMC's statement, and agree on the split: the leak itself is company-confirmed; the 46,000-record scale, the 3 BTC price and the sample fields are the seller's claims. No outlet has independently verified the dataset. Media conversions of the 3 BTC price vary slightly (NT$7.25-7.4 million). Trust label: Core fact officially confirmed by TSMC; claimed scale and data fields unverified.
Still uncertain
Which cloud provider held the data and how it got out, how many people are actually affected, which fields were truly leaked, and whether the seller publishes the data at the one-week deadline all remain open.
