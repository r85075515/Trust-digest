10:T60a,What happened
According to reporting by KJZZ on November 26 and a report released by Prospect Partners and Hawk Eye Strategies, federal spending on water conservation in the Colorado River Basin declined by more than 70% during the first 18 months of the Trump administration. Across five Department of the Interior and Department of Agriculture programs, net commitments fell from USD 675 million to USD 200 million amid historic drought pressures.

Why it matters
The Colorado River supports water supplies across the American West as prolonged drought strains reservoirs and water management. Sharp reductions in net federal commitments across five Interior and Agriculture conservation programs alter near-term funding available for basin water-saving projects.

Source differences
KJZZ published its article under "Spending on Colorado River water conservation down by 70% under Trump administration," reporting the 70% drop in federal conservation spending. Prospect Partners published the report release under "Federal Water Conservation Cut 70% in Colorado Basin During Historic Drought," detailing the reduction in Colorado Basin water conservation funding during historic drought.

Still uncertain
Future federal budget allocations for Colorado River conservation programs and how basin water users adapt to lower commitments remain subject to ongoing policy and funding decisions.
