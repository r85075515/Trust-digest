What happened
Rockström told The Guardian that climate science is foundational to the Paris Agreement and to worldwide progress on tackling global heating. Attempts to delay new scientific reports from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, he warned, threaten the global cooperation needed to confront the crisis. His message is blunt: slowing the panel down invites disaster rather than buying time.

Why it matters
IPCC reports are the shared factual basis for climate negotiations and national emissions policy, so a delay is not a scheduling footnote — it pushes back the evidence that governments use to set targets and measure progress. Rockström connects a procedural fight directly to a substantive outcome: if the science arrives late, the cooperation built on it frays. That framing is why a single interview carries weight beyond one scientist's opinion.
