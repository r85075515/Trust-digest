10:T761,「What happened」
Law enforcement authorities from 11 countries, backed by Europol, INTERPOL, and the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO), dismantled 57 illicit Internet Protocol Television (IPTV) streaming services and traced more than 26 million euros (approximately 28.5 million USD) in cryptocurrency transactions linked to piracy networks. The EUIPO confirmed its technical and analytical support to Europol in mapping crypto payment flows used to monetize unauthorized broadcasts.

「Why it matters」
Illegal IPTV rings increasingly rely on cryptocurrency rails to obscure subscription revenues and launder proceeds across borders. Tracing over 26 million euros in digital asset flows alongside taking 57 streaming operations offline marks a significant enforcement step in linking blockchain financial intelligence directly with intellectual property takedowns.

「Source differences」
EUIPO published an official release titled "EUIPO supports Europol operation to trace cryptocurrency payments", detailing its institutional role in tracking illicit crypto transactions behind pirate IPTV networks. NL Times reported "Dutch police join international crackdown on illegal IPTV services", specifying the participation of police from 11 countries including the Netherlands and the disruption of 57 services, while Help Net Security published "Police disrupt 57 illegal IPTV services and trace €26 million in crypto", noting INTERPOL and AAPA involvement.

「Still uncertain」
Authorities have not yet disclosed the total number of suspects arrested or indicted across each of the 11 participating jurisdictions, nor how much of the traced 26 million euros in cryptocurrency has been frozen or seized.
