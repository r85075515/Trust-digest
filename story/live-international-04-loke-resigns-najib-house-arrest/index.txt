10:Td73,Malaysia's Transport Minister Anthony Loke announced his resignation with immediate effect on Nov 10, hours after the Kuala Lumpur High Court cleared former prime minister Najib Razak to serve the remainder of his SRC International sentence under house arrest, Bloomberg reports.
What happened
Judge Aslam Zainuddin set aside the High Court's Nov 6 order and issued a fresh one: under the conditional royal pardon granted on Sept 18 by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Sultan Ibrahim, Najib will serve the rest of his reduced SRC sentence at his Jalan Langgak Duta home in Kuala Lumpur until Aug 23, 2028, after which he is to return to Kajang Prison to serve his separate 1MDB sentence. The path took two months: Najib's 12-year SRC term was halved to six years by the Pardons Board in 2024 and his fine cut to RM50 million; in September the King allowed him to serve the remainder at home once the fine was paid. UMNO, the party Najib once led, paid the fine on Nov 6, but the Attorney-General's Chambers sought clarification of the court order, delaying the transfer until Tuesday's ruling; Najib began his house arrest the same day. Loke then honoured his pledge. He had announced his position on Sept 19, after an emergency meeting of his party, the Democratic Action Party (DAP), and tendered his resignation letter to Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim on Sept 28; Anwar asked him to stay until Najib actually went home. In Tuesday's statement, Loke said the resignation was in line with the position he announced last Friday and took effect immediately.
Why it matters
This is the first time the political aftershocks of the 1MDB corruption scandal have cost Anwar's cabinet a minister — and the minister is secretary-general of the DAP, the largest party in the governing coalition. Loke's line is deliberate: the DAP respects the King's pardon prerogative, but letting Najib serve at home sends the wrong message about accountability. He resigned only the ministry, keeping his party post and his parliamentary seat, and no other DAP minister followed him — so this is not a coalition rupture. It is a political bill handed to Anwar: the controversy over favourable treatment for Najib has moved from the courts into his cabinet, and the Transport Ministry succession, with the East Coast Rail Link and the Johor Bahru–Singapore RTS among its flagship projects, is now an immediate problem.
Sources agree / disagree
Bloomberg and national outlets across Malaysia agree on the core facts: the fresh court order, house arrest through August 2028, and the immediate resignation. Political reactions diverge: fellow DAP minister Hannah Yeoh said she should have been the one to step down, but Loke wanted DAP ministers to remain in government to fulfil their promises to voters; MCA president Wee Ka Siong said nobody forced Loke to resign. These are positions, not factual disputes. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — the resignation rests on Loke's own statement and the court order is a matter of record.
Still uncertain
The terms and conditions of Najib's house arrest have yet to be announced, and no successor at the Transport Ministry has been named; Loke said only that he would ensure a smooth handover and that ministry programmes would continue as planned. Whether Anwar reshuffles his cabinet, and when, is unknown.
