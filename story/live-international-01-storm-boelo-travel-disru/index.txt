10:T7a7,The UK is bracing for a second storm this weekend, and the north is taking the worst of it. Storm Boelo arrived a day after Storm Austen, bringing heavy, frequent showers and winds that forecasters say could top 80mph.
What happened
An amber warning for wind will remain in place until 9pm on Sunday, after Storm Boelo battered the north of the UK. Scotland, parts of northern England and Northern Ireland are forecast to be hit with the strongest winds from the country's second storm in as many days. Storm Boelo's winds will peak from Saturday evening, leaving areas in the warning zone braced for gusts of 60-70mph, while the BBC's headline puts the top gusts at 80mph. Travel is disrupted and events are cancelled, and thousands remain without power after Friday's Storm Austen.
Why it matters
The judgment for readers in the north is practical, not abstract: two storms in two days means the second one hits infrastructure that never recovered from the first. Power cuts and travel disruption compound when crews are already stretched, and an amber warning lasting into Sunday night leaves little room to catch up. Anyone travelling or outdoors in the warning zone should plan around the peak on Saturday evening.
Sources agree / disagree
Sky News, the BBC and The Guardian all cover the same storm with consistent facts: second named storm of the season, amber warning to Sunday night, disruption in the north. The gust figures differ in wording — 60-70mph for warning-zone gusts, up to 80mph for peak gusts in the BBC headline — so this digest prints both rather than picking one. Trust label: consistent reporting, with the gust range shown, not smoothed over.
