What happened
Yan said she was rushed to hospital after four days of severe vertigo, then spent half a month waiting before returning for her report on November 25. She wrote that she woke up every morning feeling like an old woman and woke in pain on two consecutive nights. Her doctor diagnosed vestibular migraine and ordered her off five foods, and a blunt eight-character red stamp on her health-education sheet, together with her exchange with her doctor, drew fellow sufferers out online.

Why it matters
A celebrity health post became a peer-support thread: patients with the same condition recognised themselves in her description and spoke up. The episode also shows how a manageable diagnosis gets sensationalised: one headline in the same feed called her condition an incurable disease.

Sources agree / disagree
ETtoday's account, drawn from Yan's own post, reports the diagnosis, the five banned foods and the red stamp. A LINE TODAY heat headline frames the same episode as an incurable, lifelong condition, a far darker framing that the ETtoday account does not support. The two sources disagree on framing, not on the underlying events.

Still uncertain
The five banned foods are not named in the snippets, and the exact wording of the eight-character stamp is not quoted. No medical detail beyond the diagnosis name is given, so readers should not treat the post as medical advice.
