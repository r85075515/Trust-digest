10:T83e,Costco members can buy Nintendo eShop credit at a discount in a bundle offer reported by Slickdeals, paying $160 for $200 of credit or $80 for $100 of credit, delivered by email.

What happened
Slickdeals reported that Costco Wholesale has select Nintendo eShop eGift Card bundles on sale, valid for Costco members only, with email delivery. The headline prices are $200 for $160 and $100 for $80. The listing, credited to community member captainguy, notes that buyers must log in to a Costco account with an active membership to view pricing and complete a purchase. One available option named in the text is a four-pack of $25 Nintendo eShop eGift Cards with email delivery. The post carried a Thumb Score of +24 in the feed excerpt.

Why it matters
Both headline tiers work out to the same proposition: paying twenty percent less than the face value of the eShop credit. For members who already buy Nintendo eShop content, the decision is mainly about how much credit to hold at once — the $200 bundle requires more money upfront for the same proportional saving as the $100 bundle. The membership requirement also means the price cannot be checked or claimed by non-members through this listing.

Sources agree / disagree
This cluster contains two Slickdeals items from the same outlet, and they are about different products: the Nintendo bundle in the headline and a separate PlayStation gift card bundle at $150 for $120 or $100 for $80, including two $75 PlayStation eGift Cards for $120 and four $25 cards for $80. The $200 and $150 figures therefore do not conflict; they belong to different brands' bundles that happen to share the same discount structure.

Still uncertain
The feed text is truncated and does not state the sale end date, purchase limits, or whether the $200 bundle is a single card or a combination of denominations beyond the four-pack of $25 cards named as one option. It also does not say whether email delivery timing or regional restrictions apply.
