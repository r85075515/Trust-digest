10:T868,The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has rejected Summit Therapeutics' closely watched PD-1/VEGF drug ivonescimab for a form of lung cancer, sending Summit's shares down and delaying the company's first chance at a U.S. approval. The decision turns on a single hard endpoint: the drug slowed the disease, but did not show patients living longer.
What happened
In a complete response letter, the FDA cited a late-stage trial in which ivonescimab significantly delayed disease progression yet failed to demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in overall survival. BioPharma Dive reports the agency refused to overlook that survival miss, and ET Pharma frames it the same way: the FDA declined to approve the Summit lung cancer drug after the trial miss. The rejection sets back Summit's first attempt to win U.S. approval for the drug, which had been closely watched as a PD-1/VEGF bispecific — a class designed to attack tumors on two fronts at once.
Why it matters
Overall survival is the endpoint regulators weigh most heavily in cancer drugs: delaying progression matters, but without a survival benefit the FDA was unwilling to approve. For Summit, the decision is a commercial as well as a scientific setback — the U.S. approval was meant to be its first. For patients, the practical question is what Summit does next: a redesigned trial, a narrower indication, or an appeal built on the progression data.
Sources agree / disagree
BioPharma Dive and ET Pharma agree on the core facts — the complete response letter, the progression benefit, the survival miss, and the rejection. Neither outlet gives the trial's size, the magnitude of the progression benefit, or Summit's response, so those details are missing from this card rather than disputed.
