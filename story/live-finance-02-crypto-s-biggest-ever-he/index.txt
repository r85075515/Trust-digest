The exchange temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals on Thursday while it conducted security checks, froze funds, and worked with law enforcement, according to CNBC, The Hill, and Bloomberg News.
What happened
Binance estimated the impact at 6.3 billion dollars and said around 3 billion dollars in crypto assets were frozen, with cryptocurrency firms freezing about 3 billion dollars in assets after the suspected hack. The exchange described the situation as a security issue and temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals while it conducted security checks, froze funds, and worked with law enforcement. Binance vowed to cover all losses, according to the summaries.
Why it matters
A 6.3 billion dollar impact estimate at the largest crypto exchange is a direct test of whether customer losses can be contained without wider market contagion. The direct judgment is that the pledge to cover all losses is the decisive fact for users, because it promises that customers will not bear the loss if Binance honours it, while the suspension shows that normal access remains interrupted.
Sources agree / disagree
CNBC, The Hill, and Bloomberg News agree on the 6.3 billion dollar impact, the roughly 3 billion dollars frozen, the suspension of deposits and withdrawals, and the pledge to cover losses. The flagged disagreement between 3 and 6.3 is a false positive: the 3 refers to billions of dollars in frozen assets, while the 6.3 refers to billions of dollars in estimated total impact, so the numbers refer to different things. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — three outlets agree on the core figures and the pledge.
