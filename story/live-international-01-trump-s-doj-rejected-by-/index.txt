10:T872,The Supreme Court on Monday rejected the Trump administration's last-minute bid to force California to set aside more than 20,000 ballots that President Donald Trump claims were cast by noncitizens, dealing the administration a high-profile setback in its midterm election fight with the state.

What happened
The Justice Department asked the court to intervene in its feud with California over the state's voter rolls and to require officials to separate ballots from registered voters it suspects are noncitizens. A federal judge on the eve of Election Day had already barred the administration from demanding that California set aside those ballots, finding the effort likely violates the Constitution. The Supreme Court then denied the administration's request to hear the case, leaving the lower court's block in place. The administration has described its fight as an effort to ensure all eligible voters can vote and have their ballots counted, as the law requires. Trump has pressed claims of widespread illegal voting by noncitizens that the reporting describes as unproven.

Why it matters
Courts had already rejected the Justice Department's attempts to force California to separate the ballots, and the Supreme Court's refusal extends that rejection to the highest court. The decision removes an immediate federal threat to the counting of the disputed ballots and weakens the administration's legal leverage in its broader election claims against California.

Sources agree and disagree
Axios, Bloomberg News and NPR News agree on the core outcome: the Supreme Court rejected the administration's bid over the disputed California ballots. Some coverage describes the fight as concerning thousands of ballots, while the administration's case concerns more than 20,000, a difference in scale of description rather than a conflicting outcome.

Still uncertain
The feed snippets do not give the court's reasoning or any vote breakdown, and they do not say whether the administration will pursue further legal challenges over California's voter rolls. Full reporting from the outlets will be needed for those details.
