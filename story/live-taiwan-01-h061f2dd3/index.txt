A second scholar at the same event offered a different explanation for why the purges continue.

What happened
Ma Zhenkun said that, whether Xi intended it or not, the substantive effect of the large-scale clearing out of senior generals is that the People's Liberation Army has lost both the ability and the strength to intervene in the development of the political situation. Separately, after the close of the Chinese Communist Party's 20th Central Committee fifth plenary session, scholar Cai Wenxuan told the seminar that Xi's anti-corruption campaign also strikes people he personally promoted. Cai said the reason is that Xi must continually find new enemies within the party in order to maintain the legitimacy and justification for long-term rule.

Why it matters
The two scholars, as reported, address the same purge from different angles. Ma focuses on its institutional consequence: a military leadership that, in his assessment, can no longer intervene in political developments. Cai focuses on its political function: a continuing search for enemies inside the party that, in his account, sustains long-term rule. Together the remarks frame the purge both as a weakening of the military's political role and as a mechanism that the second scholar says serves the leader's continued hold on power.
