What happened
The outage hit vital UK services, with the NHS suffering what was described as serious and widespread disruption, and people were told of possible delays in receiving prescriptions. Hospital staff resorted to paper records, and the disruption sent some sectors back to the 1990s, with court staff among those affected. Courts and governments worldwide felt the impact of the outage, which sidelined work and stymied communication.

Why it matters
For some offices the outage meant an extended lunch break, but for courts and governments it raised questions about the vulnerabilities of the technology underpinning justice and governance. The disruption also fuelled concerns about an over-reliance on a small number of tech companies for essential public services.
