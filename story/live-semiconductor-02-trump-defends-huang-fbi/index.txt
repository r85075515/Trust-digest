10:Tbb2,US President Donald Trump said on Monday that he had spoken with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and publicly defended him, confirming for the first time that the FBI is looking into the affair-and-espionage scandal, Reuters and AP report.
What happened
Speaking at a White House briefing on Monday, Trump said he had called Huang that day. "I asked him that question 'Did you have any idea that she was a spy?' He said 'I had no idea that she was a spy' and I really believe him," Trump said, calling Huang "a very honorable man." He added: "I know the FBI is looking into it in terms of something else, and I think that he'll be in good shape." The call marks a shift from Sunday, when Trump praised Huang but said the two had not yet spoken about the allegations. The scandal began when the New York Times reported that Alice Huang, a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, is suspected by US intelligence of passing information to Beijing and had a close relationship with Huang; Huang admitted on Sunday in a staff memo that he had been unfaithful to his wife, and Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation while keeping him as CEO. Nvidia shares hit a seven-month low on Monday.
Why it matters
A sitting president has now publicly vouched for the central figure of an active FBI matter involving his close Silicon Valley ally — before any investigator has reported. Congress is moving the other way: on Monday, Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren and Republican Senator Jim Banks said in a joint statement that Huang's confirmation of the relationship makes the national-security concerns "even more urgent," and lawmakers expect chip-security bills aimed at China's access to advanced AI chips to gain momentum in the fiscal 2027 defense authorization. The story is no longer only about one CEO's conduct; it is becoming a fight over who controls the investigation's narrative, and over export-control law.
Sources agree / disagree
Reuters and AP agree on the call, the quotes and the FBI confirmation. AP's fuller quote carries a qualifier Reuters' headline omits: Trump said the FBI is looking into it "in terms of something else," which leaves open whether Huang himself is a target. Both note the New York Times' earlier reporting that Trump was briefed on the intelligence about a year ago and chose not to act; the White House did not respond to Reuters on the relationship. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — direct presidential remarks at an on-record White House briefing, carried consistently by two wire services; the FBI inquiry's scope is officially unspecified.
Still uncertain
What exactly the FBI is examining — Huang, Alice Huang and Megaspeed, or chip smuggling more broadly — has not been stated by the FBI or the White House. Whether Huang knew of the alleged intelligence link rests so far on his own account relayed by Trump.
