Gasoline prices alone are up 34% from a year ago, adding to the inflation pressures squeezing households ahead of the midterm elections.
What happened
NYT Economy reports that Democrats blamed Trump after the CPI release showed energy-driven inflation at 3.7%. The report keeps pressure on both the president and the Federal Reserve to tame intensifying price pressures, and the politics are shifting: inflation and economic frustrations have expanded the map of midterm districts deep into Republican territory. In a related energy-price data point, Bloomberg reports Australians are pulling back at the pump as fuel prices surge — diesel sales dropped in August by the most since 2022, and the decline in petrol sales deepened. The BBC, separately, covers bipartisan backlash against data-centre development and how it could shape the midterms.
Why it matters
3.7% inflation with gasoline up 34% year on year is the kind of number that decides elections: it is visible every day, it hits every household, and it hands the opposition a simple story. That districts are now stretching into Republican territory shows the White House's economic message is losing ground where it can least afford it. The Australia data point is a reminder that the energy-price squeeze is global, not just American.
