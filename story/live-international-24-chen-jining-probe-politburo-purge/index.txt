10:Tb85,China's anti-graft watchdog announced on Tuesday evening that Chen Jining — Shanghai party secretary, Politburo member and one of the most closely watched rising figures in Chinese politics — is under investigation for "suspected serious violations of discipline and laws", the standard euphemism for corruption. The Central Commission for Discipline Inspection gave no details. Chen has made no statement and could not be reached for comment.

What happened: Chen, 62, is the fourth member of the 24-man Politburo formed in 2022 to fall, after generals He Weidong and Zhang Youxia and regional chief Ma Xingrui; the body now has 21 acting members, in what the Wall Street Journal calls the largest purge at this level since 1976. An Imperial College London-trained environmental scientist, Chen rose from Tsinghua University president to environment minister in 2015, then Beijing mayor, entering the Politburo in 2022 and taking the Shanghai post — historically the springboard to the Politburo Standing Committee, held before elevation by both Xi Jinping and Jiang Zemin, and by Chen's predecessor Li Qiang, now premier. Chen was widely seen as a strong contender for the Standing Committee at the 21st Party Congress in fall 2027. His last public appearance was on November 19, inspecting a shipping conglomerate's headquarters. Shanghai's party leadership held a late-night meeting pledging loyalty to Beijing, the city's Jiefang Daily reported. No successor has been named.

Why it matters: The Shanghai-to-Standing-Committee pathway — reliable for nearly four decades — is now broken, and with it the main tool outsiders used to read Beijing's succession politics. Analysts read the timing, a year before the congress where Xi is expected to take a fourth term, as Xi clearing the ground and making loyalty the sole criterion: as one expert put it, no one other than Xi is indispensable. For foreign business, Chen was the face of a more open Shanghai; his fall removes a known interlocutor and chills local officials' willingness to experiment.

Sources agree/disagree: Reuters, Bloomberg, the Wall Street Journal and CNN agree on the announcement, Chen's profile and the purge count. Interpretation differs in emphasis — Bloomberg frames a political "earthquake", Reuters stresses the broken succession pathway — but no source disputes the facts, and none has any detail of the alleged wrongdoing, because the watchdog disclosed none.

Still uncertain: Everything about the case itself — the alleged conduct, whether Chen is detained, whether he will be expelled and tried (in past cases, near-certain conviction followed), who succeeds him in Shanghai, and whether the purge reaches further into the Politburo before the 2027 congress.
