What happened
TechCrunch's headline emphasizes the SpaceX-powered flight; The Verge's stresses the full stunt list — flying, upside-down driving, sub-second 0–60. Both note the heavy caveats: a production version would face serious restrictions, if it ever ships.
Why it matters
The takeaway is two-layered: as engineering theater it is unmatched — no rival can stage this demo. As a product, the thruster package's legality, safety certification, and price remain entirely open. Treat it as a halo project, not a lineup addition.
Sources agree / disagree
TechCrunch and The Verge describe the same Waco demo with consistent stunt details. Trust label: Multi-source agreement on what was shown. Figures like "3 vs 30" flagged in snippets appear to be headline artifacts, not real conflicts.
Still uncertain
Production timeline, pricing, and regulatory approval are all undisclosed — and cold-gas thrusters on a street car face obvious safety-certification hurdles no automaker has cleared before.
