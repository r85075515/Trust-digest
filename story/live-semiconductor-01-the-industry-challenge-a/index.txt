What happened
Demand for custom silicon is rising because off-the-shelf chips no longer fit: workloads need architectures optimized for specific tasks, memory bandwidth, latency, and energy, the piece argues. The story is anchored by Applied Materials president and CEO Gary Dickerson appearing with EPIC Center founding partners on October 12 in Sunnyvale, California. The same feed batch carried two unrelated chip stories: EE Times on why chip-design AI benchmarks must go beyond functional correctness, and Semiconductor Engineering's October 20 technical paper roundup, including Argonne National Laboratory and University of Illinois work on agentic AI for heterogeneous system design and Sandia work on GAAFET leakage paths.
Why it matters
The verdict is a shift in where the money goes: when the constraint moves from transistor density to system-level integration — memory, packaging, power — the winners are equipment and EDA players selling the whole stack, not just the lithography step. For investors, that reframes which capex lines matter.
