10:Ta34,A skyscraper in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was struck again on Tuesday, five days after a first Houthi attack on the same tower caused damage but no casualties. The Saudi-led coalition called the strike a "terrorist attack"; Saudi civil defence figures carried by regional outlets put the injured at 11 — two Saudis, two Indians, three Nepalis and four Egyptians — with two in critical condition. Some of the injured were workers repairing damage from the first strike.

What happened: AFP witnesses identified the building as the 385-metre PIF Tower, headquarters of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and the tallest tower in the capital; the coalition's statement did not name the building. The tower was first hit on Thursday, November 19, in an attack the Houthis claimed, which blew out windows across seven floors without injuries; on November 8 the Houthis had hit a Saudi Aramco building in the same district. Saudi forces said they intercepted two drones over the eastern province the same day, and coalition aircraft retaliated against Houthi targets including Hodeidah port; the Houthis claimed 26 people were killed in strikes in Lahj, a figure not independently verified. Britain sanctioned four senior Houthi leaders the same day.

Why it matters: The financial district is the showcase of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's Vision 2030 — home to the Saudi stock exchange and offices of firms such as Goldman Sachs — and it is now being hit repeatedly. A second strike on the same sovereign-wealth headquarters within a week, this time with casualties among foreign workers, signals the Houthis can sustain pressure on the capital's symbolic core despite coalition air defences, raising the risk premium on Saudi assets and Red Sea shipping alike.

Sources agree/disagree: Reuters, AFP, Bloomberg and the Wall Street Journal agree a tower in the financial district was struck Tuesday and that the coalition labelled it a terrorist attack. The identification of the building as the PIF Tower rests on AFP witnesses, as the coalition did not name it; the injury breakdown comes from Saudi civil defence via regional media, and the Houthis' Lahj death toll is their own claim.

Still uncertain: Whether the Houthis formally claim this second strike, the full extent of structural damage, the condition of the two critical patients, and how far Riyadh escalates in Yemen in response.
