What happened
Machado left Venezuela almost a year ago to receive the Nobel Peace Prize, according to The Guardian’s account. She had been out of the country for nearly ten months when she announced her return on Sunday lunchtime. Her departure came shortly before the US government seized President Nicolás Maduro, after which Delcy Rodríguez became interim president. Machado’s stated purpose in returning, as reported by The New York Times, is to challenge the leaders who sidelined her and to press for new elections.
Why it matters
The verdict is straightforward: the most prominent face of Venezuela’s opposition is no longer campaigning from abroad. Her physical return raises the stakes for the interim government, which now has to decide how to respond to a Nobel laureate demanding elections at home rather than from exile. For readers, the next question is not whether Machado is back, but whether her return produces a political opening or a crackdown.
