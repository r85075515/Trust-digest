10:T5f1,What happened
Paris-based pharmaceutical company Sanofi announced an agreement to acquire U.S.-based Insmed Incorporated in an all-cash transaction at USD 175 per share, representing a total equity value of roughly USD 41 billion. Both companies' boards have approved the deal.

Why it matters
The acquisition bolsters Sanofi's respiratory medicine portfolio ahead of future patent expirations on blockbuster drug Dupixent. Sanofi gains Insmed's marketed therapies Brinsupri (brensocatib) for non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis and Arikayce for Mycobacterium avium complex lung disease, plus phase 3 candidate treprostinil palmitil inhalation powder.

Source differences
Sanofi's official announcement ("Press Release: Sanofi to acquire Insmed in strategic step that strengthens long-term future") outlines the strategic rationale for expanding long-term growth drivers. Fierce Pharma ("Sanofi inks $41B Insmed buyout for post-Dupixent growth drivers") focuses on how the USD 175-per-share buyout addresses Sanofi's post-Dupixent revenue transition. AJMC ("Sanofi to Acquire Insmed, Expanding Respiratory Treatment Portfolio") adds commercial context, noting brensocatib generated USD 400.1 million in Q3 2026 revenue.

Still uncertain
Antitrust regulatory review timelines across the United States and Europe, shareholder tender completion dates, and exact closing conditions remain pending.
