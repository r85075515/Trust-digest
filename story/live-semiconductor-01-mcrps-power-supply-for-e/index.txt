10:T7b6,A modular common redundant power supply, or MCRPS, has become the practical standard for enterprise server racks, combining a compact mechanical envelope, redundancy support and a common output. The standard arrives as data centers hit a different wall: getting approval to connect to the electric grid can take as long as a decade.

What happened
EE Times reported that MCRPS units now serve as the practical power standard for enterprise racks. MIT Technology Review reported the parallel story: some developers are unwilling to wait for grid hookups and are instead building their own off-grid, or behind-the-meter, gas power plants to fuel the AI boom. xAI's Memphis campus has famously used dozens of natural gas turbines to power its operations.

Why it matters
Power is now the pacing item for AI infrastructure, not chips. A standardised, redundant supply like MCRPS makes racks easier to build and keep running, but it does not conjure megawatts. Developers who build their own gas plants are buying speed at the cost of emissions scrutiny and local opposition, a trade-off that will shape where the next data centers can go.

Sources agree / disagree
Two outlets cover two distinct stories, bundled into one card. EE Times covers the MCRPS power standard; MIT Technology Review covers data centers building their own gas plants. Neither outlet contradicts the other, because they report different facts. The card should be read as two stories sharing a theme — data-center power — not as one event.

Still uncertain
The snippets give no adoption figures for MCRPS, no cost comparison with older supplies, and no count of data centers running their own gas plants beyond the xAI Memphis example. How widespread behind-the-meter generation becomes is untested.
