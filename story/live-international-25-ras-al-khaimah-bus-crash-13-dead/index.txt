10:T92e,A passenger bus carrying 41 people slammed into a truck stranded on the roadside of Al Watan Road in Ras Al Khaimah in the early hours of Tuesday, killing 13 people. Eleven died in the initial collision and two more later in hospital. The passengers were returning from Oman after completing UAE visa renewals. India's consulate in Dubai confirmed Indian nationals were among the victims — 11 of the dead, according to officials cited by regional media — and said it was assisting the families, with the consul general visiting them.

What happened: The truck had stopped on the roadside after a technical breakdown when the bus hit it, Ras Al Khaimah police said. National Ambulance crews treated casualties at the scene with support from Dubai Ambulance. Injury counts differ by measure: 26 people were treated at the scene, while 14 were reported hospitalized. It is the UAE's second deadly mass-casualty road crash in two weeks, after the 23-vehicle Fujairah pileup on November 13 that killed 14.

Why it matters: The victims were largely expatriate workers on a routine "visa run" — the border trip built into the UAE's migrant labour system — and the crash has reopened questions about overnight bus operations, roadside breakdown protocols on high-speed roads, and rest rules for long-haul drivers. For the Indian community, the UAE's largest expatriate population, the consulate's rapid identification and family support became the story's second thread.

Sources agree/disagree: UAE police and interior ministry figures, Reuters, Khaleej Times and Gulf News agree on 13 dead and the stranded-truck sequence. Injury figures diverge by definition rather than contradiction — 26 treated at the scene (National Ambulance) versus 14 hospitalized — and early reports on Tuesday initially put the toll at 11 before two hospital deaths raised it to 13.

Still uncertain: The cause of the collision — speed, visibility, driver fatigue or the stranded truck's warning setup — has not been officially determined; the full nationalities list beyond the Indian victims, and whether charges or regulatory changes follow, are also open.
