10:T7bf,The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) allowed Nvidia and AMD to sell hundreds of thousands of advanced GPUs to entities that the Department of War classifies as "Chinese military companies" helping Beijing build its military.

What happened
The disclosure came from Brian Mast, chair of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, who asked BIS to provide information about Nvidia's and AMD's sales. Lawmakers released confidential information showing the Commerce Department had approved sales of more than 800,000 AI chips to Chinese companies, some with military ties. The Register describes the scale as "bucketloads of chips," while the New York Times headline names Nvidia and Megaspeed, a company tied to the Chinese military, though the snippets do not explain Megaspeed's role.

Why it matters
The judgment here is about enforcement, not technology: the same department that restricts advanced chips also approved more than 800,000 of them for sale to firms on a military-companies list. That contradiction is why a congressional chairman, rather than the department itself, is the source of the disclosure.

Sources agree / disagree
The Register and the New York Times agree on the core facts: BIS approved large-scale GPU sales to military-linked Chinese companies, and the disclosure came through the House Foreign Affairs Committee. The figures are consistent — "hundreds of thousands" in one account and "more than 800,000" in the other describe the same scale, and the two phrasings involve no substantive conflict.

Still uncertain
The snippets do not say which Nvidia or AMD chip models were approved, when the approvals were granted, what Megaspeed bought or did, or whether any sales have been blocked since. The full list of recipient companies is not in the snippets.
