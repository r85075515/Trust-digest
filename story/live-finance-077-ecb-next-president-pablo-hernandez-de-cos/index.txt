He will succeed Christine Lagarde (克里斯蒂娜・拉加德), who announced plans to step down nine months early.

The selection stabilizes leadership succession at the world's second-most influential central bank as the eurozone navigates sluggish growth, sovereign debt pressures, and inflation management. Hernández de Cos secured backing from France, Germany, and a majority of eurozone governments after the Netherlands withdrew support for rival candidate Klaas Knot (克拉斯・諾特).

Formal confirmation still requires non-binding consultations with the European Parliament and the ECB's Governing Council before final adoption.
