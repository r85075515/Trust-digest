What happened
The Guardian summary says Apple told investors the higher prices will eat into its profit margin, and its shares fell in after-hours trading after the warning. It also says a decision to raise prices has offset some of the impact, as revenues were stronger than expected. Tom's Hardware, citing Reuters, says Apple expects to pay more for memory in the December quarter, with chief executive John Ternus describing the current price increases as unprecedented. The company has so far softened the blow by using memory bought earlier at lower cost, according to that summary, but that buffer is presented as temporary as new purchases reflect higher prices.
Why it matters
The story matters because memory is a core component in iPhones and other devices, so a sustained surge affects margins, pricing, and supply planning at once. Competition with data centres for the same supplies links consumer electronics costs to demand for computing infrastructure. If Apple, a very large buyer, describes the increases as unprecedented and expects margin pressure, smaller buyers may face the same market without the same purchasing cushion, though the snippets do not quantify that wider effect.
