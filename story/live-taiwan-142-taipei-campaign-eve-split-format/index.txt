Supporters gathered across more than 320 independent bookstores, cafes, and churches in Taipei to watch together, while President Lai Ching-te (賴清德) and Taipei 101 Chairwoman Janet Chia (賈永婕) made guest appearances on the broadcast, which drew over 520,000 views.

The contrasting election-eve strategies highlight how urban road-permit rules and public sensitivity to traffic disruption are reshaping traditional Taiwanese campaign mobilization. Turning a canceled street rally into a decentralized digital watch-party network marks a notable experiment in Taiwan's electoral history.

Focus Taiwan, CNA, and Wealth Magazine all verified the split format between Chiang's Ketagalan Boulevard rally and Shen's online livestream, as well as the appearances by Lai Ching-te and Janet Chia. Reports noted differing perspectives on the earlier road-use controversy, with DPP supporters alleging rivals pre-booked prime venues in October, whereas city authorities cited traffic spillover concerns.

Whether decentralized online watch parties can match the ballot-box mobilization power of traditional street rallies in Taipei's closely watched mayoral contest will only become clear once votes are counted on November 28.
