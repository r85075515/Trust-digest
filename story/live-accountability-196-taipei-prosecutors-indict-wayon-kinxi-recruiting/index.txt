Prosecutors allege that after being hired by Shanghai-based Wayon Electronics Co., Yang established Kinxi in April 2025 without approval from the Ministry of Economic Affairs' Administration of Commerce, renting office space and hiring staff to conduct sales, customer negotiations, and engineering recruitment in Taiwan.

Taiwanese authorities treat unauthorized semiconductor and electronics recruiting by Chinese firms as a direct threat to industrial competitiveness and national security. Prosecutors asked the court to impose a prison sentence of at least 16 months and order the forfeiture of 48,000 USD—roughly 3,000 USD per month—allegedly paid by Wayon to cover salaries, rent, and Yang's compensation while staff reported back via WeChat.

Focus Taiwan (CNA) and Taipei Times both report the same core indictment facts, including the 16-month minimum sentence request, the 48,000 USD forfeiture figure, and the April 2025 incorporation of Kinxi, though the defendants' allegations remain unverified by a court verdict. Chinese-language reports by TaiSounds and Liberty Times further identify the Chinese parent as Shanghai Wayon Electronics (上海維安電子), noting its background linked to the Shanghai Academy of Sciences, while Liberty Times' headline refers to the defendant as male whereas Focus Taiwan, Taipei Times, and TaiSounds identify the defendant Yang as female.

The indictment has now moved to the Taipei District Court for trial, where guilt, intent, and the final forfeiture amount remain to be adjudicated.
