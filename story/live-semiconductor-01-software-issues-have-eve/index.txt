What happened
The Register reported that the spacecraft arrived with a software problem complicating the final phase of its arrival. Nature News described the arrival as the end of an eight-year odyssey. The mission is a joint effort by the European Space Agency (ESA) and Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA). Reaching Mercury is difficult because the planet's gravity is weak, its orbit is fast, and its closeness to the Sun complicates navigation. The European and Japanese probes will trace different paths around the planet to study its surface and its surroundings, according to Nature News, whose report was published online on 21 November 2026.
Why it matters
Only two missions have reached Mercury before BepiColombo, so fresh data from its two probes could reshape understanding of the planet's surface and its environment close to the Sun. A software fault at arrival threatens that payoff just as the science phase should begin, putting the mission's early operations under pressure.

No numeric or factual conflict appeared between the two reports.
