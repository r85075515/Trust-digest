10:T652,A U.S. jury has found Huawei guilty of stealing American trade secrets, closing a years-long legal battle against the Chinese telecom giant. The verdict was handed down in a federal court in New York after a months-long trial.
What happened
The jury found Huawei guilty of bank fraud, money laundering and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Prosecutors accused the company of stealing source code and proprietary technology from several companies, including T-Mobile, Cisco and Motorola, in a case whose charges date back to 2019 and build on a 2018 U.S. Department of Justice criminal case. The BBC reports that Huawei was acquitted of racketeering, the one charge on which the jury cleared the company.
Why it matters
The verdict is straightforward: the U.S. government has now secured a criminal conviction against the world's largest telecom equipment maker on trade-secret theft, after years of sanctions, export controls and diplomatic pressure. The conviction gives Washington a court-tested finding to point to in the tech rivalry with Beijing, and it sets up a sentencing phase that will show how severely the court treats the conduct.

The BBC, CNBC, The Verge and Engadget all report the guilty verdict on trade-secret conspiracy; the BBC adds the racketeering acquittal, which the other snippets do not contradict. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — four outlets agree on the core claims.
