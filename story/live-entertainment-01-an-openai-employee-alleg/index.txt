10:T87e,A bug wrapped in toilet paper, hidden in a conference-room TV — and now a federal case at the heart of the AI industry.
What happened
Eight outlets — BBC, CNBC, TechCrunch, The Verge, Engadget, Wired, Al Jazeera, and CBS — report that an OpenAI engineer has been arrested after the company said it discovered a 'sophisticated recording device' hidden in one of its offices. The employee was identified as Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national. Prosecutors say the device, wrapped in toilet paper, was found inserted in a USB port on the back of a TV in an executive conference room, and that Alfozan allegedly used a chatbot to help plant it. He is charged with using or endeavoring to use a device to intercept oral communications, according to a Department of Justice press release. FBI Director Kash Patel said the scheme was intended to 'capture sensitive communications about the company's super intelligence technology.' The incident occurred Tuesday and comes as the ChatGPT maker, along with its competitors, faces heightened scrutiny over its safety practices. The exact purpose of the recording device remains unclear.
Why it matters
An alleged insider bugging operation at a frontier AI lab is the espionage scenario the industry has quietly feared: the most sensitive conversations are the ones about capabilities themselves. That the FBI director is personally framing the case around 'super intelligence technology' signals Washington now treats AI-lab security as a national-security matter, not just corporate housekeeping — and it lands amid already-intense scrutiny of OpenAI's safety practices.
