10:T624,Cambridge researchers have created a $10 optical sensor that delivers the precision of laboratory equipment in a device small enough for wearable technology, identifying foods, plastics and medicines with 100% accuracy in tests.

What happened
The sensor was built by Cambridge researchers as a low-cost optical device for wearable use. In tests it identified foods, plastics and medicines with 100% accuracy, and it demonstrated the ability to monitor blood glucose, alcohol and other health markers without invasive procedures. The price is part of the point: at $10, the device is framed as a medical lab shrunk to wrist size rather than a laboratory instrument with a laboratory price.

Why it matters
Wearable health tracking today mostly estimates, while a lab-grade optical sensor promises measurement. If the test accuracy holds outside the lab, routine checks for glucose, alcohol and other markers could move from clinics to wrists, which would change who can monitor their health and how often.

Sources agree and disagree
This card's main story rests on a single outlet, ScienceDaily, so there is no second source in the snippets to confirm or challenge the $10 price or the 100% test accuracy. A second bundled item about medical cannabis and sleep is a separate story and is not part of this card's main thread.

Still uncertain
