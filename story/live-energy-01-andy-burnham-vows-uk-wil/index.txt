10:T642,Andy Burnham has pledged that Britain will still hit its net zero targets even after the government approved the Jackdaw gasfield. Writing in The Guardian, he said he will not abandon the responsibility of fighting the climate crisis, describing his determination as unwavering, and promised to block new North Sea licences to keep the transition on track.
What happened
The Guardian article is Burnham's answer to the Jackdaw approval: rather than defend the gasfield, he pairs it with a forward-looking constraint. The pledge is to stop future licences, not to reverse the approval that has already been granted. A second Guardian item on the same approval is a cartoon by Martin Rowson, noted here for completeness and not expanded.
Why it matters
The piece is a test of whether climate credibility can survive a concession. Burnham is betting that voters and the climate movement will judge him on the direction of travel, blocking new licences, rather than on a single approval he did not stop. If the licence block holds, it becomes a real constraint on future fossil expansion; if it slips, the article reads as damage control.
Sources agree-disagree
This card rests on a single outlet, The Guardian's climate coverage, with two items on the same approval. There is no second outlet to corroborate or contradict the pledge. Trust label: single source only.
Still uncertain
The article summary gives no net zero timetable, no count of licences that would be blocked, and no mechanism for enforcing the block.
