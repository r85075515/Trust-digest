10:T9d3,Semiconductor progress has entered a feedback loop: AI is now accelerating the design of the very chips that run AI, a turn the futurist Ray Kurzweil (雷·庫茲韋爾) calls the semiconductor singularity. The argument, set out in Semiconductor Engineering, is that software and silicon are no longer advancing in sequence — each is now speeding up the other.

What happened
Kurzweil describes the shift as the semiconductor singularity, an extension of his Law of Accelerating Returns, under which accumulated technical capability helps create the next computing paradigm. AI is dismantling long-standing bottlenecks in integrated-circuit design and verification, and it is entering the inner loop of semiconductor engineering by reducing the effort needed to develop software, operate design tools and carry out engineering analysis. The essay draws on the author's recent white paper on the same theme. Its central condition is that real acceleration demands solutions that are secure, explainable and trustworthy from design through manufacturing — speed alone is not enough.

Why it matters
If the loop holds, chip development cycles could shorten just as demand for AI computing keeps climbing, which would matter for every company building accelerators and for the fabs that make them. The insistence on trustworthiness is the counterweight: tools that design chips must themselves be auditable, since a flaw introduced by an AI assistant could propagate into hardware used everywhere. The piece is an argument about direction rather than a report of a single breakthrough, so its value lies in the framing it offers engineers and investors.
