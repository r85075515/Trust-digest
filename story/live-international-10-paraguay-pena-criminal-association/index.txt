10:Td13,Paraguay's prosecutor's office on Friday (Nov 13) expanded its case against former President Santiago Pena, adding a criminal association charge that accuses him of heading an organized structure inside the state to protect Ueno Bank and its parent Grupo Vazquez, Reuters and Paraguayan media reported.
What happened
Prosecutor Christian Benitez told reporters the new charge is an expansion of the Nov 7 imputation, which accused Pena of breach of trust (as instigator) and self-dealing administration (as author) over the collapse of digital lender ueno. Prosecutors allege Pena held periodic clandestine meetings at the presidential residence, Mburuvicha Roga, with group president Federico Vazquez and gave instructions to avoid intervening in the bank, allowing it to keep operating and taking deposits despite warnings about its deteriorating finances. The criminal association charge also names former central bank (BCP) president Carlos Carvallo, former banking superintendent Holger Insfran, and former BCP board members Liana Caballero, Miguel Mora and Marco Aurelio Gonzalez; the three ex-board members were detained on Friday and Insfran was arrested at his home on Thursday. Prosecutors also raided the presidential residence, seizing a phone, a computer and some 500 pages of Superintendency documents on Ueno Bank. Benitez said the expected penalty for Pena remains up to 15 years in prison. Pena, removed from office on Nov 6 in a fast-track Senate impeachment, is in preventive detention at the Presidential Escort Regiment in Asuncion.
Why it matters
The expansion moves the case from individual wrongdoing to an alleged capture of the state itself: prosecutors say Pena placed trusted or corporately linked figures in the regulator's key posts - most pointedly Carvallo, who left the presidency of Financiera Ueno on Aug 14, 2023 and was named central bank chief by Pena on Sep 7, 2023 - and that the BCP's board knew of alerts from June 2026 yet did not act. Ueno, which the central bank intervened on Nov 3 and ordered dissolved over solvency problems tied to loans to linked companies, had absorbed large public funds during Pena's mandate, much of it from pension funds, and its failure left depositors cut off from their accounts. Prosecutors also classify criminal association and breach of trust as predicate offences for money laundering.
Sources agree / disagree
Reuters, ABC Color, Ultima Hora and multiple regional outlets agree on the new charge, the co-defendants and the 15-year expected penalty. Details of the prosecution's theory (the residence meetings, the appointment chronology, the June 2026 alerts) come from the imputation document as reported by Paraguayan outlets; all of it remains the prosecutors' allegation, not a finding of guilt.
Still uncertain
No response from Pena or his defence appears in the reports so far, and a judge must still rule on precautionary measures and whether the expanded imputation proceeds. The parallel track against the bank's own executives - ten detained, including Miguel Vazquez - is separate from the case against Pena and Carvallo, and how the two converge is not yet clear.
