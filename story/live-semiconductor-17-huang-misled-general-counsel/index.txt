10:Td3d,Jensen Huang misled Nvidia's own top lawyer about his affair with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, telling general counsel Tim Teter and chief marketing officer Alison Wagonfeld in October that his interactions with her were ordinary client business, according to a Reuters report published on Nov 14 based on two people familiar with the matter and internal correspondence the news agency reviewed.
What happened
Reuters said it reviewed several exchanges from October - on condition of not quoting them verbatim - and is the first to report on the correspondence. In two separate messages that month, Huang told company leaders including Teter and Wagonfeld that the interactions reflected standard business dealings with customers. It is unclear when the relationship became intimate; photos posted online and verified by Reuters show the two together in the US, China and elsewhere as far back as December 2023. Reuters reviewed only the October exchanges, not earlier ones from 2025. The report lands two days after Huang resigned as CEO on Nov 12. His Nov 8 apology statement acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife but did not name Alice Huang, a former top executive of Nvidia customer Megaspeed International whom the US was investigating over suspicions of spying for China (the two Huangs are not related). On Nov 10, Nvidia's independent directors hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to investigate after the New York Times and the Wall Street Journal reported on the relationship and the spying suspicions. Teter did not respond to Reuters' request for comment; Wagonfeld did not immediately respond.
Why it matters
The person Huang is said to have misled is not a bystander: Teter is Nvidia's general counsel, the officer responsible for exactly the disclosures and internal controls this scandal turns on, and House committees expect him to sit for a transcribed interview on Dec 2, with Huang himself due before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec 3. If the CEO was still describing the relationship as client business in October - weeks after telling Bloomberg (Oct 29) that the US government had not informed him of suspicions about Alice Huang - the question for the board's Wachtell investigation and for Congress shifts from what Huang did to what Nvidia's leadership was told, and when.
Sources agree / disagree
This is a single-outlet exclusive: Reuters is so far the only newsroom reporting the October correspondence, relying on two unnamed sources and documents it reviewed but did not quote. No other outlet has independently confirmed the misleading claim yet; the surrounding facts (the affair, the apology, the Wachtell hire, the resignation) are established by earlier reporting from the New York Times, the Wall Street Journal and Reuters itself.
Still uncertain
Reuters has not independently verified whether Alice Huang worked for the Chinese government, and the October messages were reviewed under a no-verbatim-quotes condition, so their exact wording is not public. When Nvidia's board and other executives learned the truth, what the Wachtell investigation has found, and whether Teter's Dec 2 interview goes ahead as scheduled all remain open.
