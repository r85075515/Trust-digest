10:T841,What happened
On November 27, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) filed a final rule on Federal Register Public Inspection (Docket No. FDA-2026-N-12256, Document No. 2026-24214, scheduled for formal publication on November 30, 2026) classifying post-ablation tissue response prediction software into Class II (special controls) by adding 21 CFR 892.2052. Following a De Novo classification request, the agency established binding special controls covering clinical ablation-zone volumetric validation, scanner modality qualification across ultrasound, CT, and MRI platforms, and detailed labeling of target pathologies and validation demographics.

Why it matters
Under federal medical device law, novel software types automatically default to Class III, requiring costly and lengthy Premarket Approval (PMA) trials. By codifying 21 CFR 892.2052 in Class II, the FDA establishes a clear predicate pathway allowing future interventional oncology and radiology software developers to seek 510(k) clearance if they satisfy the codified special controls.

Source differences
The Federal Register Public Inspection filing ("Federal Register :: Public Inspection: Medical Devices: Radiology Devices; Classification of the Post-Ablation Tissue Response Prediction Software") provides the primary regulatory text, docket identifier FDA-2026-N-12256, document number 2026-24214, and November 27 filing timestamp. CASRAI ("FDA Classifies Post-Ablation Software as Class II - CASRAI") summarizes the codified special controls under 21 CFR 892.2052(b)(3) and explains the clinical implications for interventional radiology departments.

Still uncertain
How rapidly competing interventional oncology software vendors will submit 510(k) applications under the newly codified 21 CFR 892.2052 predicate, and whether insurers will expand reimbursement codes for predictive ablation modeling, remains to be seen.
