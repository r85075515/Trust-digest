10:T9d7,Democrat Mary Peltola defeated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska, the Associated Press and Reuters reported Tuesday — the last U.S. Senate race to be resolved in this year's midterms. Peltola won 50% of first-place votes to Sullivan's 46%, clearing the majority threshold and avoiding further rounds under Alaska's ranked-choice system. Her victory gives Democrats a ninth flipped seat — the most by either party since 1958 — turning a 53-47 Republican majority into a 56-44 Democratic one.

What happened: Peltola, a former U.S. congresswoman completing a political comeback in a state President Donald Trump has repeatedly won by wide margins, said on social media there "is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs and put Alaska first." Sullivan's campaign said it would let the process run its course until every legal vote is counted and certified; he has not conceded.

Why it matters: The result locks in the Senate map for the next two years: a commanding Democratic majority for legislation and for confirming or blocking nominees, though still short of the 60 votes needed to overcome a filibuster. The race also ends under a legal cloud. Days before the election, the New York Times reported that Peltola's campaign had helped recruit a retired teacher also named Dan J. Sullivan — who took about 2.5% — as a spoiler; Peltola denied involvement, and the former campaign manager behind the account was fired before the primary and later sought to recant. Trump called for Peltola's prosecution; the Justice Department sued to block certification if the incumbent lost, a federal judge dismissed the case, and the department appealed while confirming a criminal investigation. On the final numbers, Peltola's margin is more than double the other Sullivan's vote share, so the dispute is unlikely to change the outcome — but the investigations continue.

Sources agree / disagree: AP and Reuters agree on the result, the vote shares and the 56-44 final map. The spoiler-recruitment allegation remains contested: the Times' reporting versus the campaign's denial, with the key source having partly recanted.

Still uncertain: Whether the DOJ appeal or the criminal and state investigations produce any action, when Alaska certifies the result, and whether Sullivan ultimately concedes.
