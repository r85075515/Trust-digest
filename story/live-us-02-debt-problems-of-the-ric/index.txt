What happened
Developing countries' debt load jumped to $9.4 trillion, driven by high interest rates and other global shocks hitting their economies. The NYT frames the story as the debt problems of the richest countries trickling down to the poorest.
Why it matters
When the poorest borrowers carry record debt at high rates, servicing it crowds out health, education, and climate spending. A rich-world rate cycle becomes a developing-world fiscal crisis — and the countries least able to absorb the shock pay the most.
