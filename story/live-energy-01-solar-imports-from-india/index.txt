10:T873,The U.S. International Trade Commission (USITC) has issued a final affirmative injury determination in its Solar IV investigation targeting crystalline silicon photovoltaic cells, whether or not assembled into modules, imported from India, Indonesia and Laos. The decision clears the final legal hurdle for federal trade protection, finding that dumped and subsidized imports from the three Asian nations harmed the domestic industry.
What happened
PV Magazine reports the USITC's final affirmative determination: imports of crystalline silicon photovoltaic cells from India, Indonesia and Laos — whether or not assembled into modules — materially injured US solar manufacturers. Utility Dive's headline puts it plainly: the ITC found the domestic solar panel manufacturing industry was harmed by unfairly priced imports from the three countries. Solar Power World adds the consequence: the determination means antidumping and countervailing duties (AD/CVD) will be placed on silicon solar cells and modules from the three countries, with tariffs beginning in November — and clears the way for the Department of Commerce to finalize the duty orders.
Why it matters
This is the enforcement end of a years-long trade fight: an affirmative USITC injury finding is the last gate before duties actually bite. For US manufacturers it promises breathing room against low-priced imports; for developers and installers it likely means higher module costs and a reshuffling of supply chains toward non-targeted countries. The November start date turns a legal conclusion into a procurement deadline.
Sources agree / disagree
PV Magazine Global, Utility Dive and Solar Power World agree on the core outcome — affirmative injury finding, three countries named, AD/CVD duties to follow. The feed notes figures differ across outlets in headlines and snippets, so treat any specific numbers as outlet-specific until verified.
Still uncertain
The snippets do not give the duty rates, the exact November effective date, or how existing contracts and inventory are treated.
