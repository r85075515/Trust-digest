What happened
Taiwan stocks opened sharply lower, breaking below the 40,000 mark and falling through the annual moving average of about 38,827 points. The index touched a low of 37,837.05 points, down 3,737.67 points at its worst. Electronics heavyweights and high-priced stocks fell across the board, many hitting their daily limits, according to CNA reporter Chung Jung-feng (鍾榮峰) in Taipei. The trigger, the report says, was uncertainty over AI data-centre policy after the US midterm results, which drove all four major US indices sharply lower and dragged Japanese and South Korean markets down at the open.
Why it matters
A 3,737-point intraday fall is a record for Taiwan's market, and the breadth of the sell-off — TSMC limit down, electronics and thousand-dollar stocks alike — shows the rout was market-wide rather than a single-stock event. TSMC is the market's bellwether, so its limit-down open set the tone for the session. The chain of causes also matters: a US policy question about AI data centres travelled through US, Japanese and Korean markets before hitting Taipei, a reminder of how tightly Taiwan's market is tied to the global AI trade.
