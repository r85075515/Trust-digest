10:Tb55,Fourteen people were killed and 30 injured shortly before midnight on Wednesday (Nov 11) when a truck lost control at the entrance to Fujairah in the United Arab Emirates, overturned and crashed into vehicles that had slowed in traffic, triggering a pile-up of 23 vehicles and a fire.
What happened
The crash occurred on Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Road, the main road into the emirate from Dubai and Sharjah. Fujairah Police said the truck struck another vehicle and overturned before colliding with slowed or stopped traffic; 18 cars and five trucks were involved in total. The Interior Ministry, posting on X on Thursday (Nov 12), put the toll at 14 dead and 30 injured and described the figures as preliminary. Police said 15 of the injured have been discharged from hospital and 15 remain under medical care. The dead include Emirati nationals; the injured include citizens of seven countries - the UAE, Oman, India, Brazil, Pakistan, Jordan and Egypt. Three of the dead were members of one family from Vengara in Kerala's Malappuram district: 21-year-old twins and their three-year-old nephew (Indian outlets differ slightly on the spelling of their names). The Consulate General of India in Dubai confirmed Indian nationals were among the dead and said it is assisting the families. On Friday (Nov 13), five victims had been formally identified and Fujairah's Crown Prince attended funeral prayers for victims; Azerbaijan and Saudi Arabia sent condolences.
Why it matters
Fujairah Police described the crash as among the deadliest road accidents in the Emirates' history, in a country whose highways usually record far lower casualties in a single incident. The victims span at least seven nationalities, turning a local road disaster into a multi-country consular operation, and the identification process - including DNA testing for victims badly burned in the fire - is still under way days later.
Sources agree / disagree
Reuters, the Interior Ministry's own statement and multiple regional and Indian outlets agree on the core facts: 14 dead, 30 injured, 23 vehicles (18 cars and five trucks), a truck losing control shortly before midnight on Nov 11. Indian reports differ slightly in transliterating the Kerala victims' names, so this card describes the family without fixing one spelling.
Still uncertain
Police have not said why the driver lost control - no finding yet on speed, a mechanical fault or road conditions - and the precise sequence of collisions remains under investigation. The full list of the dead has not been released; some identities await DNA results, and with 15 people still in hospital the ministry warns the preliminary toll could change.
