The column frames the backlash not as a threat to progress but as a possible brake on an overheated build-out.
What happened
The column argues that public anger at AI, and at the data centers being built to power it, is now strong enough to slow the boom. Some economists cited in the piece see a silver lining: cooling demand for new data centers could ease pressure on power grids, land, and local communities.
Why it matters
The piece reframes the backlash as an economic question, not just a political mood. If voters push back hard enough to change policy, the AI build-out — one of the biggest capital-spending waves in years — could slow, with knock-on effects for chipmakers, utilities, and construction. The takeaway is that the data center boom now has a political risk attached, and economists are split on whether slowing it would hurt or help.
