10:T977,The United States Federal Communications Commission has given SpaceX the green light to deploy one million satellites intended to create data centers in space, a decision that has raised skepticism among scientists.

What happened
National Public Radio (NPR) reported that the Trump administration gave Elon Musk's SpaceX approval to deploy one million satellites that the company said would create data centers in space. The Register reported that the regulator on Wednesday published an order allowing SpaceX to operate a non-geostationary satellite orbit constellation of 1,000,000 satellites, deployed into orbital shells each spanning 50 km at altitudes from 500 to 1,002 km and in 30-degree inclinations. The constellation is called Starmind, and it aims to be the most cost and energy-efficient way to meet the growing demand for AI computing power. The FCC dismissed some key concerns raised by opponents, including potential threats to scientific research, because the agency determined they fell outside its regulatory authority.

Why it matters
Approval at this scale would move the idea of orbital data centers from concept toward deployment, tying the growth of AI computing directly to a vastly larger satellite population. The same scale explains the scientific skepticism highlighted in the coverage, and the FCC's reasoning matters because concerns about research were set aside on jurisdictional grounds rather than resolved on their merits.

Sources agree / disagree
BBC World, NPR News, The Register and TechCrunch all report the same core decision: the FCC approved SpaceX's plan, with TechCrunch framing it as putting AI data centers in space and the BBC framing it as a million data satellites. The outlets differ mainly in emphasis — NPR and the BBC stress the approval, The Register stresses the orbital details and the FCC's dismissal of opponents' concerns. No conflicting satellite counts appear; every version uses one million.

Still uncertain
The feed excerpts do not give a launch timetable, the number of satellites SpaceX will deploy first, or the cost of the constellation. They also do not detail the specific scientific objections beyond the threat to research, or whether legal challenges or further regulatory conditions may follow the order.
