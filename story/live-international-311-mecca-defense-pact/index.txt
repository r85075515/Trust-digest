10:T780,What happened
Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan ratified the Mecca Mutual Defense Agreement on Friday, November 27, 2026, finalizing Turkey's approval following parliamentary passage on November 5. On the same day, delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey held talks in Islamabad under the trilateral pact—originally signed on August 7, 2026—and vowed to support Saudi Arabia against ongoing attacks by Houthi rebels.

Why it matters
The pact treats an armed attack on any one member as an attack on all three and binds the signatories for 10 years, creating a formal security framework linking NATO's second-largest military, a major Gulf oil exporter, and a nuclear-armed state. Under the agreement, a secretariat has been established in Saudi Arabia led by retired Pakistani general Nauman Mahmood as its first secretary-general.

Source differences
AP News ("Pakistan and Turkey pledge to support Saudi Arabia against Houthi") emphasized the Islamabad talks and noted that Turkish officials insist no Turkish troops will be deployed to fight inside Yemen. Al Arabiya ("Erdogan completes Turkey’s ratification of Mecca Defense Alliance pact") focused on Erdogan's formal sign-off and the strategic profiles of the three nations, while Al Jazeera ("Can the Mecca pact turn its collective defence promise into real support?") analyzed operational limits, noting Pakistan has ruled out offensive strikes inside Yemen and command structures remain unspecified.

Still uncertain
The alliance has not publicly detailed the exact command-and-control mechanisms, force numbers, or deployment timetables that would govern a joint military response, nor how Ankara and Islamabad would respond in the event of a direct attack involving Iran.
