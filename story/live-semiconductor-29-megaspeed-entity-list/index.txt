10:Tdc1,The United States on Monday added Megaspeed International to the Commerce Department's Entity List, formally cutting the Singapore-based chip firm at the center of the Nvidia scandal off from US technology without a government license. The Bureau of Industry and Security rule, filed Monday and scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on Nov. 25, adds seven companies in all — Megaspeed entities in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand plus affiliates Apex Enterprise Solutions, Swiftdata, MSFusion and Speedmatrix — and subjects them to license requirements for all items under US export rules, reviewed under a presumption of denial.

What happened: Megaspeed, historically one of Nvidia's largest chip customers and still listed as an official Nvidia cloud partner, has been under US investigation since 2024 over alleged shipments of advanced Nvidia AI chips to China in violation of export controls, the Justice and Commerce departments said this week. Its controlling shareholder, Alice Huang, is the Chinese businesswoman whose relationship with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang triggered the scandal that cost him his job on Nov. 12 — and whose return the Nvidia board approved the same day as the listing, after US officials assured directors it posed no national security risk. The timing is stark: Washington blacklisted the alleged partner's company on the very day it cleared the CEO's comeback. The listing is the Commerce Department's first addition to the Entity List since October 2025. Megaspeed has said Alice Huang left the company in August 2025 and that it conducted "lawful business." The BIS rule itself is a risk finding: it does not state that any listed company diverted products or committed a crime, and it names neither Nvidia nor Jensen Huang. The New York Times reported last month that US authorities had concluded Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset; Reuters could not independently verify that allegation, and China has called the spy accusations "groundless."

Why it matters: The Entity List is Washington's bluntest export-control weapon, and using it on the scandal's core company — while simultaneously signaling comfort with Huang's reinstatement — splits the administration's response in two: the individual is rehabilitated, the network is cut off. For Nvidia, the practical exposure is now a named, licensed-out customer channel into China-linked buyers; for other firms on the list's edge, it shows the blacklist is back in active use after a year of restraint toward Beijing.

Sources agree/disagree: Reuters, Bloomberg Law and the BIS filing agree the listing happened Monday, covers seven entities, and imposes presumption-of-denial licensing. No source disputes the action itself; they differ only in framing — whether it is an espionage response (the intelligence allegation remains unverified) or an export-control risk measure (the rule's own stated basis).

Still uncertain: Whether criminal charges follow — US and Singapore investigations into Megaspeed and Alice Huang are ongoing, and no court has found wrongdoing. What happens to Megaspeed's existing Nvidia GPU inventory and contracts, whether Nvidia removes it from its partner directory, and whether Alice Huang herself is designated individually are all open.
