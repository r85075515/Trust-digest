10:Tabf,Reuters reported for the first time on Friday that the US Securities and Exchange Commission's investigation of Nvidia is focused on the company's accounting practices — two people briefed on the matter said enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues. The Wall Street Journal had reported on Tuesday that the SEC was probing Nvidia but could not ascertain the focus. The same day, Nvidia's stock closed down 14%, its worst day since January 2025, capping a seventh consecutive weekly loss — the longest such streak since 2008 — and leaving the shares more than 47% below their October 6 record close.

What happened: The accounting focus sharply raises the stakes of the SEC probe first disclosed this week. Reuters could not determine when the inquiry opened or exactly which practices are under examination; the Financial Times has noted that Nvidia last year changed how it reports geographic revenue — by customer headquarters rather than billing address — just as Singapore sales soared to almost half of global revenue. Interim CEO Colette Kress told Reuters the company would be "far more selective" about AI-industry financing deals. The market verdict was brutal: Friday's 14% drop to $125.53 erased roughly $500 billion in a day; the stock is down about 23% this week, has shed some $1.16 trillion in seven sessions, and has lost roughly $2.7 trillion since its October peak, dragging the Nasdaq down 5.3% on Friday.

Why it matters: An accounting probe is categorically different from the disclosure questions that had been reported. It reaches into the numbers themselves at the company whose revenue figures anchor the entire AI trade — eight days after its founder-CEO resigned in scandal and three days after its board publicly ruled out his return. If the SEC's scrutiny extends to the geographic-revenue change or AI financing deals, the fallout spreads to every customer and partner in Nvidia's orbit.

Sources agree / disagree: Reuters, the WSJ and MarketWatch agree on the Friday rout (-14%, seventh straight weekly loss, worst stretch since 2008) and the October 6 peak as the reference point. Estimates of total value lost since the peak vary by methodology: the WSJ puts it at roughly $2.7 trillion; CNBC at about $2.5 trillion.

Still uncertain: When the SEC opened the inquiry and which specific accounting practices are under scrutiny; whether the geographic-revenue reporting change is part of it; whether Nvidia has received a formal Wells notice; and who the board's permanent CEO will be.
