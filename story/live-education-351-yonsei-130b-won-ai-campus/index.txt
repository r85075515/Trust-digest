10:T744,What happened
South Korea's Yonsei University unveiled an eight-year strategic initiative on November 27, 2026, committing more than 130 billion won (approximately 97.4 million USD) to build a human-centered artificial intelligence campus. According to The Korea Times, The Korea Herald, and The Chosun Daily, the plan spans university-wide education, research, and administrative operations and will require incoming students to take mandatory AI courses beginning in 2028.

Why it matters
Major Asian research universities are moving beyond isolated AI electives toward institution-wide curriculum and infrastructure overhauls. By embedding ethical AI use and interdisciplinary coursework into baseline graduation requirements, Yonsei aims to standardize AI literacy across both STEM and humanities disciplines while upgrading campus computing infrastructure.

Source differences
The Korea Times ("Yonsei University unveils $97.4 mil. plan for human-centered AI campus") highlights the eight-year investment timeline exceeding 130 billion won (97.4 million USD) toward a human-centered AI campus. The Korea Herald ("Yonsei to invest W130b in AI-driven university overhaul") notes that the plan was unveiled at the university's main campus in Sinchon, Seoul, while The Chosun Daily ("Yonsei University Mandates AI Courses for New Students from 2028") emphasizes the 2028 mandatory AI course requirement for new students and its focus on ethical use.

Still uncertain
Specific annual budget allocations between computing hardware, faculty hiring, and student scholarships, as well as detailed syllabus standards for non-technical majors, have not yet been published.
