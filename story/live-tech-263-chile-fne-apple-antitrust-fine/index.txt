10:T80f,「What happened」
Chile's competition authority, the Fiscalía Nacional Económica (FNE), announced in a November 26 filing that it has brought an antitrust case against Apple Inc. before Chile's Tribunal for the Defense of Free Competition (TDLC). The FNE alleges that Apple has abused its dominant position in the iOS operating system market since at least 2019 by restricting app distribution and in-app payment channels, and asks the tribunal to impose a fine of roughly 80 million USD—calculated at 30 percent of associated sales—alongside structural remedies.

「Why it matters」
The enforcement action extends global regulatory pressure on Apple's App Store economics into Latin America, mirroring mandates seen in the European Union and other jurisdictions. Specifically, the FNE is asking the court to compel Apple to permit iPhone app sideloading, third-party app marketplaces, alternative in-app payment processors, and developer steering toward external payment options.

「Source differences」
The FNE's official release is titled "FNE acusa a Apple por abuso de posición dominante y pide al TDLC multa de US$ 80 millones", stating the charge of dominant-position abuse and the 80 million USD penalty request before the TDLC. 9to5Mac covered the case under "Apple faces potential $80 million fine and App Store overhaul in Chile", breaking down the requested behavioral remedies including sideloading and external payment steering, while MLQ.ai reported under "Chile's competition prosecutor seeks $80 million fine from Apple over iOS" noting Reuters' Friday follow-up.

「Still uncertain」
Whether Chile's competition tribunal will uphold the FNE's allegations, adopt the full 80 million USD fine calculation, or enforce all proposed App Store remedies remains pending judicial proceedings, and Apple's formal legal response has not yet been ruled upon.
