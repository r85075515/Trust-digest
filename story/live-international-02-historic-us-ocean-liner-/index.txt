10:T5f4,The SS United States, once the fastest ocean liner in the world, was sunk Wednesday off Destin, Florida, to begin a new life on the floor of the Gulf of Mexico as the world's largest artificial reef.
What happened
The nearly 1,000-foot American-made vessel, which set a transatlantic speed record on its maiden voyage in 1952, was scuttled about 25 miles southwest of Destin, between Panama City and Pensacola. Officials on Florida's panhandle coast carried out the $13.5 million project, designed to attract divers and give the wreck a second life underwater as a diving destination and a home for marine life.
Why it matters
The end of a legendary liner is the start of a marine habitat. An artificial reef shelters sea life while drawing dive tourism, letting a retired giant keep serving a purpose in a different form.
Sources agree / disagree
The Guardian, Mongabay and NPR agree on where the ship was sunk, the cost of the project and the claim that it will be the world's largest artificial reef. The figures are not in conflict: 1,000 feet is the ship's length, while 25 miles is the distance from Destin to the sinking site, so the two numbers describe different things. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — three outlets align on the core facts of the sinking project.
