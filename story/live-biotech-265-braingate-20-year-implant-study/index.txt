10:T84b,「What happened」
Researchers from the BrainGate consortium published a 20-year performance evaluation in Nature Medicine on November 26, 2026, analyzing 2,289 neural recording sessions from 20 intracortical microelectrode arrays implanted in 14 clinical trial participants enrolled between 2004 and 2019. Individual recordings extended up to 7.3 years, with electrodes recording neural spike waveforms across an average of 35.6 percent of channels and 11 of 14 evaluated arrays successfully decoding movement intentions over multi-year spans.

「Why it matters」
Long-term durability has been a primary hurdle for clinical brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), as scar tissue and signal degradation can limit device lifespan. Demonstrating multi-year neural decoding stability across the largest longitudinal human dataset to date provides critical benchmarks for next-generation implantable neuroprosthetics aiding people with paralysis.

「Source differences」
MLQ.ai reported the findings under "BrainGate study: 11 of 14 brain implant arrays decoded movement for years", highlighting the 2,289 recording sessions, 20 arrays, 14 participants enrolled from 2004 to 2019, and 11-of-14 decoding success rate. Taylor Tailored covered the study under "How Long Do Brain Implants Last? BrainGate's 7.3-Year Data", focusing on the 7.3-year maximum recording span, while Chinese life-science portal Ebiotrade published "植入式脑机接口患者皮层内微电极阵列20年长期性能综合评估", detailing the 35.6 percent average electrode spike recording rate alongside the underlying Nature Medicine paper "Performance of intracortical microelectrode arrays in people with implanted brain-computer interfaces over a 20-year period".

「Still uncertain」
How newer wireless, higher-channel-count electrode designs compare against these legacy wired arrays in multi-decade real-world home use remains to be established in ongoing clinical trials.
