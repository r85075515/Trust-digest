10:T71f,More screen time made for sharper teenage minds — at least in one Finnish study. Researchers who followed children for eight years found that those who spent more time using screens tended to have better cognitive processing abilities as teenagers, a result that cuts against the usual warnings about screens.
What happened
A Finnish study tracked children over eight years and found that more screen time in childhood was associated with better cognitive processing abilities in the teenage years, according to ScienceDaily. The researchers suggest that what children do on screens may matter more than how much time they spend using them, challenging the assumption that screen time is always harmful.
Why it matters
The finding matters because screen-time guidance for parents usually focuses on limiting hours. If the type of screen activity matters more than the total time, advice would need to shift from counting minutes to looking at content — though a single study cannot settle that question.
