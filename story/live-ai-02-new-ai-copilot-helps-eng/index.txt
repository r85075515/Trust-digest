What happened
Algorithms that design structures have already produced new parts optimised for ultralight aircraft, high-bandwidth antennas and F1 racing cars. The problem is the process around them: the best algorithmic design techniques still require lots of computation and time, and users only see the final design after it is complete. Making changes often means starting the design process all over again. The new AI copilot is presented as a way to help engineers design better structures within that slow, restart-heavy workflow, though the short report does not explain how the copilot works or what it changes in practice.

Why it matters
Design is where engineering time and computing budgets are spent, so a tool that shortens the loop between an idea and a finished part has value across aircraft, antennas and motorsport alike. The copilot framing matters too: the tool is presented as working alongside engineers rather than replacing them, keeping human judgement in charge of the final structure. If the approach works as described, fewer restarts would mean faster development and less wasted computation.
