10:T70f,The European Union plans a sovereign technology fund worth up to €50 billion, the Financial Times reports, to bankroll long-term, high-risk bets on European artificial intelligence and quantum computing companies.

What happened
The Financial Times disclosed the plan, which both TechNews and CNA relayed. The fund would invest in European AI and quantum computing firms, aiming to reduce Europe's dependence on American and Chinese technology. TechNews frames the goal as narrowing the gap with the US and China; CNA's headline stresses cutting reliance on the two powers. Neither report gives a launch date, a funding timetable, or the fund's governance structure.

Why it matters
A €50 billion state-backed fund would be one of Europe's largest direct bets on strategic technology. If it materialises, European AI and quantum startups would gain a patient capital source that private markets rarely provide for high-risk, long-horizon work, and Brussels would gain leverage in its push for technological sovereignty.

Sources agree / disagree
TechNews and CNA agree on the core facts: a fund of up to €50 billion, aimed at AI and quantum computing, sourced to a Financial Times disclosure. Both trace to the same original report, so their agreement is not independent corroboration. No figure or factual conflict appears between the two.

Still uncertain
The fund remains a reported plan, not a confirmed EU decision. The material gives no start date, no breakdown of funding sources, no list of eligible companies, and no statement from the European Commission. The Financial Times report itself is not quoted beyond its headline claim.
