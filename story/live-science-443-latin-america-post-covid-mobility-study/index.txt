10:T76a,What happened
Researchers at the University of Liverpool's Geographic Data Science Lab, led by first author Carmen Cabrera (Carmen Cabrera), published a study in Nature Communications titled "Sustained changes to urban mobility after COVID-19 reinforced socioeconomic inequalities in Argentina, Chile and Colombia". Analyzing more than 170 million anonymized, aggregated mobility records provided by Meta Data for Good between April 2020 and May 2022, the team found that everyday urban mobility in densely populated cities across Argentina, Chile, and Colombia remained below pre-pandemic baselines two years into the pandemic. In Colombia, mobility levels stayed 25% to 50% below baseline, and across the studied cities, the wealthiest third of neighborhoods recovered the slowest.

Why it matters
The findings demonstrate how remote work capacity and socioeconomic status created persistent divergence in urban travel behavior, reshaping public transit demand, local retail foot traffic, and urban inequality across major Latin American metropolitan areas.

Source differences
Both Phys.org and University of Liverpool News published on November 26, 2026 under the identical headline "Study finds lasting changes in daily mobility patterns across Latin America after COVID-19". Both articles derive from the same University of Liverpool institutional release summarizing Cabrera's Nature Communications paper and its bias-corrected Meta dataset.

Still uncertain
Because Facebook users do not fully represent the entire population of each city, the authors applied statistical bias corrections, though mobility trends beyond the May 2022 dataset window or among non-app populations remain outside direct observation.
