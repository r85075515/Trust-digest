10:T69c,Flexcompute has integrated Siemens' Calibre verification software into PhotonForge 2.0, its photonic chip design platform, enabling engineers to run design-rule checking (DRC) and layout-versus-schematic (LVS) verification using foundry-specific rule decks during development.
What happened
Photonic engineers make design decisions at many levels: a laser has its own performance requirements, so does an optical fiber, and the interface between them introduces another set of variables. The components ultimately have to perform together as a complete optical link, and meeting individual targets does not guarantee the system works as expected. The integration supports Siemens Calibre nmDRC and nmLVS, allowing designers to identify layout errors before submitting a design.
Why it matters
The judgment is that photonic design has become a system-level challenge: as optical links get more complex, checking each part in isolation is no longer enough. Baking foundry-grade verification into the design platform shortens the loop between design and sign-off, which is exactly where photonic startups lose time to incumbents.
Sources agree / disagree
EE Times Asia reports the integration itself; Semiconductor Engineering frames the same shift as photonic design becoming a system-level challenge. The two angles agree, and the version numbers in the snippets (PhotonForge 2.0) are not a conflict. Trust label: Two-source agreement — two outlets agree on the core claims.
