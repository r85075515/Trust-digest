The proposed monument would rise 250 feet on a patch of grass off the bridge that spans the Potomac River, connecting the city with Arlington National Cemetery, and it is now the subject of three lawsuits seeking to halt potential work.

What happened
The governments of Washington, D.C. and Virginia sued to block construction of the planned arch. In their filing, as described in the feed summaries, they claim the monument is unlawfully tall and would snarl traffic for years. The case is a last-minute effort to stop the multi-year project before it starts, potentially as soon as this coming week. U.S. District Court Judge Tanya Chutkin ordered both sides to talk and to agree on a proposed briefing schedule for the motion for a temporary restraining order by Saturday evening.

Why it matters
The dispute puts a major presidential monument proposal before a federal court before building work gets under way. The plaintiffs' arguments, as summarised in the feeds, rest on two practical points: the height of the 250-foot structure and the traffic disruption they say the multi-year construction would cause. The judge's order setting a timetable for talks and briefing means the request for a temporary restraining order is moving quickly, even though the feeds do not report any decision on the underlying request itself.
