In other words, the goal is to make sure rogue agents do not break the law, hack into unauthorized servers, or release poison gas into major cities, to use the extreme examples given in the report. Reverse game theory, as described, works by designing the surrounding system so that following the rules is in the agent's interest. Why it matters
Controlling increasingly capable AI agents is a central problem in AI safety, and incentive design offers a different lever from simply training or instructing a model to behave. If the environment around an agent rewards rule-following, misbehavior might be reduced even when direct oversight is difficult. The stakes listed in the report, from unauthorized hacking to mass harm scenarios, explain why researchers are exploring structural approaches. Still, the summary provides no evidence about effectiveness, so the significance for now lies in the direction of research rather than in demonstrated results.
