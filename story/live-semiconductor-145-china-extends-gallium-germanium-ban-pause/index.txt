10:T809,China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), led by Minister Wang Wentao (王文濤), announced ahead of the November 27, 2026 deadline that it will extend the suspension of export restrictions on several critical dual-use minerals bound for the United States by two months, running from November 28, 2026 through January 27, 2027. The extension covers export bans originally imposed in December 2024 on gallium, germanium, antimony, and superhard materials, as well as stricter end-user and end-use reviews for dual-use graphite items.

Beijing had first suspended these targeted U.S. restrictions in November 2025 as part of bilateral trade truce arrangements with Washington. While the latest notice prevents an immediate supply cutoff at the end of November, MOFCOM did not revoke the underlying export-control framework, retaining leverage to reimpose the bans if U.S. technology restrictions escalate.

Gallium and germanium are indispensable inputs for compound semiconductors, radio-frequency chips, infrared optics, and fiber-optic communications, while antimony is vital for munitions and graphite for electric vehicle batteries. Extending the pause averts near-term input shortages for U.S. chipmakers and defense contractors while trade negotiations continue into early 2027.

VOA News, Taipei Times, and EnergyNews.pro all confirm the two-month extension window running from November 28, 2026 to January 27, 2027 and the specific minerals covered. VOA and EnergyNews.pro highlighted that the underlying December 2024 prohibition remains on the books rather than being permanently lifted, while Taipei Times contextualized the move alongside concurrent U.S. chip export curbs.

Whether Washington and Beijing can reach a durable agreement on semiconductor controls before the new January 27, 2027 deadline—or whether Beijing will let the suspension lapse—remains uncertain.
