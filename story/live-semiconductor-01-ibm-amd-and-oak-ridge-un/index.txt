10:T69a,Oak Ridge National Laboratory now has a system for research into quantum error correction that combines IBM quantum processors with a high-performance computing system based on AMD silicon. The Quantum Science Center at Oak Ridge, funded by the US Department of Energy, unveiled the co-design platform, marrying quantum and high-performance computing technology.
What happened
The platform pairs IBM quantum hardware with AMD-based computing to test ways of correcting qubit errors, the faults that hold back useful quantum machines. The alliance aims to accelerate the creation of scientifically relevant quantum computers by 2028 as part of the Department of Energy's Quantum Genesis Mission. EE Times, The Next Platform and The Register all report the unveiling with the same core facts.
Why it matters
Error correction is the bottleneck for quantum computing: without it, quantum machines stay experimental. A hybrid quantum-plus-HPC testbed at a national laboratory gives researchers a practical place to find out which correction methods work at scale, and the 2028 target puts a date on the ambition.
