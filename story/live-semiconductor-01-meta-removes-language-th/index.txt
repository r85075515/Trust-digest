10:T7bc,Meta is rewriting its commercial terms for the second time in a matter of weeks, this time to strip out language that appeared to give it the right to scrape content from websites — including news sites — and use it to train its AI models.
What happened
TechCrunch, The Verge and The Register all describe the same sequence. The disputed wording sat on Meta's commercial terms page and suggested that ‘Meta may use any content from such domains, web pages, or URLs to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of’ its business products. After the backlash, Meta is removing that language. A company spokesperson insisted the update does not reflect any change in how Meta crawls the web. The Register had earlier framed the original wording as Meta granting itself the right to scrape content from its business customers for model training, and reported that Meta plans to introduce new terms at the end of the month authorizing it to collect content from websites associated with its business customers to train AI models or otherwise improve its products.
Why it matters
The episode shows how sensitive the legal fine print around AI training data has become: a single terms-of-service clause — real or perceived — can trigger a backlash the moment it touches publishers' content. For businesses that depend on Facebook commercially, the practical lesson is caution: think twice about what information ends up online when the platform's rules keep shifting.
Sources agree / disagree
All three outlets describe the same events in the same order, with no factual conflicts. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — three tech outlets agree on the terms change and the company's response.
Still uncertain
The snippets cover the disputed wording, not the full text of the new terms Meta plans to introduce at month's end.
