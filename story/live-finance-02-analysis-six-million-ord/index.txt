What happened
Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO on November 23 ended a boardroom upheaval that had rattled AI markets. CNBC framed the comeback with six key questions, including why the board reversed course and what role President Donald Trump played. DigiTimes' analysis put the focus on China: even with Huang back, Chinese cloud providers cannot secure enough computing capacity under increasingly restrictive U.S. export controls, leaving about six million orders unfilled.

Why it matters
Nvidia's chips power much of the world's AI infrastructure, so a leadership crisis at the company rattled markets globally. The China angle matters more: the supply gap shows that restoring a chief executive does not restore chip access, and Chinese cloud firms may face higher costs, longer waits, or a forced turn to domestic alternatives.

Sources agree-disagree
CNBC and DigiTimes agree that Huang returned on November 23 and that confidence in AI computing recovered. They differ in emphasis: CNBC focuses on the board's reversal and Trump's involvement, while DigiTimes stresses the unresolved China supply gap. Trust label: Two-source agreement — both outlets confirm the return; the six-million-order figure comes from DigiTimes' analysis alone.

Still uncertain
