10:T69e,OpenAI CEO Sam Altman reportedly pitched three endgames for AI safety in an internal presentation — including one he himself called "terrible" — on the same day a US judge dismissed his sister's sexual abuse lawsuit against him.
What happened
According to The Decoder, Altman laid out three paths to safe AI: an international watchdog modeled after the IAEA, a merger of the major labs into one entity, or a single company so powerful it could shut down everyone else's compute. He called the last option "terrible." Separately, US District Judge Zachary Bluestone dismissed the lawsuit Ann Altman filed last year alleging that her brother sexually abused her throughout her childhood. Sam Altman has denied the allegations. The Guardian reported earlier that over the past year, Ann had failed to appear for an independent medical examination or meet other deadlines despite warnings.
Why it matters
The three endgames read like a map of the industry's anxieties: who governs AI, whether labs should consolidate, and what happens if compute itself becomes the weapon. The lawsuit dismissal closes a personal legal chapter for the most visible CEO in AI, though on procedural grounds rather than a ruling on the underlying claims.
Sources agree / disagree
The Guardian, The Verge, and BBC Technology all report the dismissal and the missed medical examination; The Decoder reports the three endgames from the internal presentation. No conflicts appear across the snippets.
Still uncertain
The snippets give no date or venue for the internal presentation and no detail on how seriously Altman meant each endgame.
