10:T8b1,A magnitude 6.7 earthquake struck Peru's central Junín region in the Andes at 7:09 p.m. local time on Friday, November 20, at a depth of roughly 108–129 kilometres. The shaking was felt strongly in the capital Lima, about 200 kilometres away, where residents rushed out of buildings into the streets. Because of its depth, the quake's energy was spread widely but blunted at the surface: Peru's Geophysical Institute (IGP) reported a maximum intensity of V near the epicentre, and as of the first official checks there were no reports of deaths, injuries or serious damage. Peru's Navy ruled out any tsunami on the coast.

What happened: The USGS placed the epicentre about 9 km northeast of Carhuamayo at 108 km depth; Peru's IGP put it 30 km northwest of Junín at 129 km — a minor location and depth difference between agencies, with both agreeing on magnitude 6.7 (Germany's GFZ, cited by Reuters, measured 6.8). The mayor of Carhuamayo told local television there were no initial signs of victims or material damage. The USGS PAGER system issued a green alert, indicating a low likelihood of casualties and damage.

Why it matters: A 6.7 is a genuinely strong earthquake — an order of magnitude more energy than the magnitude-5 events that routinely rattle the region — and it was felt across a capital of some 10 million people in one of the world's most seismically exposed countries, on the Pacific Ring of Fire. The deep focus is the reason the first reports are benign; the story to watch is whether remote Andean communities near the epicentre, where communications are slower, report damage in the coming hours.

Sources agree / disagree: AFP, Xinhua, Reuters and Fox Weather agree on magnitude 6.7 (GFZ: 6.8), the Junín epicentre area, the Friday-evening timing and the absence of initial casualties. Depth estimates differ by agency: 108 km (USGS) versus 129 km (IGP).

Still uncertain: Whether damage or casualties emerge from communities near the epicentre as assessments reach them; aftershock activity; and whether the IGP revises the magnitude or depth.
