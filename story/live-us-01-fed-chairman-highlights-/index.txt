What happened
According to the New York Times, Warsh has set up a task force to study the link between new-technology productivity gains and the labor market. The headline frames the assignment around the economic uncertainty of the AI boom: AI is lifting productivity in some places, but what it does to employment, wages and growth is far from settled. The feed material gives no detail on who sits on the task force, what questions it has been asked, or when it will report.
Why it matters
If AI raises output per worker without creating matching demand for labor, the Fed's picture of inflation, employment and the neutral interest rate all shift — which is why a central bank would study it internally rather than leave it to Wall Street. The takeaway: the Fed is not assuming the AI boom is automatically good for workers; it wants its own analysis before the debate hardens.
