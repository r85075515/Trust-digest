What happened
The Times analysis, based on hacked government data, puts Russian military deaths in Ukraine above 700,000. The material provided gives no breakdown of the figure by period, unit or cause of death, and no description of how the data was obtained, verified or counted. The single sentence carried in the feed is the whole of the analysis available here: the data, in the Times' reading, underscores the immense human cost of Putin's war of attrition.
Why it matters
If the figure is broadly right, it reframes the war's cost in a single number: a death toll of this scale would mean the attritional campaign has consumed Russian lives at a rate with few modern parallels, and it would sharpen the question of how long either side can sustain the fighting. Casualty figures in this war are also contested ground, reported differently by each side, so a number built on hacked government records matters precisely because it does not come from either side's public statements.
Sources agree / disagree
Only The New York Times carries this analysis in the material provided. The other outlets in the same cluster — the BBC, NPR and The Guardian — report a different story, Ukraine's naval-drone strike on Russian vessels off Libya, which does not corroborate the casualty figure. That Libya story was already published as its own card on November 22 and is not repeated here. Trust label: Single source — the casualty figure rests on one Times analysis of hacked data, with no independent corroboration in the material.
Still uncertain
Almost everything beyond the headline figure is unknown: the time span the 700,000 covers, whether it counts only confirmed deaths, how the hacked data was authenticated, and whether the figure includes deaths from causes other than combat.
