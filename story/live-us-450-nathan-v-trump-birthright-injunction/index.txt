10:Tada,What happened
On November 25, 2026, U.S. District Judge Christopher R. Cooper (Christopher R. Cooper) of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia issued a 67-page opinion (Document 52) and order in Nathan v. Trump (Case No. 26-cv-3377 (CRC)) certifying a plaintiff class and granting a classwide preliminary injunction. The order bars the State Department, Department of Homeland Security, and Social Security Administration from enforcing Executive Order 14418 (signed by President Trump on August 6, 2026), which sought to deny citizenship to U.S.-born children of non-immune foreign government or international organization employees, children born via commercial surrogacy, and children whose parents are suspected gang or terrorist organization members. The certified class covers children born in the United States on or after August 6, 2026, without accredited diplomat parents or a U.S.-citizen parent whose parents fall under Sections 2(a)–(c) of EO 14418. Judge Cooper wrote that "the President cannot carve out new exceptions to birthright citizenship for children born within the United States and subject to its laws," marking the second judicial block of EO 14418 following a September Maryland federal court freeze and the Supreme Court's June ruling in Trump v. Barbara invalidating EO 14160.

Why it matters
By granting classwide preliminary relief, the ruling protects affected newborns nationwide from denial of passports and Social Security numbers while constitutional challenges proceed, reinforcing judicial limits on executive modifications to Fourteenth Amendment citizenship guarantees.

Source differences
CourtListener (Court Opinion) hosts the primary 67-page judicial filing titled "Memorandum & Opinion – #52 in NATHAN v. TRUMP (D.D.C., 1:26-cv-3377) – CourtListener.com". American Civil Liberties Union published "Federal Court Blocks New President Trump Birthright Citizenship Executive Order" highlighting class certification for affected families, while Washington Examiner published "Judge Christopher Cooper blocks Trump's second attempt at limiting birthright citizenship", situating the order after Trump v. Barbara and the September Maryland ruling. All three sources align on the November 25 ruling and EO 14418 freeze.

Still uncertain
The preliminary injunction is an interim measure rather than a final judgment on the merits; plaintiffs must post a nominal 1 USD bond within seven days, both parties must file a joint status report by December 4, and potential appellate review remains open.
