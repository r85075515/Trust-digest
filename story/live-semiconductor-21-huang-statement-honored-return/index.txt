10:Tad5,Jensen Huang broke his silence on Tuesday morning, five days after resigning as Nvidia's chief executive, issuing his first public statement since stepping down. "I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation," he said. He then opened the door to a return: "Since my departure, I've seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

What happened: The statement, carried in full by the Wall Street Journal and CNN, converts a behind-the-scenes comeback campaign into a public one: allies were reported on Sunday to have set up a war room, a petition backing Huang has gathered roughly 180,000 to 200,000 signatures, a senior employee's letter urging his return circulated among executives on Monday, and President Trump told reporters on Monday he would support reinstatement. The board's answer, so far, is no: a source familiar with the situation told CNN the board is not considering reinstating Huang, the Journal has reported the same, and on Tuesday the board instead announced a new committee to oversee national security policy and export-control compliance. Nvidia declined to comment.

Why it matters: The founder himself is now publicly lobbying for the job he lost, hours before interim CEO Colette Kress faces her first earnings call Tuesday. The board must weigh that pressure — from employees, investors and the president — against the governance and national-security questions that forced Huang out, and against open resistance in Congress, where one senator called reinstatement "one of the worst ideas I've heard today."
