10:T734,The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of its key national criminal justice database, the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), according to an AP source cited by PBS NewsHour. The database is the shared system federal, state, and local law enforcement use to exchange information, including warrants and missing-person records.
What happened
Reports on Friday said both the FBI and the US Department of Agriculture (USDA) were hacked. The FBI intrusion targeted the NCIC, and reporting describes it as the second such attack on the agency in the last few months. President Donald Trump commented on the reported hack, with CNBC reporting that he saw a possible 'positive spin' and TechCrunch reporting that he called it 'not good.'
Why it matters
The NCIC is not a peripheral system; it is the daily lookup tool for officers checking warrants and missing persons across jurisdictions. A breach there raises questions about the integrity of the records police rely on, and a second intrusion in months suggests the FBI's own defences are under sustained pressure.
Sources agree-disagree
Six outlets — the BBC, CNBC, TechCrunch, The Verge, PBS NewsHour, and Axios — agree that the FBI is investigating an apparent hack of its crime database, and that the USDA was also reported hacked. The outlets differ only in emphasis: CNBC and TechCrunch headline different Trump reactions to the same reported breach. Trust label: Multi-source agreement. The core facts are consistent across six outlets, with the hack still described as apparent and under investigation.
Still uncertain
It is not confirmed what the intruders accessed, whether any records were altered or stolen, or who was behind the attack.
