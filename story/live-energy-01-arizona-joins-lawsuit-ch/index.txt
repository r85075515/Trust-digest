10:T6d8,The water war in the American Southwest is heading to court: Arizona has formally moved to join a lawsuit challenging the federal plan to make Lower Basin states absorb cuts to Colorado River water.
What happened
The Arizona Department of Water Resources filed a motion Friday to join litigation against the federal government's plan for Colorado River operations over the next 10 years, reports Inside Climate News journalist Wyatt Myskow. Arizona argues that forcing the Lower Colorado River Basin states to shoulder the water cuts would devastate its economy. The move follows restrictions announced this summer by the Trump administration: Interior Department measures that could slash water supplies to the lower basin states, including Arizona and California. The Guardian's version frames it as Arizona joining Nevada's legal battle against the proposed cuts to their shares of the shrinking river.
Why it matters
The Colorado River sustains tens of millions of people across the Southwest; how its shrinking flow is divided decides the fate of farms, cities and whole regional economies. States suing the federal government outright signals that negotiations have failed — the next decade of water rules may end up being written by a court.
