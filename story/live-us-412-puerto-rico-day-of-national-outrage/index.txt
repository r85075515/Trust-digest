10:T878,What happened
On Friday, November 27, 2026, thousands of Puerto Ricans—including retirees, university students, and bus drivers—gathered in San Juan for a protest dubbed the "Day of National Outrage." Organized by roughly 30 labor unions, environmental advocates, and religious organizations, demonstrators marched behind banners protesting record-high electricity bills, frequent power and water outages, and a steep rise in everyday living costs. Organizers estimated turnout at approximately 8,000 participants, with many waving current Puerto Rican flags alongside pre-1952 commonwealth-era flags.

Why it matters
The demonstration took place one day after Puerto Rico's Supreme Court ruled 5–4 to cancel private operator Luma Energy's power transmission contract, underscoring deep public anger over chronic grid instability and household affordability across the U.S. territory. Civic leaders signaled that the rally marks the start of sustained coordinated pressure on territorial officials.

Source differences
Al Jazeera ("Thousands of Puerto Ricans join 'Day of National Outrage' protest") includes organizers' turnout estimate of around 8,000 people, notes the presence of pre-1952 flags, and links the demonstration to the Puerto Rican Supreme Court's November 26 ruling against Luma Energy. SFGATE ("Protesters take to the streets in Puerto Rico's capital as standard of living deteriorates") publishes the Associated Press wire report quoting retiree Radamés García Badillo (拉達梅斯·加西亞·巴迪略), Sierra Club Puerto Rico executive director Hernaliz Vázquez (赫納利茲·巴斯克斯), and human rights attorney Eva Prados (伊娃·普拉多斯) on weekly neighborhood blackouts and business closures.

Still uncertain
Whether the territorial government will introduce emergency utility rate relief or announce concrete transition timelines following the court ruling against Luma Energy, and when organizers will schedule follow-up protests, has not been announced.
