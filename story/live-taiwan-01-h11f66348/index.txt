What happened
Chiu Chui-cheng (邱垂正), minister of Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council, said Beijing has stepped up combined pressure on Taiwan since 2022, including military aircraft and naval vessels operating around the island, diplomatic suppression, cross-border repression and legal warfare. He predicted that in Xi's fourth term Beijing will adopt a more aggressive approach of "governing before unification" (未統先治), which could more directly change the status quo, and that the contest over jurisdiction across the Taiwan Strait will grow increasingly severe. The remarks were reported by the Central News Agency (CNA) in a November 24 dispatch from Taipei by reporter Liao Wen-chi (廖文綺).
Why it matters
The warning matters because it is the official assessment of the minister responsible for Taiwan's cross-strait policy, and it frames Beijing's actions as a sustained, multi-domain campaign rather than a series of isolated incidents. If Chiu's prediction proves right, Taipei expects pressure to intensify rather than ease as Xi begins a fourth term, with the fight over jurisdiction — who exercises authority over Taiwan and in which arenas — becoming a sharper and more open contest. That framing also signals that Taiwan's government is preparing the public and its partners for a longer period of friction, even without any single dramatic escalation.
