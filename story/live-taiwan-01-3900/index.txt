10:T731,Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs says it has trained more than 3,900 cybersecurity professionals so far in 2026, CNA reported from Taipei on November 20. The ministry is working on two fronts — developing talent and strengthening supply-chain resilience — and is pushing security certification for semiconductor equipment, with the first SEMI E187 labels expected by the end of the year.

What happened
Reporter Pan Ziyu writes that the ministry continues to build Taiwan's independent cybersecurity capacity. The headline figure is the training total: more than 3,900 people this year. In parallel, the ministry is promoting a certification regime for semiconductor equipment security, and expects to issue the first SEMI E187 verification labels before year-end, helping local firms align with international standards.

Why it matters
Taiwan's semiconductor industry sits at the centre of global supply chains, so equipment security is a national competitiveness issue, not a back-office chore. A domestic certification label gives Taiwanese suppliers a credential buyers abroad can recognise. The training number is the other half of the same bet: certified hardware needs certified people to run it.

Sources agree-disagree
Only CNA has reported this in the feed, so the figures have no second source. Nothing in the material conflicts with itself; the training total and the certification timetable come from the same ministry account.

Still uncertain
The feed does not break down the 3,900 trainees by course, level, or employer, and gives no count of firms preparing for SEMI E187 certification or the criteria for the first labels. Whether the year-end timetable holds will only be clear when the first labels are actually issued.
