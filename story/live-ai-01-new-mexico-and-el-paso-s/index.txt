What happened
Last December, New Mexico officials celebrated the publication of a plan designed to dramatically reduce the state's greenhouse gas emissions by 2050. A few months later, just across the state line in Texas, Meta began building a massive data center on the northeast edge of El Paso, on the New Mexico border. ProPublica's reporting frames the collision directly: the region's climate spending could be wiped out by the new facilities' footprint. In a separate item, Wired AI reports that amid a wave of backlash, industry-friendly groups are employing creative, time-tested strategies to woo the youth — the headline's pitch is “your kid's field trip,” a PR plan to make data centers lovable.
Why it matters
Data centers are the physical cost of the AI boom, and their power demand lands hardest on local grids and local climate math. This story is that tension in miniature: a state writes an ambitious 2050 emissions plan while a hyperscaler builds next door. The youth-outreach angle matters too — it shows the industry knows it has a legitimacy problem and is playing a long game for public acceptance.
