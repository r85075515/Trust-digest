10:T5c3,AMD's EPYC "Venice" processors are the first chips built on the company's new Zen 6 architecture, tuned for agentic AI workloads, and early testing puts them up against Nvidia's Vera platform.

What happened
The Next Platform reports that AMD tuned its Venice EPYC CPUs for agentic AI, and Tom's Hardware has spent several weeks testing two chips from the range. The review compares the new processors with Nvidia's Vera and traces how Zen 6 departs from Zen 5, ahead of the consumer-focused Olympic Ridge launch expected next year. Tom's Hardware's headline cites dual EPYC 9996 chips with 1,024 threads delivering up to a 93% generational performance gain.

Why it matters
Agentic AI shifts the bottleneck from raw graphics compute toward CPU-heavy orchestration, and AMD is pitching Venice as the chip for that shift. A near-doubling of performance per generation, if it holds in independent testing, would reset expectations for data-center CPU upgrades.

Sources agree and disagree
Both outlets agree Venice debuts Zen 6 and targets agentic AI. The 93% figure comes from Tom's Hardware's own benchmarking, not from a second outlet, so treat it as one lab's result rather than a confirmed industry number.

Still uncertain
The feed summaries give no pricing, power consumption, or availability dates, and the full benchmark conditions behind the 93% claim are not visible in the summaries. Broader third-party reviews are still to come.
