10:T7eb,What happened
At 10:01 p.m. on Thursday, November 26, 2026, residents across a large swath of the Tucson metropolitan area experienced a sudden, momentary power flicker that briefly dimmed lights and knocked out home Wi-Fi and cable connections, with several residents on social media also reporting loud booming sounds. On Friday, November 27, Tucson Electric Power (TEP) spokesperson Joe Barrios (喬·巴里奧斯) confirmed that the disturbance was caused by debris hitting a transmission line, adding that the utility was not aware of any sustained power outages resulting from the event.

Why it matters
Because the voltage dip hit simultaneously across multiple Tucson neighborhoods on Thanksgiving night and disrupted internet service, online speculation quickly spread regarding possible grid explosions or electromagnetic pulses; TEP's official explanation clarifies that grid protection systems cleared a transient transmission fault without leaving customers without power.

Source differences
The Arizona Daily Star ("Here's what caused that power flicker across Tucson Thursday night") provides TEP spokesperson Joe Barrios's official November 27 explanation attributing the 10:01 p.m. flicker to transmission-line debris and confirming no ongoing customer outages. Hindustan Times ("Tucson residents complain of power flickering, outages, hearing ‘boom’ sound; video captures moment lights went out") compiles initial Thursday night resident reports, porch-camera footage, and Downdetector spikes among Cox internet users prior to TEP's statement.

Still uncertain
What specific type of debris struck the transmission line, the exact corridor where the contact occurred, and whether the loud booms heard by east-side residents were directly linked to the same transmission fault have not been detailed by the utility.
