10:T69c,Russia's uncrewed Progress 97 resupply spacecraft—designated Progress MS-36 by Roscosmos—successfully docked to the Earth-facing port of the International Space Station's (ISS) Rassvet module at 7:08 a.m. EST on Thursday, November 26, 2026. The spacecraft completed a two-day orbital rendezvous after lifting off atop a Soyuz rocket from the Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan on Tuesday, November 24.

Carrying approximately three tons of provisions, propellant, water, and maintenance hardware, Progress 97 arrived on U.S. Thanksgiving Day to replenish the Expedition 75 crew aboard the orbiting laboratory. Following leak and pressure checks between the spacecraft and the Rassvet hatch, station crew members opened the hatch to begin unloading cargo. NASA noted that Progress 97 is scheduled to remain docked for roughly eight months before departing for a destructive reentry over the Pacific Ocean.

Regular automated cargo flights remain vital to sustaining continuous human habitation and orbital reboost operations aboard the ISS as the outpost enters its late-decade operational phase.

NASA's Space Station blog, Global People Daily News, and SpaceIsTheLimit consistently corroborate the 7:08 a.m. EST docking time on November 26, the Rassvet module port, and the roughly three-ton cargo manifest without discrepancy.

Whether any specialized holiday treats or new experiment payloads aboard Progress 97 will be prioritized in upcoming Expedition 75 research schedules has not yet been detailed by mission managers.
