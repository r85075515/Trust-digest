10:T691,Scientists have discovered a way to operate multiple silicon qubits simultaneously with remarkable accuracy, overcoming a major challenge in scaling up quantum computers. Their technique maintained accuracy of approximately 99.9% even when all five qubits were running at once.
What happened
The new technique lets five silicon qubits run at the same time without losing accuracy, opening the door to larger and more powerful quantum processors. A second headline bundled into the same card reports a different step: IBM linking two quantum chips in a move toward bigger computers. A closing snippet line adds that superconducting cables connect quantum bits on different chips, but improvements are still needed.
Why it matters
Scaling is the hard part of quantum computing: adding qubits is of little use if operating them together destroys accuracy. A method that holds accuracy near 99.9% with five qubits running at once removes one hidden obstacle to processors that are larger and genuinely more powerful.
Sources agree / disagree
The two outlets do not corroborate each other, because they report different developments: the silicon-qubit result comes from ScienceDaily, and the IBM chip-linking headline comes from Science. They should be read as two separate reports on the same scaling theme, not as two witnesses to one result. Trust label: Bundled stories — the card combines two distinct quantum reports; treat each on its own evidence.
Still uncertain
