10:T6e9,What happened
Singapore-headquartered renewable energy developer Vena Energy announced it has secured a 113 million USD green loan facility from international lenders BNP Paribas and Standard Chartered. The proceeds will be used to refinance 114 MW of operational renewable energy assets across Vena Energy's portfolio in Indonesia.

Why it matters
Refinancing operational wind and solar projects through dedicated green-labeled debt lowers long-term financing costs and frees up capital for new clean-energy buildouts in Southeast Asia. The deal also demonstrates sustained appetite among global commercial banks for bankable utility-scale renewable assets in Indonesia's energy transition.

Source differences
Vena Group's official announcement ("Vena Energy Secures US$113 Million Green Loan Facility to Refinance Indonesian Operating Assets") confirms the 113 million USD green loan facility arranged with BNP Paribas and Standard Chartered for operating Indonesian assets. PV Tech ("Vena Energy raises US$113 million for 114MW Indonesian portfolio") and EnergyNews.pro ("Vena Energy secures $113 million green loan for its Indonesian assets") specify the 114 MW portfolio capacity. DealStreetAsia ("Singapore's Vena secures $113m green loan after INA investment") places the borrowing in context following prior investment from the Indonesia Investment Authority (INA).

Still uncertain
Vena Energy and the lending banks have not disclosed the loan's maturity tenor, interest rate spread, or the exact breakdown of wind versus solar megawatts within the 114 MW refinanced portfolio.
