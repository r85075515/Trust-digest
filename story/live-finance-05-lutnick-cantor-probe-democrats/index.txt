10:Tab1,Three House Democrats — Jamie Raskin, Gregory Meeks and Sydney Kamlager-Dove — have opened a probe into whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick used his office to help Cantor Fitzgerald, the investment firm now controlled by his sons, avoid losses linked to the scandal at Nvidia, the Financial Times reported on Wednesday. In a November 17 letter to Brandon Lutnick, Howard Lutnick's son and Cantor's chair, the lawmakers said they are seeking information on whether any of the commerce secretary's actions were "improperly influenced by Cantor Fitzgerald's holdings or business interests." The letter demands that Cantor preserve and produce records dating back to January 1, 2024 — covering Nvidia-related trading and export-control communications — and confirm its preservation steps by December 1.

What happened: The probe moves the Nvidia scandal's Washington front from the chipmaker itself to the cabinet secretary whose department issues chip export licences and decides which foreign firms land on the entity list. Lutnick's Commerce Department has been criticised by both parties for declining to blacklist dozens of Chinese firms and for clearing Nvidia sales to China. Howard Lutnick ran Cantor Fitzgerald for decades before joining the cabinet; his sons Brandon and Kyle now run the firm.

Why it matters: The allegation, if substantiated, would be a conflict-of-interest case at the centre of US chip policy: the official overseeing export controls accused of shaping them around his family's firm. For now it is an allegation in an opposition-party letter — no evidence has been made public, and Cantor says it holds no significant proprietary position in Nvidia shares. Lutnick has already agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 10; this probe adds a House track aimed at his family's firm rather than at Nvidia.

Sources agree / disagree: The Financial Times first reported the letter; Reuters carried the report while noting it could not independently verify it, and Politico reported the same letter with the preservation demands. Cantor's response — that it has no significant proprietary Nvidia position — is on the record via those reports.

Still uncertain: Whether any of Lutnick's export-control decisions were actually influenced by Cantor's interests; what Cantor's Nvidia-linked exposure really was; whether the Commerce Department or Lutnick himself will respond on the record; and whether the Republican-controlled House committees will act on a Democratic inquiry.
