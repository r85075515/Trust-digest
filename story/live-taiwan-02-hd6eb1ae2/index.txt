What happened
According to a Central News Agency report by reporter Lu Chia-jung (呂佳蓉) from Taipei on November 26, the Chinese Communist Party announced the fall of Chen Jining, a member of the party's Central Politburo and secretary of the Shanghai municipal party committee. Mainland Affairs Council spokesperson Liang Wen-chieh said the same day that, regarding the falls of officials inside the CCP and related circumstances, the council as a rule does not comment specifically, but will maintain a high degree of attention. The CNA item is short and is the only source in this feed for both the announcement and the council's response.
Why it matters
Shanghai's party secretary is one of the most senior posts in the CCP system, and its holder sits on the Politburo, so a fall at that level is a significant internal event by rank alone. For Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council, which handles cross-strait policy, personnel upheaval at the top of the CCP is directly relevant to its watching brief, even when Taipei chooses not to comment publicly. The council's formula — no specific comment, close monitoring — is the standard posture the spokesperson himself described.

Still uncertain
