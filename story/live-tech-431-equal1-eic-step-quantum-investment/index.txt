10:T9a2,What happened
On November 27, 2026, Dublin-based quantum computing company Equal1 announced its selection to advance to due diligence under the European Innovation Council (EIC) STEP Scale Up program for a potential equity investment of up to EUR 30 million (approximately 32.8 million USD). Spun out of University College Dublin, Equal1 integrates silicon spin qubits, control electronics, and error correction onto a single chip fabricated with standard complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) foundry processes. The EIC STEP Scale Up scheme, backed by a EUR 300 million budget in 2026 and EUR 290 million in 2027, provides EUR 10 million to EUR 30 million in equity alongside private co-investors; Equal1 and Delft-based quantum control firm Qblox were among seven deep-tech firms selected in the November cohort totaling up to EUR 113 million.

Why it matters
Conventional quantum computers rely on bulky dilution refrigerators and bespoke cryogenic wiring that limit deployment outside specialized national labs. By fabricating qubits and control circuitry on a single standard CMOS silicon chip, Equal1 aims to lower hardware infrastructure barriers and enable rack-mounted, on-premises quantum systems compatible with existing semiconductor supply chains.

Source differences
The Quantum Insider published "Equal1 Selected to Advance in EIC STEP Scale Up Program For Potential Equity Investment of up to €30 Million", focusing on Equal1's single-chip CMOS integration and on-premises deployment plans. Quantum Computing Report published "Equal1 and Qblox Selected for European Innovation Council STEP Scale Up Equity Program", pairing Equal1 with Dutch quantum control provider Qblox and noting the cohort's combined EUR 113 million allocation. Equal1's corporate press release carried the title "Equal1 Selected to Advance in EIC STEP Scale Up Program for Potential Equity Investment of up to €30 Million".

Still uncertain
Selection for the STEP Scale Up cohort advances Equal1 into due diligence rather than constituting a binding payout; the final equity tranche size, private co-investor syndicate composition, and closing timeline remain subject to EIC Fund review.
