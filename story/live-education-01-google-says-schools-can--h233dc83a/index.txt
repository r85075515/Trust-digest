10:T6c2,Google told schools they could keep its search engine only if they also accepted its artificial intelligence tools — and at least one large district decided to work around the condition instead of accepting it.
What happened
Months before Google delivered that message, Fairfax County Public Schools in Virginia, the ninth-largest district in the United States, developed its own solution: a browser plug-in that hides AI-generated answers in students' search results. The district was not alone in looking for workarounds, according to EdSurge, which reported the story.
Why it matters
The standoff puts a price on a tool schools have long treated as basic infrastructure. If search now arrives bundled with AI features districts did not ask for, administrators must choose between accepting tools they have not vetted for classrooms and building their own filters. Fairfax County's plug-in shows the second path is possible, but smaller districts may lack the staff to build one.
