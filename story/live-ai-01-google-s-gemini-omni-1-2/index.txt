10:T6a7,Google DeepMind is making Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generally available, stretching AI video clips to 15 seconds with stronger consistency and step-by-step conversational editing — at $0.07 per second for 720p, 30% less than its predecessor.
What happened
The video-generation model now creates clips up to 15 seconds long, can be edited step by step through conversation, and costs $0.07 per second at 720p, 30% less than the model it replaces, The Decoder reports. Google's own announcement, "Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash: Fast and cost-effective with longer clips," presents the same figures. Users who draft in low resolution and upscale only the final result can save even more, according to the reports.
Why it matters
Fifteen seconds is long enough for a complete short scene rather than a fragment, and the price cut lands at the same time, so the practical effect is cheaper short-form video. Conversational step-by-step editing also matters: it turns revision into a dialogue instead of a fresh generation each time, which should cut wasted renders for creators.
Sources agree / disagree
Google AI Blog and The Decoder agree on the core facts: 15-second clips, conversational editing and the 30% price cut to $0.07 per second at 720p. No clear numeric or name conflicts appear in the short feed snippets. Trust label: Two-source agreement — two outlets agree on the core facts.
Still uncertain
The snippets give no rollout dates or regional availability, no pricing for higher resolutions, and no quality benchmarks against rival video models.
