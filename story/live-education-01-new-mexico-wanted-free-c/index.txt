The state opened the door to everyone, but it does not have the capacity to meet the demand that followed.
What happened
In November last year, New Mexico began offering free childcare to every eligible family with no income test, NPR reports. The policy made it the first state to try universal free childcare. The catch is capacity: there are not enough providers, staff and places to serve all the families who now qualify. A second NPR item in the same bundle looks at what has worked and what has not in the first year, but the feed material does not spell out the numbers — how many families applied, how many are waiting, or how large the shortfall is.
Why it matters
The New Mexico experiment is being watched as a test case for universal childcare in the U.S.: if the first state to try it cannot staff it, other states will hesitate. The takeaway is structural rather than political — eligibility is cheap to legislate, capacity is slow and expensive to build, and the gap between the two is where families actually wait.
