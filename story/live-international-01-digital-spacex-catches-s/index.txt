10:T660,SpaceX has pulled off a first: its Mechazilla launch-and-catch tower has caught a Starship upper stage with the mechanical arms nicknamed "chopsticks." The company had caught boosters this way before, but never the Starship vehicle itself — the part that actually flies to orbit and back.
What happened
Starship Test Flight 15 was the first attempt to catch both the booster and the upper stage of the rocket. SpaceX aborted the planned catch of the Flight 15 booster, then successfully captured the Starship with the Mechazilla tower. Coverage from the BBC, CNBC, Bloomberg, The Verge, The Hill and NPR all describe the same sequence: a historic first for the ship itself, with the booster recovery called off.
Why it matters
Catching the upper stage matters because Starship is only cheap to fly again if both halves come home. Recovering the ship proves the tower can handle the more delicate half of the vehicle, not just the booster. The Hill's coverage notes praise in Washington, a sign the milestone registered beyond the space industry.
Sources agree / disagree
Six outlets — BBC World, CNBC, Bloomberg, The Verge, The Hill and NPR News — agree on the core facts: first-ever catch of the Starship vehicle, booster catch aborted. No outlet disputes the sequence.
