What happened
NPR, The New York Times, and The Guardian report that President Trump's agreement with Vladimir Putin to temporarily lift sanctions on U.S. imports of Russian diesel has provoked fury across Europe. Ukraine's President Zelenskyy questioned Washington's motives, with Ukraine calling the deal 'a gift' to President Putin, while European officials coalesced around a cautious diplomatic message and allies vowed to keep pressure on Moscow. Trump also announced a partial ceasefire, but Zelenskyy said the deal was 'news to me.' Analysts cited in the coverage say the fuel will take weeks to ship, the quantity is too small to affect prices, and the full deal is unlikely to be met given damage to Russian refineries.
Why it matters
The backlash exposes a widening transatlantic rift: Europe reads the diesel deal as Washington softening economic pressure on Moscow, while Kyiv fears being sidelined in decisions about its own war. If analysts are right that the volumes are too small to matter economically, the deal's real cost may be diplomatic — trust spent for fuel that barely moves the market.
