10:T5db,Cambridge Dictionary has named 'human' its Word of the Year for 2026, announcing the choice on 18 November. AI can now write, generate images and mimic human tone and emotion, and the dictionary's pick turns that familiarity into a question: what about people can machines not replace?
What happened
Cambridge Dictionary announced 'human' as its 2026 Word of the Year on 18 November, PTS reports. Vogue Taiwan reports the same choice. The dictionary passed over new technology terms and internet slang and chose a word everyone already knows, framing the selection around AI's growing ability to write, draw and imitate human speech and feeling.
Why it matters
The choice treats the AI moment as a question about people rather than about machines. As AI output grows harder to tell apart from human work, the word 'human' marks the qualities — left unnamed in the short reports — that the dictionary invites readers to weigh.
Sources agree / disagree
PTS and Vogue Taiwan agree on the word, the year and the 18 November announcement. The PTS headline ties the choice to a sense of crisis in the AI era; Vogue Taiwan frames it as rethinking what it means to be human. No numeric or name conflicts appear in the short feed snippets.
Still uncertain
The dictionary's selection criteria, search data and full rationale are not in the snippets, and neither report lists the human traits meant to be irreplaceable. Check the publisher pages for the full announcement.
