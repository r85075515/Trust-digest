10:T793,Silicon Valley is still defending Jensen Huang — and, in the same breath, still insisting it can see the future. That is the double portrait in this cluster: Wired on an industry in denial over the chip king's sudden resignation, and MIT Technology Review on visionaries whose foresight failed at the ballot box.
What happened
Wired reports that Nvidia employees and tech insiders never thought Huang would lose his job, and that many of them are still in denial despite his sudden resignation. MIT Technology Review turns to the same crowd's other claim: leaders like OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman and SpaceX boss Elon Musk have built careers claiming to see the future. Long-term vision is the industry's currency — Meta's Mark Zuckerberg built a 10-year road map for Facebook in 2016, and Google told investors before its public offering that it was unconcerned with short-term results. And yet, the Review notes, they got the midterms wrong.
Why it matters
The two pieces belong together because they test the same myth from opposite ends: if Silicon Valley cannot see a CEO's fall coming, and cannot call an election, the long-term vision it sells to investors and the public deserves a discount. Huang's fall is the sharpest test yet, because he was the industry's most celebrated prophet of the AI boom.
