What happened
The subpoenas follow the departure of former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. Bonta said the investigation covers concerns that the company may have misled investors, among other issues. Separately, a shareholder civil lawsuit against Supermicro is moving forward, with investors alleging that former Chief Growth Officer Cenly Chen could not have lacked knowledge of the company's illicit sales; Chen is married to outgoing Nvidia vice president Jay Puri. Bloomberg's audio bulletin in the same cluster also carried news of Anthropic's historic IPO debut, but no details of that listing appear in the available feed text.
Why it matters
A state attorney general subpoenaing the world's leading AI chipmaker puts export controls, investor trust and US-China technology rivalry on the same docket. The Supermicro lawsuit widens the picture by raising questions about who inside the supply chain knew about the alleged diversions, so the inquiry may reach beyond Nvidia itself.
Sources agree / disagree
BBC, CNBC, The Hill and the Los Angeles Times all report the California subpoena as the central fact, with no contradictions among them. Tom's Hardware focuses on the Supermicro lawsuit and Chen's role, while Bloomberg bundles the Anthropic IPO debut into the same cluster; those are separate stories carried together, not conflicting accounts of one event.
Still uncertain
The subpoenas' contents, the chip models and quantities involved, and whether Nvidia knew about any diversion have not been disclosed in the coverage. Details of Anthropic's IPO debut are likewise absent from the feed text. Further clarity depends on the attorney general's office and on Nvidia's response.
