10:T7bd,Hopes for an agreement on tackling climate change collapsed at the UN's COP31 climate conference after a row about mentioning fossil fuels, and the talks in Antalya, Turkey ended on Sunday afternoon without a key deal. Countries could not find consensus on climate finance, fossil fuels and carbon capture, making this the longest-running summit in the conference's history.
What happened
The COP31 talks ended on Sunday afternoon in Antalya after negotiations broke down two days after they had been due to end. A key deal text that did not mention fossil fuels was rejected, with the European Union and others refusing to accept it. Mohamed Adow, director of Power Shift Africa, called the latest text a total mess and said countries should throw it out, adding that it fails to deliver vital climate finance to the vulnerable. Inside Climate News reports the conference centre resembled a going-out-of-business sale at a pawnshop for broken dreams by the time the talks ended.
Why it matters
A climate summit that cannot even agree on mentioning fossil fuels is a signal, not just a scheduling failure: the divide over the core driver of warming is now blocking the rest of the agenda, including money for the countries most exposed to it. The collapse also strands the finance question, which vulnerable nations count on, with no new commitment to point to.
Sources agree-disagree
The BBC, Inside Climate News, The Guardian and NPR all report the same breakdown: no deal, divisions over fossil fuels, finance and carbon capture, and the longest summit on record. Framing differs, the Guardian stresses the fractious ending, Inside Climate News the fizzle, but the facts do not conflict. Trust label: Multi-source agreement, with different framing of the same collapse.
Still uncertain
The summaries give no detail on what happens next, no date for resumed talks, and no final text to examine.
