10:Taa0,Almost a third of Amazon's Black Friday deals “provided no savings at all” over the past two years, according to a new report covered by MarketWatch — and Amazon is pushing back as the shopping season opens. The claim lands just as retailers roll out early Black Friday discounts, so the question of what counts as a real deal is suddenly practical, not theoretical.
What happened
MarketWatch covered a report finding that nearly one in three Amazon Black Friday deals over the last two years offered no savings at all, and Amazon has disputed the finding while defending its pricing. Around the same bundle, The Verge rounded up steep PlayStation 5 discounts — controllers, headsets and major titles, in some cases 50 percent off — as Sony's promotion runs ahead of the big day. Engadget, meanwhile, asked whether the Jensen Huang fallout could mean cheap RTX graphics cards this Black Friday, and answered its own question: GPU prices are driven by forces that make deep discounts unlikely, scandal or not. A separate thread in the bundle covers the Trump administration's move to require export licences for AI chips routed through Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil, aimed at blocking China's remote access.
Why it matters
For shoppers, the report is a warning to treat “deal” labels as marketing until the price history says otherwise — especially with retailers starting discounts weeks early. For the industry, the GPU thread is the more durable story: if graphics cards barely discount even in a scandal year, bargain hunters should reset expectations. The export-licence thread shows the same week also tightened the rules around who can reach advanced AI hardware at all.
Sources agree / disagree
The eight outlets in this bundle are not covering one story but several: MarketWatch on the Amazon report, The Verge on PlayStation deals, Engadget on GPU pricing, Al Jazeera and ESG Dive on the Nvidia and export-licence angles. They do not contradict each other; they simply describe different corners of the same shopping-and-chips week. The report's underlying data and methodology were not in the feed material, so the “almost a third” figure rests on the report's own counting.
Still uncertain
Which deals the report counted, how it defined “no savings,” and Amazon's specific rebuttal were not in the snippets — verify them in the full MarketWatch piece before quoting the figure. The GPU and export-licence threads are separate stories bundled into one card, not parts of the Amazon finding.
