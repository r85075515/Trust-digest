10:T95a,Andy Taylor, the founding guitarist of Duran Duran, has died at 65 after living with prostate cancer for years, and fellow musicians have been paying tribute to a player one account calls possibly the most underrated to emerge from the early 1980s.
What happened
Taylor died on Monday, November 23, following a years-long battle with prostate cancer, according to multiple outlets in the feed. He had been treated for the disease, and related coverage in the same feed notes he had previously missed Duran Duran's Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction because of stage 4 cancer. Taylor was a founding guitarist of Duran Duran, co-wrote some of the band's biggest hits, later formed The Power Station, and also enjoyed a solo career and a reputation as a serial collaborator. Billboard's tribute report gathers reactions under the words 'What a talent', including from Belinda Carlisle, UB40 and Spandau Ballet. Carlisle recalled that Taylor played the guitar solo in her song 'Mad About You', appears in its video, and made special appearances onstage at some of her first solo shows, calling him a genius and herself blessed to have worked with him.
Why it matters
Taylor's playing helped define Duran Duran's sound in a golden era for electric guitarists, and the breadth of the tributes — from pop peers across several bands — reflects a career that reached beyond one group. The coverage also closes a public illness story that fans had followed through his Hall of Fame absence and his earlier comments about treatment extending his life.
Sources agree / disagree
BBC, Billboard, Rolling Stone, Hollywood Life, Sky News and NPR agree on the central facts: Taylor's death at 65, the prostate cancer battle, and his Duran Duran role. A figure conflict flagged in the raw feed ('8211 vs 23') is an artefact of a punctuation character code and the November 23 date, not a genuine disagreement, so it is not treated as one here.
