10:T753,What happened
California-based drone delivery company Zipline is seeking Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) authorization to scale commercial drone operations across Arizona's Phoenix and Tucson metropolitan areas. Under the FAA's draft environmental assessment, Zipline proposes up to 65,000 daily deliveries in Phoenix and up to 65,000 daily deliveries in Tucson—totaling 130,000 flights per day—supported by up to 65 docking towers and 1,300 pickup locations in each metro area to deliver food, prescriptions, and retail goods.

Why it matters
If approved, the Arizona expansion would rank among the highest-density commercial autonomous drone networks in the United States, testing airspace integration, noise thresholds, and last-mile logistics across two major Sun Belt cities.

Source differences
The FAA's official document ("Draft Environmental Assessment for Zipline International Inc") sets out the exact operational ceilings of 65 docking towers, 1,300 pickup sites, and 65,000 daily flights each for Phoenix and Tucson. AVweb ("Zipline Seeks FAA Approval For 130,000 Daily Drone Deliveries In Arizona") and DroneXL ("Zipline Asks FAA for 130,000 Drone Deliveries a Day Over Arizona") summarize the statewide 130,000-flight threshold and note separate public comment deadlines for the two metro areas, while 12News ("Drone delivery giant Zipline plans Phoenix expansion, with potential for 65,000 deliveries per day") highlights local community engagement in Phoenix.

Still uncertain
When the FAA will issue its final environmental finding and operating authorization, as well as how quickly Zipline will phase in flights toward the 130,000 daily cap, remains undecided pending public comment review.
