10:T6a1,What happened
Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (魯拉) confirmed that veteran banker Luiz Carlos Trabuco Cappi (路易斯・卡洛斯・特拉布科), chairman of Banco Bradesco, will lead Brazil's finance ministry. According to the presidency, Trabuco accepted the role following an off-agenda meeting at the Alvorada presidential residence earlier in the week and is scheduled to take office in 2027. Brazilian markets rallied on the confirmation, with the benchmark Ibovespa index jumping 6.1% as investors anticipated the appointment.

Why it matters
Previous finance chiefs under the administration, including Fernando Haddad (費爾南多・哈達德) and Dario Durigan (達里奧・杜里根), faced internal pushback from the government's progressive wing when advocating fiscal restraint. Tapping a prominent private-sector banker—who had previously declined the post in 2014—is widely viewed by investors as a move to bolster fiscal credibility and stabilize Brazil's public debt outlook.

Source comparison
The Associated Press (AP), Bloomberg, and the Financial Times all reported Lula's confirmation of Trabuco and the subsequent market surge. AP emphasized the immediate market rally upon official confirmation, while Bloomberg and local outlets noted the 6.1% Ibovespa jump and the 2027 start timeline.

What remains uncertain
It remains unclear how Trabuco will balance investor demands for spending restraint with social and economic priorities championed by Lula's political allies once he assumes office in 2027.
