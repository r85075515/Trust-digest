10:T8c1,What happened
On November 28, 2026, Villepinte-based medical imaging contrast agent manufacturer Guerbet (Euronext Paris: GBT) announced it had finalized a comprehensive debt amendment and maturity extension agreement with its bank and bond creditors. The restructuring covers EUR 400 million in outstanding principal as of September 30, 2026, encompassing a syndicated credit agreement (Tranche A, Tranche B, and a EUR 100 million revolving credit facility), Euro PP private placement bonds extended to December 1, 2030, and EUR 50 million in state-guaranteed Relance bonds maturing July 25, 2031. Interest rate revisions are projected to add roughly EUR 5 million in financing costs in fiscal year 2027.

Why it matters
Extending medium-term debt maturities removes near-term refinancing pressure for a key European diagnostic imaging supplier that generated EUR 786 million in 2025 revenue. Alongside the debt deal, Guerbet issued concrete 2027 guidance targeting 2% to 4% constant-exchange-rate revenue growth, a restated EBITDA margin around 12%, and a return to positive free cash flow, scaling toward EUR 900 million in revenue by 2030.

Source differences
The company's official GlobeNewswire release ("Guerbet secures its medium-term liquidity: Comprehensive agreement finalised with creditors on extending its financing maturities") provides the complete debt breakdown, leverage-linked interest rate grids (7.125% to 7.79% for Euro PP bonds), collateral pledges, and 2027–2030 financial targets. WebDisclosure ("Guerbet secures its liquidity thanks to an agreement with its creditors") summarizes the November 28 announcement for market participants without reproducing the full tranche schedules.

Still uncertain
Whether the agreement remains subject to final administrative closing conditions before year-end 2026, and whether Guerbet will meet the partial revolving credit facility clean-down requirement starting in fiscal 2028 to avoid an automatic upward interest rate step-up on January 1, 2029, remains to be seen.
