10:T8a3,What happened
The ATLAS collaboration released the first search at the Large Hadron Collider for quantum chromodynamics (QCD) instanton processes, analyzing 2015 low-pileup proton-proton collision data at 13 TeV corresponding to 1.6 nb^-1. According to ATLAS Experiment at CERN, University of Bonn, and Phys.org, the analysis identified isotropic low-mass events exceeding background expectations with a significance of 3.5 sigma. In the most sensitive region, 32,789 events were observed against roughly 28,800 background events and about 3,900 possible signal events, with a fitted signal rate of 2.3 ± 0.6 times the baseline prediction, as detailed in preprint arXiv:2611.22092; 3.5 sigma is only a hint and has not reached the discovery threshold.

Why it matters
QCD instantons correspond to non-perturbative tunneling transitions between distinct vacuum configurations of the strong force. Testing for them in high-energy proton collisions probes a non-perturbative regime of quantum chromodynamics that standard perturbative calculations cannot capture directly.

Source differences
ATLAS Experiment at CERN ("Catching the vacuum in the act with the ATLAS Experiment") presents the collaboration briefing on searching for QCD instantons in 2015 low-pileup 13 TeV data (1.6 nb^-1) and cites preprint arXiv:2611.22092. University of Bonn ("ATLAS finds hints of the hidden structure of the vacuum") shares the same title as Phys.org ("ATLAS finds hints of the hidden structure of the vacuum"), with University of Bonn issuing the institutional news release and Phys.org carrying the coverage from that same release chain; both report the 3.5-sigma significance, 32,789 observed events versus about 28,800 background and about 3,900 possible signal events, and the 2.3 ± 0.6 signal rate factor.

Still uncertain
Because 3.5 sigma is only a hint and below the discovery threshold, more data and refined analysis are needed, while background modeling and the theoretical signal rate prediction still carry uncertainties.
