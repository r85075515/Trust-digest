What happened
Huang, the cofounder who led Nvidia, stepped down abruptly on Thursday after revelations of alleged extramarital affairs. BBC Business reports that the former Nvidia chief executive has been summoned to testify before Congress about an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Under Huang, Nvidia became the engine for the AI boom and one of the tech industry's biggest environmental liabilities, according to The Verge, with its sought-after chips triggering a new wave of water- and chemical-intensive factories and draining power grids. Tom's Hardware reports that Malaysia and Singapore have stepped up Nvidia AI chip probes, with charges brought over 917 smuggled servers, and that the firm at the centre of the Huang controversy has announced an inventory audit to account for all restricted GPUs.

Why it matters
Huang was a founder-figure at the centre of the AI boom, so his sudden departure raises immediate questions about succession, since he did not name his own replacement, and about accountability at Nvidia. The scandal has also cast a spotlight on Singapore's success as a chip hub and as a location for alleged illicit trade, while Wired frames the episode as the end of the founder fairy tale and notes that Silicon Valley is still defending Huang.
