10:Tb60,The independent members of Nvidia's board said on Tuesday (Nov 10) they have retained Joon H. Kim and his firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to carry out the 'thorough and independent investigation' announced on Sunday; the one-line statement was issued at 14:07 ET via GlobeNewswire and the New York Times reported the hiring as breaking news.
What happened
The board announced its inquiry on Sunday (Nov 8) — the same day CEO Jensen Huang admitted an affair in a letter to staff. The board said then that it was looking into reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort, that it had 'no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised,' that its inquiry would 'examine all available information,' and that Huang fully supports the review — but it did not name its outside lawyers. The names revealed on Tuesday are heavyweight: Kim is a former acting US Attorney for the Southern District of New York who led the investigation into former New York governor Andrew Cuomo, and Wachtell Lipton is a top-tier firm for corporate governance and white-collar matters. The Wall Street Journal reported the probe will examine whether Nvidia was the target of a foreign espionage effort.
Why it matters
Who a board hires tells you what it thinks it is investigating. A former federal prosecutor signals this is not only about a CEO's private conduct but about national security: whether Huang was exploited and whether company secrets leaked. Yet the board's own independence is under simultaneous scrutiny — Reuters notes Nvidia has no board chair among its 11 directors and that three independent directors have served since the 1990s, alongside Huang for decades. The calendar is tight: Nvidia reports earnings on Nov 17, and three Republican House committee chairs have asked Huang to sit for a closed-door briefing on Nov 18. The takeaway: the investigator is credible; what is found, and whether it is published, still rests with a board whose independence is the question.
Sources agree / disagree
The board's GlobeNewswire statement gives only the names and the words 'thorough and independent' — no scope, timetable or publication commitment. The Wall Street Journal adds the espionage focus; Bloomberg Law and the New York Times agree on the retention itself. Reuters' analysis focuses instead on board composition and the governance problem of a suspected 'honey pot' operation — a different angle, not a factual conflict. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — the retention is on the record in the board's own statement.
Still uncertain
The board has not said how long the inquiry will take or whether its findings will be made public, nor whether it will examine what the board itself knew and when.
