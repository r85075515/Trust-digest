10:Ta25,Paraguay's president, Santiago Peña, has been impeached, removed from office and charged with breach of trust after the collapse of digital bank Ueno froze the accounts of nearly 3 million customers, Reuters reports.
What happened
Regulators seized Ueno on November 3 after its solvency ratio fell below half the legal minimum. The app-based lender had grown to roughly 2.9 million customers — more than 60 percent of the country's adults — and held about $800 million in public funds, including state pension money. Peña announced his resignation in a video on Thursday, but the Chamber of Deputies voted 78-0 to impeach him anyway, and the Senate removed him on Friday; Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in to serve out the term to 2028. Peña was arrested within hours, charged on Saturday with breach of trust and administration for personal gain, and ordered held in pretrial detention at a military facility. He denies wrongdoing and is pleading not guilty. Eight Ueno executives, including controlling shareholder Miguel Vázquez, were also arrested, and central bank chief Carlos Carvallo — a former Ueno executive appointed by Peña — resigned and was charged as well. Peña had held a stake in Ueno's parent company until March 2025 and publicly defended the bank as recently as last month.
Why it matters
A sitting president has fallen in five days because a bank he was personally tied to — and whose supervisor he appointed — collapsed with the savings of half the country inside it. It is only the second impeachment ouster since Paraguay's dictatorship ended in 1989, and it removes one of Washington's closest South American allies mid-term. The deposit-guarantee fund had paid out about $160 million to 1.2 million savers by Saturday; whether the roughly $800 million in public and pension deposits is made whole is the question that now decides whether this stays a political crisis or becomes a financial one.
Sources agree / disagree
Reuters, AFP reporting carried by multiple outlets, the BBC and The Hindu BusinessLine agree on the sequence — seizure, resignation attempt, impeachment votes, arrest and charges — and on the customer and public-fund figures.
