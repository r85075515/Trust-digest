10:T7f4,What happened
Four people familiar with the matter told Reuters that Indonesia's government is weighing an increase in subsidized fuel prices in early 2027, which would mark the first hike in five years. The deliberation comes as Indonesia's energy subsidy bill is projected to reach 500 trillion rupiah (27.2 billion USD) this year—30 percent above budget—while the rupiah sank to a record low of 18,395 per USD on Thursday, November 26.

Why it matters
As a net oil importer, Indonesia faces mounting fiscal strain under a statutory budget deficit cap of 3 percent of GDP, with the official deficit estimate sitting at 2.85 percent. Consumers switching from higher-grade fuels to cheaper subsidized grades have also lengthened queues at fuel stations, making subsidy reform both a macroeconomic necessity and a politically sensitive test given past fuel-price protests.

Source differences
MLQ.ai ("Indonesia weighs first subsidised fuel price rise in five years - MLQ.ai") and New Straits Times ("Indonesia considers subsidised fuel price hike as fiscal pressure") both carry the core Reuters exclusive detailing the 500 trillion rupiah subsidy overrun, the 2.85 percent GDP deficit estimate, and the rupiah's drop to 18,395 per USD. Investing.com ("Exclusive-Indonesia considers subsidised fuel price hike as fiscal pressure mounts, sources say By Reuters") additionally highlights consumers shifting away from higher-quality unsubsidized fuel, station queues, and the historical political backlash of fuel hikes in 1998 and 2015.

Still uncertain
Indonesian authorities have stated there is no hike this year and President Prabowo Subianto (Prabowo Subianto) has not issued a formal directive for 2027, leaving the exact timing, price adjustment percentage, and whether volume quotas will also be revised undecided.
