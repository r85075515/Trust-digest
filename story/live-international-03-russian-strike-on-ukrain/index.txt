10:T6d0,At least 26 people were killed in Russian strikes on a school being used as a humanitarian aid centre in Ukraine, according to BBC and Al Jazeera reports, in an attack Ukraine's president called utterly horrific.

What happened
Officials say at least nine 3,000kg aerial bombs hit a southern Ukrainian village in the Zaporizhzhia region. The strike came a day after a brief humanitarian truce ended in neighbouring Kherson. The New York Times headline describes the toll more broadly as dozens killed at a humanitarian hub, which is consistent with the figure of at least 26 rather than a competing count.

Why it matters
Striking a building used to distribute aid crosses a line that even war usually observes in public debate. If the target was a functioning aid centre, the attack will be cited as evidence about how Russia treats civilian relief, and it will shape how donors and neighbouring states judge the safety of humanitarian corridors.

Sources agree-disagree
BBC and Al Jazeera agree on at least 26 killed at the aid centre, and the New York Times account is consistent with that scale. The raw feed's note about differing figures does not describe a real casualty conflict: the number 10 in the bundled material is a 10% data-centre capacity figure from a separate Tom's Hardware item about Russian data centres, not a death toll. That item is unrelated to the strike and is mentioned here only to avoid mistaking it for corroboration.

Still uncertain
The material provided gives no confirmed final casualty count, no named village beyond the Zaporizhzhia region, and no Russian response to the specific strike. Those gaps leave the full toll and the stated reason for the attack unconfirmed.
