10:T854,「What happened」
Santa Cruz County Treasurer Alejandro Paz (Alejandro Paz) submitted his resignation effective November 23, 2026, after serving in the role following predecessor Liz Gutfahr's (Liz Gutfahr) embezzlement scandal. At a special meeting on November 25, the Santa Cruz County Board of Supervisors unanimously accepted Paz's departure, appointed County Manager Jesus Valdez (Jesus Valdez) as interim treasurer, and opened public applications to fill the remainder of the term ending December 31, 2028.

「Why it matters」
The abrupt leadership transition marks the county's second treasurer search in three years as officials work to maintain uninterrupted tax disbursements to local school, fire, and community college districts. Under Arizona Revised Statutes Section 16-230, the Board must appoint a registered Democratic Party member—matching Paz's party affiliation—with interviews scheduled for January 19, 2027, and the new appointee slated to take office January 25.

「Source differences」
Santa Cruz County's official notice is titled "Santa Cruz County names interim treasurer and opens applications to fill vacancy", specifying the November 23 resignation letter, November 25 interim appointment of Jesus Valdez, statutory Democratic Party requirement under A.R.S. Section 16-230, and January 8 application deadline. Arizona Public Media (AZPM) published "Santa Cruz County treasurer resigns, triggering search for replacement", adding that the board plans an external financial review covering August through November following a November 4 CPA presentation confirming properly reported cash balances. Hoodline carried "Santa Cruz County Treasurer Paz Resigns Abruptly", noting complaints over delayed tax distributions to local taxing districts.

「Still uncertain」
Paz's resignation letter offered no explanation for stepping down, and the results of the planned August-to-November financial review have not yet been completed.
