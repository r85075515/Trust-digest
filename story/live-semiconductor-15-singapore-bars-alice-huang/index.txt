The ministry said she left Singapore in February and was investigated over server purchases routed through Singapore to Malaysia; investigators found insufficient evidence that she committed offences, issued her a stern warning, and barred her return. The ministry was explicit about one thing: its investigation did not concern espionage.
What happened
Per the ministry statement carried by Reuters, Alice Huang was investigated in her capacity as Megaspeed's then-CEO, chairperson and a key controller, over suspected involvement in possible offences including buying servers that were exported via Singapore to Malaysia without entering the Singapore market - the same transshipment pattern at the heart of U.S. scrutiny of how restricted Nvidia chips reached China. The probe concluded there was insufficient evidence of offences by her personally, but she was nonetheless warned and excluded. Beijing has previously denied she is a spy; she has not commented. Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia cloud partner, said Thursday it had cooperated with U.S. inspections and takes export-control law seriously.
Why it matters
This is the first hard state action against Alice Huang herself - and it cuts both ways. For Washington's narrative, a host country investigated her company's server flows and decided she should not come back. For her defense, Singapore's own investigators looked and did not find offences, and said espionage was not even the subject. Both readings will be quoted in Washington, where Huang's agreed congressional testimony will now inevitably cover her Singapore chapter: when she left, what the probe asked, and what Nvidia knew. The takeaway: the Alice Huang file is no longer just American suspicion - it is a two-country record, and the two halves do not say the same thing.
Sources agree / disagree

Characterizations diverge elsewhere: U.S. officials are reported to have concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset; Beijing denies it; Singapore's investigation neither confirms nor addresses that claim. Trust label: Official statement, single-country record.
