10:T7a2,Keisei Electric Railway has suspended reservations for its Skyliner express trains after unauthorized access to the seat booking system that may have exposed as many as 3.37 million personal records. The company announced the breach on November 13, saying the system was improperly accessed on November 11, and it has stopped taking online bookings while the incident is handled.

What happened
Keisei Electric Railway said on November 13 that the seat reservation system for the Skyliner, the limited express running between central Tokyo and Narita Airport, was improperly accessed on November 11. As many as 3.37 million personal records may have been exposed, and the company has suspended the reservation service. A second report confirmed the service has stopped online bookings.

Why it matters
A breach affecting up to 3.37 million records is large for a single railway booking system, and the Skyliner is a main route for travelers between Tokyo and Narita Airport. Passengers who booked seats may need to check whether their personal information was involved and watch for official guidance from the railway.

Sources agree / disagree
CNA and the Liberty Times agree on the core facts: the November 11 access, the November 13 announcement, the figure of up to 3.37 million records and the suspension of reservations. One rendering difference appears in the Chinese text: CNA renders the operator as Keisei (京成電鐵) while the Liberty Times renders it as Jingcheng (京城電鐵); both refer to the same Keisei Electric Railway and Skyliner service.

Still uncertain
Neither feed description says what kinds of personal data were involved, whether the records were actually copied, or how long the system will stay closed. The company's statement, as summarized, does not give a cause or a timeline for restoring bookings.
