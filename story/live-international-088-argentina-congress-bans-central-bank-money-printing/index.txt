President Javier Milei's administration framed the legislation as ending decades of deficit monetization after annual inflation dropped from nearly 300 percent in early 2024 to 33 percent last month.

The overhaul codifies currency stability as the central bank's primary mission, aligning institutional rules with Milei's fiscal austerity program even though he fell short of his original campaign pledge to abolish the monetary authority entirely. However, lawmakers softened leadership protections, allowing a president to dismiss central bank governors with a simple Senate majority of 37 votes rather than a two-thirds supermajority.

AP News and Bloomberg both confirmed the 141–0 vote tally, the ban on state financing, and the Senate's modification lowering the threshold for removing central bank officials.

Economists note that long-term credibility will depend on whether future administrations respect the charter amid recent preliminary indicators showing Argentina's economy slipping into recession.
