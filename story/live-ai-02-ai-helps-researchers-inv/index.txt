10:T767,MIT researchers are using artificial intelligence to investigate how thousands of medical studies share the data and code behind their results. The work turns a quiet corner of research integrity, whether methods can actually be checked, into something measurable at scale.

What happened
The data and code underpinning a research study show what was actually done, and how. In a medical study, that means how patients were selected for inclusion, how missing values were handled, and how results were evaluated. MIT researchers are using AI to examine these sharing practices across thousands of medical studies, asking whether the material needed to verify a study is available at all. Access to a study's data and code helps researchers answer those questions, examine the methods, and build on the work rather than starting from scratch.

Why it matters
Published findings are only as trustworthy as the methods behind them, and methods cannot be checked when data and code stay hidden. An AI-assisted audit across thousands of studies gives the field its first large-scale picture of how open medical research really is, and where the gaps sit. That picture is the precondition for fixing reproducibility, not a footnote to it.
