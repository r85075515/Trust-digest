10:T768,Education researchers at a Taipei seminar organized by the Huang Kun-huei Education Foundation urged Taiwan's government to establish strict safety and pedagogical standards for classroom artificial intelligence and recommended barring elementary-school students from independently using commercially available general-purpose AI tools, according to a November 28, 2026, Taipei Times report based on Central News Agency coverage. Scholars argued that schools should only procure AI teaching systems incorporating age-appropriate safeguards and Socratic-style progressive prompts rather than tools that hand students direct answers.

Experts warned that unrestricted reliance on generative AI risks student 'deskilling' and erodes critical thinking, collaborative reasoning, and verification habits. University College London professor Mutlu Cukurova cautioned that AI-generated outputs can undermine learners' confidence and incentive to master core skills, while Deputy Minister of Education Chu Chun-chang compared copying direct AI answers to 'air dropping someone onto the summit' without climbing the mountain—noting that Taiwan's own AI Ark digital learning project focuses on adaptive progress tracking rather than replacing teachers.

Taipei Times (citing CNA) and Taiwan's National Education Radio both document the Huang Kun-huei Education Foundation seminar on K-12 AI transformation and the participation of international scholars from the UK, Japan, and Hong Kong, with no conflicting facts reported.

The recommendations are currently advisory proposals from the policy seminar; the Ministry of Education has not yet issued binding regulatory rules banning general-purpose AI in elementary schools or finalized new procurement mandates.
