10:T809,Amazon and Meta are negotiating an end to Amazon's block on Muse, Meta's AI shopping agent, which Amazon kicked off its platform in September. The talks have stalled over advertising revenue and who controls search results, even though Muse already commands 51% of all agentic shopping orders and Amazon's advertising business generates $68.6 billion a year.

What happened
The Decoder reported that the two companies are in talks to bring Muse back to Amazon's online store. Amazon removed the agent in September, and the unresolved questions are how advertising revenue is shared and who gets to control the search results Muse shows shoppers. A separate BBC hands-on review by the broadcaster's technology editor, published ahead of Muse's UK launch, described a week using the agent as helpful but awkward, and quoted Meta's ambition for Muse to do for AI what WhatsApp did for messaging.

Why it matters
The negotiation decides whether the dominant AI shopping agent can operate inside the largest Western online store. Amazon has to weigh $68.6 billion in annual advertising revenue against the risk that an outside agent controls the search results its shoppers see, while Meta needs Amazon's storefront to turn Muse's 51% share of agentic shopping orders into a lasting habit.

Sources agree and disagree
The two items are different kinds of story bundled together: The Decoder reports the talks, and the BBC reviews the product. They agree on the basics, Muse is Meta's AI shopping agent, Amazon blocked it in September, and Meta is pitching it as a mass-market AI product, but the BBC review offers no independent confirmation of the talks.

Still uncertain
Neither item says how close a deal is, what revenue split is on the table, or when Muse might return to Amazon. The 51% order share and the $68.6 billion figure come from The Decoder's reporting and have not been independently verified here.
