10:T660,Despite persistent questions about overheated artificial-intelligence investment and a possible bubble, international demand for computing power remains strong, the Financial Times reports via Central News Agency. AI server suppliers are even in a position to demand price increases of up to 45% months after signing sales contracts with customers.
What happened
The Financial Times reported on November 22 that compute demand is still strong around the world, according to a Central News Agency dispatch from London by reporter Chen Yun-yu. Suppliers of AI servers have enough leverage to ask customers for price rises of up to 45% even months after the sales contracts were signed.
Why it matters
A 45% post-contract price rise is a blunt measure of who holds the leverage in the AI hardware market. If demand still outruns supply this far into the buildout, the bubble debate misses the near-term point: buyers are paying more, not less, and the cost of AI infrastructure keeps climbing. That feeds directly into the economics of every AI company renting or buying that capacity.
