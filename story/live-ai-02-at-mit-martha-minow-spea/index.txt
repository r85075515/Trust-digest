10:T6c9,Legal scholar Martha Minow delivered this fall's Compton Lecture at MIT on Thursday, examining the civic foundations of democracy and describing how artificial intelligence is undermining it, but could be used to help reinforce traditional US governance. Minow, the 300th Anniversary University Professor at Harvard University, put the moment bluntly: it is not a good time for democracy or constitutionalism.
What happened
The lecture's argument runs in two directions at once. AI is eroding the civic conditions democracy depends on, from shared facts to public trust, and yet the same technology could be turned toward reinforcing traditional governance, if institutions learn to use it rather than merely submit to it. Minow's framing refuses both panic and complacency: the danger is real, and so is the agency.
Why it matters
Compton Lectures are meant to set a public agenda, and this one names the central political question of the AI era: who governs the tools that now mediate public life? If universities and courts treat AI as a force of nature, democratic self-government shrinks by default. Minow's answer is that democracy must be sustained deliberately, by using AI and by overcoming it.
