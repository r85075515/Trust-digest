10:T6db,發生了什麼事
Paleontologists led by Richard Howard at London's Natural History Museum published findings in the journal Palaeontology resolving a more than 150-year-old taxonomic debate over Praearcturus gigas, an Early Devonian arthropod that lived around 415 million years ago before Earth's first forests appeared. Using computed tomography (CT) scanning on fossils recovered from England and Wales and comparing them with the well-preserved Silurian scorpion Eramoscorpius brucensis from Canada, the researchers confirmed that Praearcturus gigas was a true scorpion rather than an isopod crustacean or sea scorpion. Measuring over one meter (more than three feet) in length with 16-centimeter pincers—more than four times longer than those of today's largest living scorpion—it ranks as the largest scorpion ever discovered.

為什麼重要
Confirming Praearcturus gigas as a true scorpion reshapes understanding of early terrestrial ecosystems, demonstrating that giant arachnid predators were already hunting along ancient shorelines and early land habitats during the Devonian Period.

來源異同
ZME Science and Around Prague both accurately summarize the Natural History Museum study in Palaeontology, the 150-year misidentification history, the 16-centimeter pincers, and the over-one-meter body length estimate.

仍不確定
Whether Praearcturus gigas hunted primarily in shallow water or fully on dry land, and whether younger fossil fragments from Somerset prove the giant lineage survived for another 40 million years, require more complete specimens.
