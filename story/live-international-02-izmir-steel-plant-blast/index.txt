10:T887,A huge explosion and fire at the HABAŞ iron and steel plant in Aliağa, in Turkey's western İzmir province, left at least 10 people dead on Monday as rescuers searched for four missing workers, the Associated Press reports.
What happened
The blast occurred around noon local time (0900 GMT) during the casting of liquid steel. The governor's office said an overhead crane toppled into an electric arc furnace; the company said the accident happened during planned maintenance on one of its furnaces and that causes were being examined with the authorities. The explosion occurred during a shift change, with about 200 workers at the plant. The governor's office initially reported three dead, 14 injured and 10 missing; Governor Süleyman Elban later confirmed in a televised update that 10 had died and 15 were injured, at least two of them critically, with four people still believed to be under the wreckage. İzmir Mayor Cemil Tugay said the exact toll would only be known once debris removal is complete. Four prosecutors have been assigned and a workplace-safety investigation launched. The plant, established in 1987, produces about 4.5 million tonnes of steel a year.
Why it matters
This is a mass-casualty industrial disaster at one of Turkey's major steelworks, and the context is damning: a labour-rights monitor says two other workers died in accidents at the same plant within the past two weeks. Whether the investigation confines itself to the crane failure or widens to the plant's safety record will decide whether this is treated as an accident or as negligence with a paper trail.
Sources agree / disagree
The AP, Reuters and AFP reporting agree on the toll progression, the crane-into-furnace account and the four missing; sources differ slightly on how many injured are critical (two to four). Trust label: Multi-source agreement — wire services align on the core facts, with casualty figures per the İzmir governor's office.
Still uncertain
