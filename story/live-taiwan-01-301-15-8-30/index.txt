10:T937,The United States has released its structural overcapacity Section 301 investigation report, recommending a 15% non-stacking tariff on Taiwan, the lowest tier. Executive Yuan Vice Premier Cheng Li-chun will explain the report at an 8:30 a.m. briefing with finance chiefs.

What happened
Central News Agency (CNA) reported that the U.S. published the Section 301 report on structural overcapacity, with a recommended 15% non-stacking rate for Taiwan, the lowest tier. The Executive Yuan scheduled the briefing for 8:30 a.m. on November 26, led by Cheng with finance chiefs explaining the findings and the response. Cheng told CNA that the U.S. is also running a Section 232 investigation alongside the Section 301 probe. Non-semiconductor Section 232 tariff relief is already in effect, covering auto parts, wood and wood products, and drones at 15% non-stacking. The semiconductor rate has not been released, but Taiwan has secured zero tariffs within quota, and 15% non-stacking or the lower applicable rate beyond quota.

Why it matters
Taipei landed the lightest proposed rate, and that matters more than the headline number. The 15% non-stacking terms on autos, wood and drones are already locked in, and the quota deal on chips — zero inside, capped outside — protects Taiwan's most important export before its rate is even set. The open question is not whether Taiwan was hit hardest; it was not. It is what the final semiconductor rate looks like once Washington sets it.

Sources agree / disagree
Only one outlet, CNA, reports this story, across two items that agree with each other: one on the Section 232 relief already in effect, one on the Section 301 report and the morning briefing. No second outlet corroborates the 15% figure in this digest. The ingest flag of '232 vs 8' is not a conflict: 232 is the investigation section number, and 8 is the 8:30 a.m. briefing time.

Still uncertain
The full Section 301 report text is not in the summaries, and the quota size for semiconductors is unstated. The semiconductor tariff rate is still pending, and the briefing itself had not yet happened when the feed items were published, so Cheng's full remarks are not yet on record.
