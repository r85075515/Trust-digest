10:T724,Pope Leo XIV has delivered a stinging critique of Argentine President Javier Milei's harsh austerity measures, telling a Buenos Aires audience that the economy must be at the service of the people, rather than people being at the service of the economy. The speech, met with rapturous applause, came on the second stop of his 12-day Latin America tour.
What happened
Leo arrived in Argentina after a successful trip to Uruguay, where tens of thousands packed the main square in Florida on Sunday to hear him speak. In Buenos Aires he told Milei's government that growth must benefit all, a direct challenge to the president's austerity programme. The visit is by far the most politically delicate of his three-nation South American tour, and a place where Leo also confronts the complicated legacy of Argentine Pope Francis, who never returned home during his 12-year pontificate.
Why it matters
A pope publicly rebuking a sitting president's economic programme, in the president's own capital, is rare and deliberate. Milei's austerity is the central fight in Argentine politics, and Leo is staking a moral frame for it: growth that does not reach ordinary people is not success. The speech will be quoted back at Milei for as long as the programme bites.
Sources agree / disagree
BBC World, The Guardian, PBS NewsHour, and NPR News agree on the speech and its message, though they sharpen it differently: the BBC calls it a veiled rebuke, The Guardian a stinging rebuke, and PBS stresses that people, not the economy, should come first. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — four outlets agree on the speech and its message.
