10:T5b5,Generative AI has changed enterprise analytics in just a few years, MIT Technology Review reports, mainly by making analysis accessible to users across an organisation rather than only specialists.

What happened
Agentic AI promises to go further, automating entire workflows and taking actions based on agent insights. The problem, the article argues, is that the quality of insights agents currently generate leaves much to be desired. Fewer than half of agent-generated insights clear the quality bar described in the material provided, which means a large share of machine-produced analysis cannot yet be trusted on its own.

Why it matters
The gap matters because automation multiplies whatever it touches. A flawed dashboard misleads one team; an agent acting on flawed insights can mislead a whole workflow, at machine speed, before anyone notices. Firms that treat agent output as finished analysis, rather than a draft to check, are buying speed at the price of accuracy.

Sources agree-disagree
This is a single-outlet article, so the material provided offers no second account to compare against, and no conflicting figure or name appears in it.

Still uncertain
The material provided does not spell out how insight quality was measured, which agents or vendors were tested, or what the failing insights got wrong. Without that method and those examples, readers cannot tell whether the problem is narrow or industry-wide.
