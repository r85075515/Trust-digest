10:T5dc,Over a third of Gen Z-ers rely on AI for friendship, a new Gallup report finds, and those who lean on AI companions face higher odds of loneliness.
What happened
Young people who have online-only friends or use AI as a social companion face higher odds of loneliness than those who do not, the Gallup report reveals. The headline finding is that more than a third of Gen Z-ers rely on AI for friendship. The report, covered by Wired, ties AI companionship to loneliness rather than presenting it as a cure for it.
Why it matters
AI companions are moving from novelty to daily social infrastructure for young people, so evidence that heavy reliance tracks with loneliness changes the stakes for parents, schools and the companies building these tools. If companionship deepens isolation, design and guidance matter as much as access. Takeaway: the question is no longer whether young people befriend AI, but what that friendship costs them.
Sources agree / disagree
Only Wired has reported the Gallup findings so far, so the "over a third" figure and the loneliness link rest on a single outlet's account of the report. No second source confirms the numbers yet.
Still uncertain
The feed description gives no sample size, no exact share beyond "over a third," and no figures for how much higher the odds of loneliness run. The full Gallup report is needed to judge the strength of the link.
