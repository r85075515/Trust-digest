10:T899,「What happened」
Researchers led by postdoctoral fellow Natan Feter (Natan Feter) in the Evolutionary Biology of Physical Activity Lab at the University of Southern California analyzed 17 eligible studies screened from 6,270 records in a systematic review and meta-analysis published in The Lancet Public Health. The analysis found that recreational exercise was associated with a 24% lower risk of dementia before benefits leveled off, while physically demanding occupational work showed the opposite pattern, correlating with a 20% higher dementia risk.

「Why it matters」
Public health messaging has long treated all physical movement as equally beneficial for brain health. The findings highlight a physical activity paradox in cognitive aging, indicating that leisure time, safe recreational spaces, and workplace protections may be critical alongside encouraging exercise.

「Source differences」
ScienceDaily reported the USC release on November 27, 2026 under the headline "One type of physical activity is linked to a 20% higher dementia risk", foregrounding the 24% lower risk from recreational exercise versus the 20% higher risk from physically demanding work. MedPage Today covered the study under "Most Physical Activity Is Tied to Lower Dementia Risk, but This Type May Raise It", summarizing the accompanying editorial by University College London researchers Mika Kivimäki (Mika Kivimäki) and Mark Hamer (Mark Hamer) on socioeconomic confounders. PubMed indexed the peer-reviewed paper under "Domain-specific physical activity levels and risk of dementia: a systematic review and meta-analysis", confirming inclusion of 17 studies across 6,270 screened records.

「Still uncertain」
Because the underlying observational studies rely largely on self-reported activity and occupational physical labor correlates closely with socioeconomic status, fatigue, and workplace exposures, whether physical labor directly causes higher dementia risk requires longitudinal studies using device-based measurements.
