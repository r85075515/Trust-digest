10:Tcb7,Two chain-reaction pile-ups tore through Bulgaria's Trakia motorway on the morning of Nov 10 after dense fog rolled in near Yambol: 86 vehicles were involved across both carriageways and at least 11 people were killed, the state news agency BTA and broadcaster BNT reported; the government declared Nov 11 a day of national mourning.
What happened
The first emergency call came at 7:10 a.m. The crashes happened around kilometres 289–294 near the Zimnitsa interchange on the A1 Trakia motorway, which links the capital Sofia with the Black Sea port of Burgas. Acting Interior Ministry secretary general Lyubomir Nikolov said the first pile-up, on the Burgas-bound carriageway, began when a lorry blocked the path of a passenger bus; a second chain collision followed on the Sofia-bound side. Deputy Interior Minister Kaloyan Kaloyanov put the final vehicle count at 86 — 63 toward Burgas, 23 toward Sofia — with wreckage, including cars, vans, buses, trucks and tankers, strewn over about a kilometre. No children were among the dead or injured. The motorway remained closed in both directions into the evening, and Yambol regional governor Georgi Chalakov declared a partial state of emergency in the municipalities of Straldzha, Tundzha and Yambol.
Why it matters
Bulgaria records the European Union's highest road death rate — 71 deaths per million inhabitants in 2025, according to European Commission figures. This crash detonated that standing problem in a single morning: fast-forming fog, a wet surface and unsuited speeds combined on the country's main east–west artery to produce a near-hundred-vehicle pile-up. Prime Minister Rumen Radev fronted the response personally and the cabinet declared national mourning; the two pre-trial investigations will now test whether responsibility extends beyond the weather — to fog warnings and speed management. The takeaway: the fog was the trigger, but the EU's worst road-safety record is the background.
Sources agree / disagree
The casualty figures moved during the day and must be read in parallel: Health Minister Katya Ivkova reported 12 dead during the day (10 at the scene, two in transport) and 46 injured; Prime Minister Rumen Radev and the evening BTA/BNT official line put the toll at 11 dead (10 at the scene, one in Yambol hospital), with injuries reported between 48 and 51. This card follows the latest official line — at least 11 dead — and the figures may still change. The vehicle count was likewise revised from an early estimate of about 50 to the Interior Ministry's 86. On the cause — dense fog, visibility collapsing from 70–80 metres to about 10, a wet surface and unsuitable speeds — all sources agree, with officials stressing that nothing is conclusively established. Trust label: Multi-source agreement; casualty figures still being revised — latest official figures govern.
Still uncertain
The final toll is not settled and could rise. The exact collision sequences and any liability await the two pre-trial investigations, expected to be handled by the National Investigation Service.
