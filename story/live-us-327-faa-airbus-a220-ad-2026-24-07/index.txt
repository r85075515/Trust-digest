10:T675,What happened
On November 27, 2026, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) published final Airworthiness Directive (AD) 2026-24-07 (Docket No. FAA-2026-15285; Amendment 39-23531; Document No. 2026-24137) in the Federal Register for all Airbus Canada Limited Partnership Model BD-500-1A10 and BD-500-1A11 airplanes (the Airbus A220 family). The directive takes effect on December 14, 2026, with public comments accepted through January 11, 2027.

Why it matters
The FAA issued the directive to address an unsafe condition that could cause loss of thrust and possible dual engine shutdown during critical phases of flight. The rule affects an estimated 228 airplanes on the U.S. registry, with initial compliance costs estimated at 85 USD per airplane (1 work-hour) or 19,380 USD across the U.S. fleet.

Source differences
The FAA's official Federal Register filing ("Airworthiness Directives; Airbus Canada Limited Partnership (Type") and GovInfo's full November 27, 2026 Federal Register issue ("Federal Register, Friday, November 27, 2026 - GovInfo") provide the verbatim regulatory text, docket identifier FAA-2026-15285, effective date of December 14, 2026, and fleet compliance cost calculation of 19,380 USD across 228 U.S.-registered aircraft.

Still uncertain
Whether public comments submitted before the January 11, 2027 deadline will prompt subsequent revisions to the directive's compliance scope or recurring inspection intervals remains to be seen.
