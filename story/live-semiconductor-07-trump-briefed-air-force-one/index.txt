10:T106e,President Trump's account of when he learned about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy has been directly contradicted by reporting. Trump said Monday (Nov 9) that he had not heard about the relationship when he travelled to Beijing last spring. The New York Times reported Tuesday that Secretary of State Marco Rubio briefed him on exactly that intelligence aboard Air Force One in May - before Huang boarded - and CNN independently confirmed the briefing with its own source. The White House denies the reporting.
What happened
Huang was not originally on the list of business leaders invited on Trump's May trip to China. Trump belatedly invited him to join the delegation when Air Force One stopped to refuel in Anchorage, Alaska. According to the Times, when Rubio learned Huang would board, he briefed the president orally on the sensitive intelligence: US officials had concluded that Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive with whom Jensen Huang was in a relationship, worked for Chinese intelligence. Trump kept the arrangement; Huang boarded, met Trump only briefly and spent most of the flight in a separate cabin area. The Times says this was at least the second time in seven months Trump had been told: he first received a briefing in late autumn 2025 and took no action, and in spring 2026 Rubio oversaw a warning memo circulated among a small group of senior officials. On Monday, asked directly whether he had known during the Beijing trip, Trump answered: 'No, I hadn't heard about that at the time,' said he had learned of it 'not too long ago', and said he would have to check whether he was briefed last year. He added that Huang will not join next week's return trip to China. A White House official responded to CNN: 'These sources have no idea what they're talking about.'
Why it matters
The contradiction moves the story from a CEO's private life to the president's candor and judgement: on the reported account, Trump was warned by his own secretary of state, in person, hours before bringing the CEO of America's most sensitive AI-chip company into a summit with Xi Jinping - and later said on camera that he had not heard about it. It also reframes the week: Senators Schumer and Gallego are already demanding to know why Trump defended Huang despite earlier warnings, and the board's independent probe and the Nov 18 closed-door House briefing now sit next to a question about the president himself. There is a Taiwan angle as well: Hong Kong outlet Pulse, citing the Times' reporting, says Alice Huang repeatedly entered Taiwan on a Hong Kong passport and that Taiwan's authorities had monitored her visits since 2024 at the US government's suggestion; Interior Minister Liu Shyh-fang said on Wednesday her entry permit has been revoked and she is barred from returning. The takeaway: the scandal's center of gravity has shifted from Huang's conduct to what the president knew, and when.
Sources agree / disagree
This is a direct conflict, presented side by side without taking sides. Trump, on the record Monday: he had not heard about the relationship at the time of the Beijing trip. The New York Times, citing people familiar with the matter: Rubio briefed him aboard Air Force One before Huang boarded in Anchorage, the second briefing in seven months. CNN, citing its own source familiar with the matter: the briefing happened as the Times described. The White House: the reporting is wrong and the sources 'have no idea what they're talking about'; the State Department declined to comment. All sides agree on the undisputed scaffolding - Huang joined the May trip late, at Anchorage, and Trump publicly defended him on Monday. Trust label: Conflicting accounts - an on-record presidential denial versus two outlets' independently sourced reporting; officially disputed and unresolved.
Still uncertain
No documentary evidence (the spring memo itself, passenger records or briefing logs) has been made public, and the Times' sources are anonymous. Whether the memo influenced the original delegation list is unclear.
