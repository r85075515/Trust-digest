10:T793,「What happened」
Japan's Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) announced that hackers breached a server supporting its Government Procurement Information System, which publishes tender and contract opportunities for eligible bidders. An external specialist investigation found that up to 163,953 records listing business and representative names and up to 1,778 email addresses—potentially including personal staff addresses—may have been exposed.

「Why it matters」
The breach adds to a broader wave of cyber incidents hitting Japanese public institutions and research infrastructure in late 2026, including an earlier intrusion into systems operating RIKEN's Fugaku supercomputer. Authorities have isolated the procurement server from outside communications and reported the incident to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission while investigating root causes.

「Source differences」
The Record reported under "Japan's education ministry discloses breach of government procurement system", detailing the compromised server's role in managing eligible procurement bidders and situating the breach within recent Japanese cybersecurity incidents. MLQ.ai published "Japan's education ministry says hackers hit its procurement system", while BigGo Finance reported "Japan's Ministry of Education procurement system hit by unauthorized access; up to 163,953 vendor records may have leaked", breaking down the exact record counts and the notification to the Personal Information Protection Commission.

「Still uncertain」
Whether the exposed vendor records or email addresses have been published online or misused for phishing or fraud has not been confirmed, and the final scope of leaked data remains under investigation.
