10:T76b,「What happened」
South Korea's Korea Power Exchange (KPX) selected preferred bidders in the country's largest-ever energy storage system (ESS) central contract market tender, valued at roughly 2 trillion won. Samsung SDI secured 751 megawatts—nearly 63 percent of the offered grid storage capacity—while rival battery maker SK On won the remaining 443 megawatts, accounting for roughly 37 percent, after LG Energy Solution opted out of the third round.

「Why it matters」
The central contract market awards long-term capacity contracts to stabilize South Korea's power grid as renewable energy generation expands. Samsung SDI's dominant share highlights a rebound in domestic utility-scale storage orders amid slowing electric vehicle battery demand, while final contract results are scheduled to be confirmed on December 4 ahead of a fourth bidding round planned for the first half of 2027.

「Source differences」
Korea JoongAng Daily published "Samsung SDI takes nearly two-thirds of record Korean ESS tender" (with subheadline "Samsung SDI wins 63% of South Korea's record ESS tender"), detailing the 751-megawatt award, KPX's role, and the December 4 finalization date. Korean industry outlet TheElec reported under "Samsung SDI Wins Majority of 2 Trillion Won ESS Tender", specifying the 2 trillion won tender scale alongside Samsung SDI's 751 megawatts and SK On's 443 megawatts, while Edaily ran "Record-breaking '2 trillion won' ESS tender… SAMSUNG SDI CO.,LTD. wins 63% of contracts, SK On 37%".

「Still uncertain」
Final contractual awards are pending formal confirmation on December 4, and exact project commissioning schedules and financial margins for each winning site have not been disclosed.
