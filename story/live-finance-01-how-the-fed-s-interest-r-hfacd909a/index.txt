10:T64c,The US Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged on Wednesday at its last meeting before the midterm elections, and Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role in pausing rate increases even though inflation remained above 3 percent.
What happened
Warsh spoke at a news conference, according to CNBC Economy and NYT Economy headlines summarising takeaways from it. The Fed kept rates at one of the highest pre-election levels in more than 20 years. Two of the 12 board members voted against the pause and preferred to raise rates. The Fed left its benchmark rate untouched, but the summary said a December increase could make holiday borrowing more costly. An NYT Economy headline also addressed credit cards and loans, but stated no specific effects.
Why it matters
The judgment is direct: a pre-election pause with inflation above 3 percent, two dissenters favouring higher rates, and a possible December increase leaves the decision unsettled for borrowers. The dissent shows the vote was not unanimous, and the holiday-borrowing warning links the decision to household credit costs.
