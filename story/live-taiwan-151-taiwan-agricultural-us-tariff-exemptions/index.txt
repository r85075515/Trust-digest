10:T607,Taiwan's Ministry of Agriculture confirmed on November 26 that 322 Taiwanese agricultural items remain exempt from additional US tariffs following the US Trade Representative Office's release of a Section 301 report on structural excess capacity across 16 economies. While the report proposed a 15 percent tariff cap on Taiwan without stacking Most Favored Nation (MFN) rates, the current tariff on Taiwanese farm exports to the United States remains at 10 percent unstacked.

Minister of Agriculture Chen Junne-jih stated at a post-Cabinet press conference that the 322 exempt items account for 36 to 40 percent of Taiwan's total agricultural export value to the United States. Twenty-six of those items carry exclusive exemptions for Taiwan, including moth orchids, flamingo flowers, and pet frogs, allowing moth orchids to enter the US duty-free while Dutch competitors face a 10 percent tariff.

Chen noted that Taiwan also retains significant rate advantages over China on edamame and tilapia, where Taiwan faces a 10 percent duty compared with 41.3 percent and 37.5 percent for China, respectively. Even if the proposed 15 percent cap takes effect, rates on Chinese edamame and tilapia would rise to 48.8 percent and 45 percent, preserving Taiwan's relative margin while the ministry prepares an NT$18 billion special support budget.
