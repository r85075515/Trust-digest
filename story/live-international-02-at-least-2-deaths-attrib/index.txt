10:T69f,Hurricane Isaias slammed into the US late Friday as a major storm, ripping roofs off buildings in Florida and Alabama before weakening to a post-tropical cyclone. The death toll is still in dispute: NPR's headline attributes at least 2 deaths to the storm, while BBC World reports four dead and thousands without power, and the Guardian's live coverage says at least four dead with hundreds of thousands without power.
What happened
Isaias became the first Atlantic hurricane of the 2026 season, strengthening to Category 3 with winds up to 120 mph before its predicted landfall along the Florida Panhandle late Friday, per PBS NewsHour and the Guardian. The National Hurricane Center's Michael Brennan briefed on the storm's approach. Grist notes the timing is "astounding": the season began in June, and this is the latest first Atlantic hurricane of the satellite era.
Why it matters
The judgment is twofold: the death-toll split is a live casualty count still moving, so treat any single number as provisional. The bigger story is the record-late first hurricane — a season that waited until October for its first Atlantic system is the kind of anomaly that rewrites seasonal forecasting assumptions.
Sources agree / disagree
BBC World, the Guardian, PBS NewsHour, Grist, and NPR agree on the storm's track, strength, and record-late timing. They genuinely disagree on fatalities: NPR says at least 2, the BBC and Guardian say 4. Trust label: Accounts differ — real disagreement on the death toll; everything else is consistent.
