10:Tef0,Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia's president, chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately, the company announced Thursday morning (Nov 12) - ending, in five days, a 33-year reign that made him the face of the AI era. CFO Colette Kress takes over as interim CEO while keeping her finance role; the board has begun a search for a permanent successor, considering internal and external candidates, and appointed former Synopsys CEO Harvey C. Jones as Nvidia's first-ever board chair to oversee it. The announcement pointedly did not mention the affairs scandal that felled Huang, thanking him instead for 33 years of contributions. Nvidia shares fell more than 10% in premarket trading, to about $155.
What happened
The end came fast. Huang admitted an extramarital affair in a letter to staff on Sunday Nov 8; the board publicly backed him that day but opened an independent investigation, later hiring Wachtell Lipton to run it. On Wednesday Nov 11, The New York Times reported a second affair - with Kimberly Powell, a healthcare vice president who reported directly to Huang - and The Wall Street Journal reported the board was preparing to remove him; the board met Wednesday evening to consider his ouster, according to a person familiar with the matter cited by Reuters. Thursday at 8:48 a.m. ET, Nvidia made it official. Jones said the board has 'full confidence in Colette and the leadership team' and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption. Third-quarter results stay on schedule for Tuesday Nov 17. Huang, 63, co-founded Nvidia in 1993, remains its fourth-largest shareholder with about 3.6%, and famously kept no succession plan, saying he intended to lead for decades more.
Why it matters
Nvidia is the load-bearing company of the AI buildout, and it has just lost the only CEO it has ever had - a founder whose personal diplomacy with presidents, kings and supply chains was itself a strategic asset - five days after a scandal broke and five days before an earnings report. The interim answer is deliberately boring: Kress, CFO since 2013, is the executive investors know best after Huang, and installing a first-ever board chair signals the directors intend to govern, not just mourn. What cannot be replaced by process is Huang himself: roughly 60 direct reports, no groomed successor, and a product-and-geopolitics strategy that lived in one man's head. The takeaway: the resignation closes the scandal's first act, but the real story now is whether anyone can run Nvidia the way its founder did - the market's 10% verdict says it doubts it, at least today.
Sources agree / disagree
The official announcement (GlobeNewswire), Reuters, WSJ, Barron's, CNBC and Le Monde agree on all core facts: resignation effective immediately, Kress interim, Jones first chair, search under way, earnings unchanged. Premarket decline figures vary by snapshot - about 8% in early reports, more than 10% (to about $155.38) in later ones. Whether Huang jumped or was pushed is framed differently: the company says 'resigned'; Reuters and WSJ reporting on Wednesday's board meeting makes the sequence read as an ouster completed by resignation. On X, Elon Musk replied 'That would be an honor' to a suggestion Tesla hire Huang - color, not a plan. Trust label: Officially announced; multi-source agreement.
Still uncertain
Who the permanent CEO will be and how long the search takes; what happens to the Wachtell investigation and the Huang Le (Alice Huang) espionage questions now that Huang is out; whether the House's Nov 18 closed briefing and subpoena threats proceed against a former CEO; Huang's severance terms, which the announcement did not disclose; and whether he takes any advisory role.
