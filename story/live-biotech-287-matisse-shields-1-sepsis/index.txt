10:T96f,What happened
Dutch clinical-stage biotech Matisse Pharmaceuticals B.V. announced that the first patient has received treatment in its global Phase 2 SHIELDS-1 trial evaluating intravenous isupartob sodium on top of standard of care for intensive care unit (ICU) patients diagnosed with sepsis. The randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-finding study is being conducted across eight hospitals in Thailand. Part one tests three isupartob dose regimens against placebo in 80 participants receiving intravenous infusions for up to 120 hours, while part two will enroll 140 participants assigned to a single active dose or placebo.

Why it matters
Sepsis affects roughly 49 million people globally each year and kills more than 20% of patients, yet no targeted therapies have been approved by regulatory authorities to halt the dysregulated host response driving organ failure. Isupartob is a highly negatively charged molecule engineered to bind and neutralize positively charged extracellular histones released into the bloodstream during severe immune activation. By interrupting this upstream cytotoxic cascade before widespread endothelial injury occurs, the therapy aims to reduce ICU mortality and improve patient recovery.

Source differences
Clinical Trials Arena published the article "First patient receives isupartob in Matisse's Phase II sepsis trial", summarizing the two-part dose-finding design, the eight hospital sites in Thailand, and the histone-neutralizing mechanism. Matisse Pharmaceuticals published the press release "Matisse Pharmaceuticals announces first patient dosed in phase 2 sepsis trial (SHIELDS-1)", providing additional epidemiological figures from the World Health Organization and details on the 120-hour intravenous infusion schedule and two-stage enrollment targets of 80 and 140 patients.

Still uncertain
Because SHIELDS-1 has just dosed its first participant, interim safety, pharmacodynamic, and efficacy readouts from part one remain pending. Whether neutralizing circulating histones translates into a statistically significant survival benefit over standard critical care will only be determined after both parts of the trial complete enrollment and undergo unblinded analysis.
