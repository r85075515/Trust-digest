What happened
The demonstration took place across Italy on October 30, with the Rome march drawing more than 10,000 students and residents, according to CNA correspondent Huang Ya-shih. Many participants were classmates or small groups of friends. They told CNA they had joined of their own accord and had not been mobilised by an organisation. Why it matters
The verdict is stark: the protest was not framed by participants as a narrow student complaint, but as a claim that education, work, and housing no longer connect into a workable future. Spontaneous, peer-based turnout puts pressure on the government to answer directly rather than dismiss the march as a one-day disruption.
