10:T92f,What happened
On November 25, 2026, the Supreme Court of Virginia issued a unanimous 7-0 opinion authored by Justice Wesley G. Russell, Jr. (Wesley G. Russell, Jr.) in Ginevan v. Commonwealth (Record No. 250051), reversing the Court of Appeals of Virginia and vacating defendant Derek Wade Ginevan's (Derek Wade Ginevan) conviction under Virginia Code § 18.2-308.2 for possessing a firearm after a prior felony conviction. The court concluded that felons are not categorically excluded from "the people" protected by the Second Amendment and held that the Commonwealth failed to demonstrate that permanently disarming Ginevan aligned with the nation's historical tradition of firearm regulation.

Why it matters
Although the ruling is narrow and applies specifically to Ginevan's record, it opens the door for other individuals in Virginia whose prior felony convictions lacked actual violence or present dangerousness to mount as-applied Second Amendment challenges against lifetime state firearm bans. At the same time, federal law continues to prohibit convicted felons from purchasing firearms.

Source differences
The official Supreme Court of Virginia opinion—titled "Opinion of the Supreme Court of Virginia"—sets forth the full constitutional analysis under Code § 18.2-308.2 and reverses the lower appellate ruling. Courthouse News Service reported the decision under "Virginia Supreme Court rules state unconstitutionally disarmed nonviolent felon", adding legal commentary on habeas petitions and noting that federal firearm purchase prohibitions remain intact. The Free Lance News covered the ruling under "FELONS HAVE RIGHT TO GUNS IF THEY'RE NO LONGER DANGEROUS, US COURTS BEGIN FINDING", detailing the 2023 domestic disturbance shotgun arrest and comparing the decision with federal Third Circuit rulings.

Still uncertain
How Virginia trial courts will apply the "historical tradition" and dangerousness standard to future as-applied challenges by defendants with varied felony records, and whether federal prosecutors will pursue parallel federal charges in similar cases, remains uncertain.
