10:T634,What happened
The Maricopa County Air Quality Department (MCAQD) declared Friday, November 27, 2026, a No Burn Day across the Phoenix metropolitan area due to elevated levels of PM2.5 fine particulate smoke pollution. Under Maricopa County Ordinance P-26, residents and businesses are prohibited from burning wood or solid fuels in indoor fireplaces, woodstoves, chimineas, and outdoor fire pits throughout the restriction period.

Why it matters
Cooler late-November evening temperatures and holiday gatherings frequently spur residential wood burning across the Valley, trapping fine particulate smoke near the ground under calm atmospheric conditions. Enforcing No Burn Days helps prevent PM2.5 concentrations from breaching federal health thresholds that aggravate asthma and cardiovascular conditions.

Source differences
Daily Independent ("Maricopa County imposes no-burn day") reports the MCAQD declaration for Friday, November 27, alongside Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) guidance urging residents to eliminate wood burning, switch to natural gas or propane, and avoid leaf blowers. ViviNavi Maricopa County Bulletin ("PM2.5 (smoke) No Burn Day issued for Friday, Nov. 27") specifies PM2.5 smoke as the triggering pollutant.

Still uncertain
Whether stagnant weekend weather patterns will prompt MCAQD to extend the No Burn Day restriction into Saturday, November 28, depends on daily air quality forecast updates.
