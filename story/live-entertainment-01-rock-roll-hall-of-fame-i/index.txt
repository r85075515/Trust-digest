What happened
The ground stop halted departures nationwide for hours. At Chicago O'Hare International Airport, an information board showed crowds of waiting passengers. The transportation secretary blamed the disruption on an old and malfunctioning air traffic control system. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told a press briefing, held with Transportation Secretary Duffy, that there was "zero evidence" of a cyberattack. Reports also noted that fewer flights amid rising fuel costs could complicate rebooking for stranded passengers. Aviation correspondent Miles O'Brien reported on the standstill for PBS NewsHour.

Why it matters
A nationwide ground stop is rare, and its effects ripple through every airport in the country. The episode exposed the fragility of the air traffic control system and left travelers facing cancellations and long waits, with recovery expected to take days. That the induction ceremony proceeded as planned shows how organizers pushed on even as the travel system around them seized up.
