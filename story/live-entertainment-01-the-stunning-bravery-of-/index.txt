10:Tad9,Days after comedian Russell Brand was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison for raping a 21-year-old woman and sexually assaulting three other women, his wife read out his message claiming he was “not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for” — and the message itself has now become a second story. The Ministry of Justice is investigating whether Brand broke prison rules, Downing Street has called the message “appalling,” and the government says it will consider staggered ages for sexual consent.
What happened
Brand, 51, was sentenced to 10 years and six months for the rape of a 21-year-old woman and sexual assaults against three other women, Refinery29 reports. Days after the conviction, his wife Laura Brand read a message from him on their podcast and YouTube channel in which he maintained his innocence. The BBC reports the Prime Minister's spokesperson called the message, read out by a convicted rapist's wife, “clearly appalling,” and the Ministry of Justice has launched an investigation into whether Brand broke prison rules by sharing it. CBS News reports the government will consider adopting staggered ages for sexual consent in the wake of what it called “appalling” stories of “abuse of power by powerful men.” The Guardian's coverage carries the same investigation. A Refinery29 essay frames the moment from the survivors' side — “shame has changed sides” and “some justice has been served.”
Why it matters
The sentence closed the trial, but the message reopened the public argument: whether a convicted offender should be able to broadcast claims of innocence from prison, and what that does to the people he was convicted of assaulting. The consent-age review is the policy consequence — the government is using the case to revisit whether one age of consent fits every situation. The takeaway: the legal outcome is settled, but the rules around prison communication and consent law are now in play.
Sources agree / disagree
The BBC, CBS News, The Guardian and Refinery29 agree on the sentence, the message, the investigation and the consent-age review. They differ only in emphasis: the BBC and The Guardian lead on the investigation, CBS on the law review, Refinery29 on the survivors. The sentencing itself was already reported on November 10; this card is the follow-up.
