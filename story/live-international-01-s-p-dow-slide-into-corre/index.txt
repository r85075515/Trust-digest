10:T7b6,S&P 500 and Dow Slide Into Correction as Tech Sell-Off Deepens

The S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slid into correction territory on Friday, dragged down by a broad sell-off in technology stocks that left no corner of the market untouched.
What happened
Chipmakers led the plunge on Friday, according to Bloomberg, with the S&P 500 and the Dow closing in correction territory. MarketWatch reported the same picture: the Dow and the S&P 500 entered correction while the Nasdaq suffered its worst week since 2020. The Nasdaq fell 5.2% on Friday, Bloomberg reported, and NPR put the S&P 500's drop at 3.7%, adding that the Nasdaq composite has now gone negative for the year. The rout was unusually broad: all 11 S&P 500 sectors finished more than 1% lower this week, the first time that has happened since April 2025.
Why it matters
The breadth is the warning sign. A correction driven by a single sector can reverse quickly; a sell-off that hits all 11 sectors at once says investors are repricing risk itself, not just one trade. AI stocks, which powered Wall Street's gains this year, are now leading the slide — their heavy market weight means their fall drags the whole market down with them.
Sources agree / disagree
MarketWatch, Bloomberg and NPR agree on the core facts: the S&P 500 and the Dow are in correction, tech led the fall, and the Nasdaq had its worst week since 2020. The two headline percentages describe different indexes, not a conflict: the Nasdaq's 5.2% Friday fall (Bloomberg) and the S&P 500's 3.7% drop (NPR) are separate figures. Trust label: Multi-source agreement — three outlets describe the same correction, the same tech-led sell-off, and the same worst-week-since-2020 Nasdaq.
