10:T7e7,What happened
A research team led by Wendy K. Jo and Jan Felix Drexler at Charité – Universitätsmedizin Berlin and the German Center for Infection Research (DZIF), alongside Thomas Pietschmann and Sibylle Haid at TWINCORE, published findings in Science Advances characterizing a bat orthopneumovirus distantly related to human respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Analyzing 1,778 samples from 18 bat species in Ghana and Benin, the scientists found active virus in 1.4% of bats—primarily straw-colored fruit bats—and antibodies in 9%, while laboratory assays showed the virus can enter human cells less efficiently than human RSV.

Why it matters
Evaluating wildlife reservoirs before spillover occurs is crucial for pandemic preparedness. Crucially, the team demonstrated that antibodies from people previously exposed to human RSV, as well as a broadly acting monoclonal antibody, neutralize this bat virus, suggesting existing population immunity and broad-spectrum therapeutics provide an immediate barrier against zoonotic emergence.

Source differences
EurekAlert! ("RSV antibodies block a related bat virus mimic from entering human cells") highlights the AAAS Science Advances publication details (DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aei7060) and notes the authors' suggestion to explore whether the bat virus could inform future RSV vaccine designs. Bionieuws ("Antibodies against human virus also effective against bat virus") and the DZIF release ("DZIF Researchers Study Bat Viruses to Better Assess Potential Risks to Humans") detail the 1,778 samples across 18 bat species in Ghana and Benin, the 1.4% detection rate, and the 9% seroprevalence.

Still uncertain
Whether widespread rollout of new human RSV vaccines could alter cross-protective immunity dynamics in the human population, or how specific genetic mutations might affect the bat virus's pathogenicity, requires further reverse-genetics investigation.

「為什麼重要」
在人畜共通傳染病外溢前評估野生動物宿主風險，對新興傳染病防範至關重要。研究證實曾感染過人類RSV者體內的既有抗體，以及一株廣效性單株抗體，皆能有效中和此蝙蝠病毒，顯示人類群體既有免疫力與廣效抗體藥物可為跨物種傳播提供重要防護屏障。

未來人類全面接種RSV疫苗後是否會改變人群對此類蝙蝠病毒的交叉保護力，以及特定基因突變如何影響該病毒致病性，仍待團隊透過反向遺傳學系統進一步驗證。
