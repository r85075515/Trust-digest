10:T901,What happened
On November 28, 2026, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) released initial laboratory results from water sampling following the U.S. Army's controlled detonation of nearly 5 tons of degraded lead azide in Iowa. The EPA reported that 38 post-detonation public drinking water samples showed no elevated lead in water bodies serving as drinking water sources, with continuous 30-hour air monitoring at 0.0 micrograms per cubic meter and more than 3,200 total samples collected.

Why it matters
Detonating nearly 5 tons of degraded lead azide raised environmental safety concerns for nearby waterways and surrounding communities. Initial EPA drinking water findings—paired with 30 hours of zero-reading air monitoring and soil test results indicating negligible contamination—provide early environmental verification while laboratory analysis continues.

Source differences
The EPA news release titled "EPA Releases Initial Water Source Lab Results Indicating Wednesday's Controlled Detonation in Iowa Did Not Result in An Elevation of Lead in Water Bodies that Serve as Sources of Drinking Water" (2026-11-28) reports that 38 post-detonation public water supply samples did not detect lead elevation, notes 30 consecutive hours of air monitoring at 0.0 micrograms per cubic meter and over 3,200 total samples collected, and cites low pre-detonation baseline readings at Mark Twain Lake (1.62 ppb), the Illinois River (1.13 ppb), and two Mississippi River locations in Illinois (1.36 ppb and 2.12 ppb), all below the 15 ppb action level. Reuters's report titled "First soil, water tests after Iowa lead-azide blast show negligible contamination, EPA says" (2026-11-28) adds that soil test results also show negligible contamination, about 88% of post-detonation samples have completed analysis, and the detonated material was nearly 5 tons of degraded lead azide.

Still uncertain
Roughly 12% of post-detonation samples remain to finish laboratory analysis, and long-term environmental follow-up beyond the initial testing window is still ongoing.
