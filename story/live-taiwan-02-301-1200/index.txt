10:T8cf,Taiwan came out of the U.S. Section 301 report on structural overcapacity with exemptions for 2,232 products, including 322 agricultural items, and its stock market rallied hard on the news. The TAIEX climbed more than 1,200 points intraday to 39,396.66, with electronics and non-financial stocks each up about 2% and the OTC index rising over 4%.

What happened
The U.S. published its Section 301 investigation report on structural overcapacity, and Taiwan secured 2,232 product exemptions. Agriculture Minister Chen Chun-chi said Taiwan will hold to the 15% rate set out in the U.S.-Taiwan reciprocal trade agreement, without stacking it on top of most-favoured-nation duties, preserving the advantage of Taiwanese farm exports to the United States. Phalaenopsis orchids, a leading Taiwanese export to the U.S., remain an exclusive exemption with a zero tariff, while edamame and tilapia also hold a competitive edge over higher-taxed Chinese products, according to the Central News Agency (CNA). A separate CNA markets report said the 15% non-stacking rate lifted the TAIEX, as domestic investors and bargain hunters bought in volume.

Why it matters
The result removes the worst-case tariff outcome for Taiwan's exporters and its stock market in a single stroke. A zero tariff on orchids and a capped 15% rate for other goods keep Taiwanese farm products price-competitive in the U.S. market, while the broad rally, led by small caps, signals that investors read the report as clearing, rather than adding to, trade uncertainty.

Sources agree and disagree
Both items come from CNA, from its politics desk and its markets desk, and they cover different halves of the same story: farm exemptions and the market rally. Their figures describe different things, 2,232 exempted products against a 1,200-point intraday gain, so there is no conflict between them.

Still uncertain
The feed summaries do not spell out the full exemption list, the timetable for the agreement, or which products outside agriculture won relief. The rally is a single session's move, and later U.S. announcements could change the terms.
