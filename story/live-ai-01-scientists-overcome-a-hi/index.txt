10:T708,Quantum computers just got a little less fragile: scientists ran five silicon qubits at once while keeping accuracy near 99.9%, and IBM separately linked two quantum chips — two different advances aimed at the same wall, machines too small and error-prone for real work.
What happened
The silicon result is the headline advance. Running several qubits simultaneously has been a scaling obstacle because operating them together usually degrades accuracy; the new technique held accuracy at approximately 99.9% even with all five qubits running at once. The feed material also notes superconducting cables connecting qubits on different chips, with improvements still needed — a separate engineering thread about linking, not computing, across chips. IBM's chip link, reported by Science, is the companion item: two quantum chips joined as a step toward bigger computers.
Why it matters
Scale is the whole game in quantum computing. A single excellent qubit proves physics; a system that stays accurate while several qubits run together proves engineering. If both threads hold — more qubits per chip and reliable links between chips — the path to processors large enough for practical problems gets shorter, and the timeline debate shifts from whether to when.
Sources agree-disagree
ScienceDaily and Science report related advances, not the same result, so there is no single fact for them to corroborate or contradict. Trust label: cautious — two parallel developments under one theme, each carried by one outlet.
Still uncertain
The snippets do not name the silicon-qubit team or institution, give no error-correction or coherence details, and do not say when either advance reaches a working processor.
