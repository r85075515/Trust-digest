10:T64c,What happened
During a November 18, 2026 governing board meeting, Chandler Unified School District officials reported projections showing enrollment could decline by an estimated 1,257 students in the upcoming school year, translating into roughly USD 17 million to USD 17.2 million in lost per-pupil state funding. District leaders cited low housing turnover in Chandler and growing competition from alternative education options.

Why it matters
Because Arizona public school funding is tied directly to student enrollment counts, sustained demographic shifts and school choice competition are forcing even large suburban East Valley districts to tighten operating budgets.

Source differences
12News ("East Valley school district projecting to lose 1200 students by next year") reports the November 18 board briefing projecting 1,257 fewer students and a USD 17 million funding loss, attributing the drop to low housing turnover and alternative schooling. Arizona Chronicle ("Chandler Unified plans larger classes, district-office job cuts to close") cites a USD 17.2 million budget gap and details proposals to add an average of one student per class from fourth grade through high school alongside district-office staffing reductions.

Still uncertain
Final governing board votes on specific administrative position cuts and whether enrollment projections stabilize before the 2027–2028 budget adoption remain undecided.
