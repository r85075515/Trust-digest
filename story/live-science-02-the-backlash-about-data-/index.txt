What happened
Writing in the Algorithm newsletter, the reporter drove through Ashburn as Americans voted last week and traced the roots of the national data center backlash to the town where the build-out is densest. The argument is that residents' complaints are less about the buildings themselves than about power: the strain on the grid, rising bills, and who pays for the extra capacity.
Why it matters
The essay reframes the backlash from 'anti-AI' to 'about electricity,' which changes the policy question. If the core grievance is power, the answers are grid upgrades, rate design, and who bears the cost — not a referendum on AI itself. It pairs with the same day's New York Times column on voter backlash against AI: one piece argues the backlash could slow the boom, the other argues the backlash is really about power. The takeaway is that the politics of data centers will be fought on electricity bills.
