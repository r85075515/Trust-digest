10:Ta5b,What happened
Researchers at the Xishuangbanna Tropical Botanical Garden of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) formally described a new epiphytic fern species, Drynaria lineatisora, in the journal Phytotaxa (DOI: 10.11646/phytotaxa.782.2.6). Although specimens were first collected in Myanmar and Vietnam during the 19th century, the fern had been misidentified as the widespread Drynaria coronans for nearly 200 years. Diagnostic traits distinguishing Drynaria lineatisora include long, continuous linear sori reaching up to 20 mm in length and dark brown lanceolate scales, whereas Drynaria coronans exhibits round or short linear sori and pale yellow scales. Scanning electron microscopy showed spores densely covered with fine spines about 1 micrometer long, and phylogenetic analysis of 84 chloroplast genes confirmed its genetic independence.

Why it matters
Resolving a nearly two-century-long taxonomic misidentification clarifies the true biodiversity and evolutionary lineage of epiphytic ferns across the southern margin of the Himalayas. The newly recognized species grows on tree trunks at elevations between 840 and 2,400 meters, with its type specimen collected in tropical broadleaf forests around 1,130 meters in Medog, Tibet, and additional occurrences recorded in northern Myanmar and northern Vietnam. Accurate species delimitation prevents distinct, range-restricted mountain flora from being overlooked under the name of a common, widespread relative.

Source differences
All three cited reports derive from the same CAS institutional announcement regarding the Phytotaxa paper. The primary release from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, titled "New Fern Species Discovered After Nearly 200 Years of Being Mistaken for Another", and Phys.org's syndicated report, titled "New fern species discovered after nearly 200 years of being mistaken for another", retain the full morphological measurements, SEM spore details, 84-chloroplast-gene analysis, and elevational distribution from Medog to northern Myanmar and Vietnam. Wisevoter, titled "Researchers Describe New Fern Species in China", presents a shorter summary highlighting the geographic discovery in China and the near-200-year misidentification.

Still uncertain
The total wild population size of Drynaria lineatisora has not yet been estimated, leaving its exact conservation status and vulnerability to habitat disturbance across Medog, northern Myanmar, and northern Vietnam undetermined.
