10:T5b3,The world's wettest rainstorms, which produce about half of global annual rainfall, are getting shorter but more intense, according to a study reported by The Guardian on November 5.
What happened
The study finds that the most intense downpours have become shorter in duration and fewer in number, compressing roughly the same amount of rain into quicker and more extreme events. The total water falling from the sky has not vanished; it is arriving in tighter, harder bursts, with longer gaps implied between them. The Guardian's headline puts the consequence plainly: the shift affects both flood and drought risk.
Why it matters
Rainfall that arrives all at once is the hardest kind to manage. Short, violent downpours overwhelm drains and rivers, causing floods, while the same water, had it fallen steadily, could have soaked into soil and reservoirs. Water managers, farmers and city planners now face both extremes at once: too much water in hours, too little over weeks. That dual risk is the practical meaning of the study.
