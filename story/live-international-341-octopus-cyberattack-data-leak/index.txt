10:T798,What happened
Octopus Cards Limited detected a cyberattack on its systems on the morning of November 27, 2026 and took immediate containment measures. According to the company and media reports, the breach involved the personal data of roughly 7,500 individual customers—including names, photos, and for some users Automatic Add-Value Service details such as bank codes and top-up amounts—as well as contact details for around 22,000 merchants.

Why it matters
Octopus is Hong Kong's ubiquitous transit and retail payment system, meaning any security breach raises immediate concerns over phishing and secondary fraud against everyday commuters and local businesses. Although credit card numbers, bank account numbers, and stored balances were not compromised, exposed merchant contacts and user auto-reload metadata create high-value targets for social engineering scams.

Source differences
Octopus Cards Limited's official release titled "新聞發佈2026年11月27日- 香港八達通" confirms the morning detection, containment actions, and exposure of roughly 7,500 customers' personal data. RTHK's report "Personal data of 7,500 Octopus users leaked - RTHK" and The Standard's article "Octopus cyberattack exposes data of 7,500 customers and 22,000 merchants" align on the customer figure and note that no financial losses have been detected, while The Standard and Inmediahk's "八達通系統遭網絡攻擊涉約7,500客戶、2.2萬商戶個人資料| 獨媒報導" additionally detail the 22,000 affected merchants and automatic top-up bank codes.

Still uncertain
The identity of the attackers, the specific technical vulnerability exploited, and the exact timeline for restoring the few temporarily restricted auxiliary functions remain under investigation.
