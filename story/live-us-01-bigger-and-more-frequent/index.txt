10:T58e,Wildfires leave behind charred trees and scorched vegetation — and, less visibly, trouble for the streams and rivers that provide drinking water to much of the country. New studies show that contamination could remain for more than a decade after the flames go out.
What happened
A record number of wildfires burned more than 14,062 square miles in the US this year. After a wildfire burns a watershed, rain carries ash and organic matter into the streams that feed water treatment systems for urban and rural communities. The new studies cited in the coverage find that contamination of those supplies could persist for more than a decade.
Why it matters
The burned acreage is the visible disaster; the water is the slow one. Drinking-water contamination does not make headlines the way evacuations do, but it reaches further and lasts longer, affecting communities far from the fire line that never saw a flame.
