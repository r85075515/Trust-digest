What happened
The cartoon’s premise is the campaign’s preparedness message, rendered as a survival checklist: food, water, plastic bags, a spare stiff upper lip and a shovel. The column’s framing, including its sign-up pitch for new cartoons and its shop for merchandise and prints, makes clear this is a cartoonist’s take on the campaign, not the campaign’s own guidance.
Why it matters
The judgment is about tone: preparedness campaigns walk a line between useful and alarming, and satire is one way the public tests which side a campaign has landed on. A cartoon that jokes about stiff upper lips and shovels is really asking whether ‘Be Ready’ reassures people or just makes disaster feel closer — a question official messaging rarely asks about itself.
