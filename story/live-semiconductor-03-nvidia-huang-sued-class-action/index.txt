10:Tad4,Nvidia and its chief executive Jensen Huang were sued on Monday in a proposed shareholder class action accusing them of concealing his ties to a suspected Chinese spy, Reuters reports.
What happened
The complaint, filed in federal court in San Jose, California, says the defendants misled shareholders about Huang's alleged affair with Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed who is not related to him, and failed to disclose that the matter raised national security concerns threatening Nvidia's reputation and business prospects. US authorities had concluded Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset, according to the New York Times' reporting last month. Huang told employees on Sunday he had been unfaithful to his wife, without disclosing details of the alleged relationship, and Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation. Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% on Monday, reducing the company's market capitalization by approximately $190 billion; the lawsuit was filed five minutes after the US stock market closed. Shareholders led by Mark Kistenmacher are seeking unspecified damages.
Why it matters
The scandal has now entered the courts and the market's ledger at the same time. Securities suits after a sharp drop are common in the US, and this one's core claim — that the risk was material and was concealed — will be tested against what Nvidia actually said and when, including the board's statement that it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised. Whatever the suit's fate, it formalizes a price tag for the affair: roughly $190 billion of market value in a single session, and a legal track that will run alongside the board probe, the FBI inquiry and Congress's November 18 briefing request.
Sources agree / disagree
Reuters puts the one-day market-cap loss at approximately $190 billion; The Times rounded the same figure to about $200 billion — a rounding difference, not a factual conflict. Reports citing the filing give case number 5:26-cv-13448 and a class period of October 9, 2025 to November 8, 2026; Reuters' report does not list the docket number. Trust label: Single-wire lead with corroboration — the suit's existence and terms rest mainly on Reuters' reading of the complaint, with The Times and Taiwanese coverage (UDN) reporting the same filing independently.
Still uncertain
Nvidia has not responded to the lawsuit in the reports so far, and the allegations are unproven: a proposed class action must still be certified, and securities suits of this kind frequently settle or are dismissed. The damages sought are unspecified.
