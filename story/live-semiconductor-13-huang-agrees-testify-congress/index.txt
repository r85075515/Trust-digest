10:Tbeb,The ultimatum worked. Jensen Huang agreed on Wednesday night - hours before Nvidia announced his resignation - to testify behind closed doors before three House committees about his relationship with Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China, and about Nvidia's safeguards against foreign espionage, House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast announced Thursday (Nov 12). The testimony is to happen by Thanksgiving (Nov 26); no date is set, and Mast said Republicans hope to bring the now-former CEO in within about a week. If he does not come voluntarily, Mast said, the subpoenas are still ready.
What happened
The sequence matters. On Nov 4, Mast, China Select Committee Chairman John Moolenaar and Intelligence Chairman Rick Crawford jointly demanded Huang appear at a Nov 18 closed briefing; he never publicly replied. On Nov 10-11 the chairmen set an explicit deadline and readied subpoenas. On Wednesday night, per Mast, Huang's side agreed to come - and on Thursday morning Nvidia announced his resignation. Mast told reporters Thursday: 'He has agreed to come. We're in negotiations for which day.' He expects the session to be video-recorded and transcribed, and likely under oath, though those terms are still being negotiated. Mast also stressed he does not want the testimony to damage Nvidia, 'a critical U.S. company,' despite past clashes with Huang over export controls.
Why it matters
Resignation ended Huang's corporate exposure; it does not end his legal one. A closed-door, transcribed appearance before Foreign Affairs, the China committee and Intelligence is where the scandal stops being about adultery and becomes a counterintelligence record: what did Huang share, with whom, and what did Nvidia's security apparatus miss? Whatever he says will be citable - to the FBI, to the Wachtell investigators, and to the shareholders suing in San Jose. It also keeps Congress's leverage alive past his exit: the committees lose their jurisdiction hook if Republicans lose the gavels in January, which is exactly why they moved this week. The takeaway: Huang traded the CEO chair for the witness chair; the Alice Huang question, not the affairs, is what history will grade.
Sources agree / disagree
TechCrunch (citing Bloomberg and NBC News), Engadget, the Daily Caller and WSJ agree: agreement reached Wednesday night after subpoena threats, closed-door format, by Thanksgiving, three committees. Details still fluid across reports: some describe the recording/transcription and oath as settled, while Mast's own phrasing presents them as expectations under negotiation; the original Nov 18 date has effectively slipped. Trust label: Multi-source agreement; logistics unconfirmed.
