10:T6a4,Los Angeles County turned large trailers into mobile polling stations that toured neighbourhoods for 10 days before the US midterm election on 3 November, in a drive to encourage voting reported by CNA from Los Angeles.

What happened
The county converted large trailers into polling stations and sent them around different locations in the 10 days before the election, operating like one-day pop-up shops at each stop. The idea is simple: instead of asking voters to find a polling place, the polling place comes to them. The same news cycle also carried a second election story, with President Donald Trump holding his final rally on 2 November at Washington's Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool and his administration pressing a legal fight over about 20,000 questioned California mail ballots, but the mobile stations are the main thread of this card.

Why it matters
Turnout efforts usually rely on advertising and persuasion, while this one changes the physical cost of voting itself. If a trailer that shows up in a neighbourhood lifts participation, the model is cheap to copy, and that makes it a practical test worth watching beyond one county.

Sources agree and disagree
CNA and PTS cover related but different angles of the pre-election moment: CNA reports the mobile polling stations, while PTS reports Trump's final rally at the Reflecting Pool. The two accounts do not conflict; they describe separate events in the same election run-up.

Still uncertain
The snippets give no turnout figures for the mobile stations, no list of stops, and no detail on how many voters used the trailers, so whether the experiment worked cannot be judged from this coverage.
