The barrage, lasting more than six hours, damaged three bridges across the Dnipro River in Kyiv — the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi bridges — in what Reuters described as the biggest strikes on routes connecting the capital's left and right banks since the war began. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said three people were killed and 15 injured in the capital; CNN reported at least five dead nationwide, including two in the western city of Lviv.

What happened: Russia's defense ministry said it launched a "massive strike using high-precision weapons from land, sea, and air," plus drones, targeting Ukrainian military and energy facilities, port infrastructure in the Odesa region and transport infrastructure in Kyiv. Traffic across the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi crossing was fully suspended and the other two bridges restricted, disrupting a city of some three million. The attack was the retaliation Putin had promised from Shenzhen: Ukraine this week struck the Crimean Bridge for the third time, destroying its road section, and Putin — insisting the bridge served exclusively civilian purposes — vowed to hit back hard, warning the response would be "far more painful."

Why it matters: The timing is the story. The Shenzhen summit closed with a four-point "peace declaration" that mentioned neither Russia's invasion of Ukraine nor the Iran conflict; within hours, the largest bridge attack of the war hit Kyiv. The sequence undercuts the summit's peace framing, signals that Moscow is answering Ukraine's Crimea logistics campaign by escalating against the capital's infrastructure, and leaves European allies — who on Thursday warned against any settlement decided over their heads — watching a peace process that coexists with intensifying strikes.

Still uncertain: The full extent of bridge damage and how long Kyiv's cross-river traffic will be disrupted; the final casualty toll across the 12 regions reported hit; and whether further retaliation waves follow.
