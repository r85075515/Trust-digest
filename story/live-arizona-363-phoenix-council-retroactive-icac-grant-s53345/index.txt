10:T7c8,What happened
The Phoenix City Council is scheduled at its December 2, 2026 formal meeting to consider Ordinance S-53345, which would retroactively authorize the Phoenix Police Department to apply for, accept, and enter into an agreement for up to 598,611 USD in federal fiscal year 2026 funding for the Arizona Internet Crimes Against Children (ICAC) Task Force. According to a city staff report cited in local civic coverage, the federal grant invitation arrived on September 23 with an October 8 application deadline, leaving insufficient time to secure prior council authorization before submission. The proposed funds are earmarked for ICAC Task Force personnel and equipment.

Why it matters
Phoenix serves as a central coordinating hub for Arizona's ICAC enforcement network, which investigates online child exploitation across state and local jurisdictions. Because the council has not yet voted on Ordinance S-53345, formal approval is required before the police department can finalize the grant agreement and deploy the federal funds.

Source differences
My Civic News' report "Phoenix Council Asked to Back a $598,611 Child-Crimes Grant, After the Fact" details the city staff report behind Ordinance S-53345, including the 598,611 USD funding ceiling, the September 23 invitation date, the October 8 deadline, and the personnel and equipment allocation. Poliscopic's agenda tracker "Phoenix Fire short on staffing and response targets — Nov 18–Dec 8" serves solely as a public-safety docket roundup confirming that item S-53345 appears on the December 2 council agenda without independently reporting the grant figures.

Still uncertain
Whether the Phoenix City Council will approve Ordinance S-53345 as drafted at its December 2, 2026 meeting remains pending the formal vote.
