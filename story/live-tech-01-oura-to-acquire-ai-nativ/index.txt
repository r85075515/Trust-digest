10:T713,Oura, the smart-ring maker, has agreed to acquire Counsel Health, an AI-native primary care startup, for $625 million, announcing the deal alongside its quarterly earnings. The acquisition comes weeks after Oura indefinitely postponed a planned initial public offering of up to $2.2 billion, citing uncertainty in the IPO market, and it follows Oura's decision earlier this year to add Counsel Health's AI chatbot to its app.
What happened
TechCrunch reported the $625 million price and the earnings-day timing; The Verge described the deal as Oura acquiring a medical AI chatbot startup. Counsel Health calls itself an AI-native primary care company: its app uses health data reported from users' wearables to deliver AI-generated responses and insights, which can then proceed to a conversation. Oura added the company's chatbot to its own app earlier this year before agreeing to buy the business outright.
Why it matters
The judgment is direct: Oura is moving from tracking health to delivering care, turning wearable data into a service people consult rather than just numbers they check. Buying instead of building gives it a running product and a team, right after its IPO delay left its growth plans without a public listing.
Sources agree / disagree
TechCrunch and The Verge agree on the acquisition. The two figures in the headlines are not a conflict: $625 million is the purchase price, while $2.2 billion was the size of the postponed IPO. Trust label: Two-source agreement — both outlets confirm the deal; terms beyond the price are not in the snippets.
Still uncertain
Neither snippet gives a closing date, regulatory review, or integration plans, and the details of how the AI responses hand off to a conversation are not spelled out.
