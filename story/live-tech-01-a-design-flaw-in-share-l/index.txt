10:T702,A share button became a privacy hole: researchers reported Monday that Meta's new general-purpose AI agent, Muse, can expose sensitive information when users share conversations through web links — and Meta is suspending the feature while it reviews the design.
What happened
Ars Technica and Wired reported the same finding: like many online services, Muse lets users share searches and conversations via links, but the design of those share links left personal and business secrets accessible. In response to questions from Ars about the behavior, Meta said it is suspending the sharing feature while the company reviews it and considers making changes. Separately, TechCrunch reported that Meta said during Q3 earnings that Muse has passed 10 million users, with shopping and personal productivity among the top activities.
Why it matters
The verdict is blunt: Meta shipped a viral sharing feature before locking down who could see what. For users, the damage may already be done — anything shared through those links may have been indexed or copied while the flaw was live. For Meta, it is an early trust test for an agent it wants inside shopping and productivity workflows. Watch how the redesign handles link permissions.
Sources agree / disagree
Ars Technica and Wired agree on the core flaw and Meta's suspension response; TechCrunch's 10M-user figure is a separate, non-conflicting data point.
