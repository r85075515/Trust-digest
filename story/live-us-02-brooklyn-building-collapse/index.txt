10:Tcbe,A three-story residential building in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood collapsed into an adjacent open construction site on Wednesday afternoon (Nov 11), and by early Thursday Mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed eight people were dead and at least 17 injured, including three firefighters. Six bodies were pulled from the rubble and two more victims died after reaching hospital; none of the dead had been publicly identified as of Thursday morning. The toll climbed through the night - officials first reported two dead on Wednesday evening - and rescuers warn it may rise further: the FDNY says it still does not know how many people remain in the collapse zone, and the search is expected to take days.
What happened
The FDNY was called at 4:01 p.m. Wednesday to 1321 Flatbush Avenue, at the corner of Flatbush and Bedford avenues, where the side of a three-story building built in 1930 - four apartments over a ground-floor commercial space - gave way into an open excavation on the neighboring lot, where construction crews had been digging. Some 69 units and more than 250 fire and EMS personnel responded. Seven children are among the injured; four victims were in critical condition. Crews working with search dogs, drones and vacuum trucks pulled several survivors from the debris, including a pregnant woman rescued about two hours after the collapse. FDNY Chief of Department John Esposito called the site a very unstable situation, with a real risk of secondary collapse slowing the work.
Why it matters
This is New York's deadliest building collapse in years, and it happened next to an active excavation - the configuration behind many of the city's worst construction disasters. Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani noted at a late-night briefing that crews next door were excavating when the building crumbled into the pit. The Department of Buildings and law enforcement are leading the investigation; one report citing city records says the adjacent site was hit with a stop-work order and a $10,000 fine in March over unauthorized demolition work, a claim officials have not yet addressed. The takeaway: until investigators say why a 96-year-old building fell sideways into a hole, every excavation-adjacent building in the city is an open question - and the rescue count is not final.
Sources agree / disagree
AP, CNN, the New York Post and El Pais agree on the core facts: the address, the 1930 building, the collapse into the adjacent excavation, the scale of the response and the rising toll. Casualty figures differ by publication time rather than substance - two dead at Wednesday's late briefing, four in later updates, and eight confirmed by the mayor early Thursday; injured counts run from at least 17 (the mayor, including three firefighters) to 19 patients treated in the FDNY's Wednesday-night tally. Trust label: Multi-source agreement; casualty figures still updating.
Still uncertain
Whether anyone remains trapped, the identities of the eight dead, what caused the collapse - in particular the role of the adjacent excavation and the reported March stop-work order - and whether neighboring buildings are safe all remain open.
