10:Tcad,Israel's opposition leaders on Monday accused caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of an "attempted coup", after Channel 12 reported that his chief of staff, Ido Norden, phoned the police commissioner's office on the eve of the October 27 election with a complaint about members of the Arab-Israeli Raam party and pointed officers to an intelligence source - a former employee of the prime minister's office - whose information led to the arrest of three Raam members. The three were later released after police determined the information was unfounded. Haaretz separately reported that Shin Bet chief David Zini sought on election day to wiretap five Arab members of the Knesset over suspected foreign interference, a request Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara turned down for lack of evidence.

What happened: Opposition leader Yair Lapid wrote on X: "As more and more details come to light, it becomes clear: this wasn't an attempt to disrupt the elections. It was an attempted coup." Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen said that if confirmed, the findings showed "a criminal mechanism that operated from the heart of government to rig the election results". Gadi Eisenkot, who has been tasked by President Isaac Herzog with forming a government, said the caretaker prime minister "cannot remain at the head of the government for a minute longer" and demanded an immediate investigation; Raam's number two, former police chief Yoav Segalovitz, also called for an urgent independent investigation.

Why it matters: The October 27 election left Netanyahu's bloc with 53 seats, Eisenkot's camp with 48 and the Arab parties with 19 in the 120-seat Knesset - neither bloc has the 61 seats needed for a majority, and Eisenkot must assemble a coalition by December 6. If police and the security service were in fact used against an Arab party on the eve of the vote, it would be a scandal at the level of Israel's democratic institutions; even unverified, the accusation directly shadows the coalition talks and the caretaker government's legitimacy.

Sources agree / disagree: AFP and multiple international outlets describe the sequence consistently - Norden's call, the three arrests and later release, and the rejected wiretap request. The sides disagree on characterisation: the opposition calls it a coup attempt and a frame-up, while the prime minister's office and Likud deny the reports, saying Norden simply fulfilled a civic duty by reporting suspected criminal activity, and accuse Eisenkot of incitement. The underlying Channel 12 and Haaretz investigative reporting has not been independently verified.

Still uncertain: Whether the attorney general will open an investigation; the full circumstances of the three arrests and whether officers acted without warrants, as some reports claim; whether information was deliberately planted in police systems; whether Netanyahu personally knew of or directed the actions; and the fallout for the December 6 coalition deadline.
