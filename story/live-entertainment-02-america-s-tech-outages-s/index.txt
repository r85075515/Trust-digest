10:T840,US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there is 'zero evidence' that the recent wave of American tech outages is connected, dismissing online theories of a coordinated attack.

What happened
Rubio spoke at a press conference on Tuesday, while travelling with President Donald Trump. He said there was 'zero evidence' supporting theories online that recent disruptions affecting Microsoft, banks and flights are connected. Microsoft, major US banks, Apple and air traffic authorities have been hit by tech glitches over recent days. Rubio said the various outages plaguing the US this week are not related. He was travelling with Trump as they prepared for key meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Why it matters
A run of high-profile outages across Microsoft services, banks and flights fed speculation online that the US was under a coordinated or systemic attack. The Secretary of State's denial, delivered on camera, is the administration's clearest attempt to calm that speculation. His words settle the political question but not the technical one: each outage still needs its own explanation, and the public will judge the denial by whether investigators find separate, ordinary causes.

Sources agree / disagree
BBC World, BBC Technology, TMZ and NPR all report the same core claim: Rubio said the outages are unrelated and there is no evidence of a malign actor. The outlets differ only in framing, with BBC emphasising that the US is not 'under systemic attack' and NPR stressing that there is 'zero' information pointing to a malign actor. No outlet contradicts Rubio's statement.

Still uncertain
Rubio's denial does not explain what caused each outage. Microsoft, the banks, Apple and air traffic authorities have not been reported as giving a single shared cause. The cause of each disruption, and whether any of them involved foul play, remains to be established by the companies and investigators.
