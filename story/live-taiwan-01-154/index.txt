10:T7bb,Taiwan's Smart Manufacturing Plan Spurs NT$15.4 Billion in Corporate Investment

Taiwan's smart manufacturing push is showing up in hard numbers: more than NT$15.4 billion in corporate investment, according to the Ministry of Economic Affairs, which says its R&D program has completed over 1,000 technology transfers and services in three years.
What happened
CNA reports that the ministry has run the Smart Manufacturing Core Key Technology R&D Program since 2023. In three years it has completed over 1,000 technology transfers and company services worth about NT$944 million, and spurred corporate investment of more than NT$15.4 billion. The headline example is a five-axis machine tool developed with Da Jun Hong Technology (大君鴻科技): it has broken into the semiconductor chamber machining market and already won 30 orders from a German buyer, projected to create about NT$450 million in output.
Why it matters
The figures, if they hold, say Taiwan's industrial policy is doing more than funding research — it is pulling private money behind it. The five-axis machine matters most: breaking into semiconductor chamber machining means Taiwanese toolmakers are moving up from commodity equipment into the precision gear that chip production depends on, a market where foreign suppliers have long dominated.
Sources agree / disagree
This is a single-source story: only CNA reported it, so no outlet can corroborate or challenge the ministry's figures. Trust label: Single source — the numbers come from one CNA report of a ministry announcement; treat them as the ministry's account until other outlets confirm them.
Still uncertain
The report gives no breakdown of the NT$15.4 billion by company or year, no timeline for delivering the 30 German orders, and no independent check on the ministry's figures. The NT$450 million output figure is a projection, not a result.
