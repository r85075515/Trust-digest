10:T793,As memory technologies continue to scale, manufacturing processes are becoming increasingly complex — and virtual twins combined with machine learning are being used to untangle that complexity and improve DRAM yield.

What happened
Additional process steps, tighter process windows, and new sources of variation are making it progressively harder to reach acceptable yield, according to Semiconductor Engineering. Systemic failures are emerging as a source of yield loss during yield ramp. Unlike random defects, these failures are often driven by complex interactions that are difficult to trace to a single cause. Virtual twins — digital models of the manufacturing process — and machine learning offer a way to simulate those interactions and find the sources of loss.

Why it matters
The judgment is that yield is no longer a matter of catching random bad chips: when failures are systemic, the factory needs a model of the whole process to find them. That shift is why virtual twins matter — they turn yield ramp from trial and error into something that can be simulated before silicon is wasted.
