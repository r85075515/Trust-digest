10:T84e,Anthropic has launched the largest initial public offering in history, dethroning SpaceX as the previous record holder — but the reports cannot agree on how large it actually was.

What happened
MarketWatch describes the IPO as "bittersweet": retail investors showed up, but the listing has so far failed to provide a broad lift to the technology sector. Wired frames the event around Anthropic's own contradictions, saying the record-breaking IPO highlights the AI giant's worst enemy — itself — and points to its AI safety strategy and growing public anger toward data centers as risks investors must weigh. The Decoder puts the IPO at $81.3 billion and says it shatters SpaceX's record as rising rates and political headwinds shake the sector. Another snippet in the cluster cites a $107 billion figure. Early investor Dustin Moskovitz is publicly distancing himself, warning that his early funding may have been an act "that ended the world."

Why it matters
The disagreement over $107 billion versus $81.3 billion is a gap of more than $25 billion, so the headline number itself is unreliable until the filings are checked. The deeper judgment is that the market is treating Anthropic's safety promises and the backlash against data centers as real risks to its growth, not as side issues.

Sources agree / disagree
All three outlets agree the IPO set a record and passed SpaceX. They disagree on the size: $107 billion in one snippet against $81.3 billion in The Decoder. The snippets do not explain whether the two figures measure the same thing — total valuation, amount raised, or something else — so neither figure can be treated as settled.

Still uncertain
The snippets do not give the final offer price, the amount actually raised, the first-day close, or what Moskovitz said in full. The claim that public anger toward data centers could affect growth is presented as an investor concern, not as a measured fact.
