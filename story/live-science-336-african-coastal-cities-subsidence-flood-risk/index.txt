10:T762,What happened
A research team led by Virginia Tech PhD graduate Oluwaseyi A. Dasho and senior author Manoochehr Shirzaei published a study in Science Advances on November 27, 2026, analyzing vertical land motion across 20 African coastal cities using European Space Agency Sentinel-1 radar data. The researchers found widespread land subsidence compounding sea-level rise, with median sinking rates reaching 6.0 millimeters per year in Alexandria, Egypt, and 5.0 millimeters per year in Lagos, Nigeria.

Why it matters
Combining local land sinkage with rising ocean levels dramatically accelerates coastal inundation hazards in rapidly urbanizing African metropolises. By 2050, the study projects that more than 7 million people, 1 million buildings, and roughly USD 180 billion in infrastructure and economic assets across these coastal hubs will be exposed to severe flooding, underscoring the need to curb groundwater extraction and update urban flood defenses.

Source differences
Courthouse News Service ("African coastal cities projected to sink by several millimeters in next two decades") highlights the Sentinel-1 radar methodology and the prominent subsidence rates in Lagos and Alexandria. EurekAlert! ("Study of 20 African megacities spotlights coastal land sinkage as an underestimated flood risk") and Carbon Brief ("'Sinking' African megacities will expose seven million people to flooding by 2050") specify the 2050 exposure projections of over 7 million residents, 1 million buildings, and USD 180 billion in assets.

Still uncertain
How quickly municipal governments in high-risk cities like Lagos, Alexandria, and Douala can enforce groundwater pumping restrictions or finance coastal adaptation projects before 2050 remains unclear.
