What happened: Alice Huang previously led Singapore-based Megaspeed International, once a major Nvidia customer accounting for billions of dollars in chip sales; US authorities have been investigating Megaspeed since 2024 over concerns it smuggled Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China. The Times' Oct. 29 article first touched off the scandal that led to Jensen Huang's resignation two weeks later and a steep fall in Nvidia's stock. Sunday's report adds the intelligence dimension: US officials tracked her contacts with an MSS officer across the same period. But US officials simultaneously pushed back on the spy framing. Why it matters: Whether the contacts were espionage, business, or something in between is now the central unanswered question of the Nvidia affair — and it is being fought over in public between intelligence reporting and the administration's own investigators. Still uncertain: The nature and content of the contacts are not public; the Justice Department has reached no conclusion on whether Alice Huang acted for Chinese intelligence; and neither she nor Megaspeed has commented on the new report.
