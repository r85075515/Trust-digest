10:T6d3,Microsoft said access to its flagship software has been fully restored, ending an outage that paralyzed government offices, traders and businesses around the world. Efforts to address the underlying problem with Outlook, Teams and Copilot are complete, the company said.

What happened
The outage struck on Thursday and Friday, affecting users of Outlook, Teams, Microsoft 365 and Copilot. The U.S. House of Representatives, health care providers, schools and many other organizations were affected. Microsoft said access has been fully restored to its flagship software, and CNBC reported that the company apologized for the massive outage.

Why it matters
Outlook, Teams and Microsoft 365 sit at the center of daily work for governments, hospitals, schools and companies. When they go down together, the disruption spreads far beyond a single app, freezing communication and work across whole organizations. A global outage lasting into a second day underlines how much of the world's office work depends on a single provider.

Sources agree and disagree
Four outlets, BBC World, CNBC, Engadget and Bloomberg, reported the outage and the restoration. They agree that services were down on Thursday and Friday and have now been fully restored. CNBC added that Microsoft apologized for the disruption. No numeric or name conflicts appeared in the short feed descriptions.

Still uncertain
The feed descriptions do not explain the underlying technical problem in detail, give a precise timeline of when each service came back, or say how many users were affected. Those details should be checked in the publishers' full reporting.
